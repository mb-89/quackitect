## detail

src/processes holds three files: note.process.yaml, standard.process.yaml and trivial.process.yaml. Between them they carry 46 comment lines, counted by se_find regex ^\s*# over path src/processes/*.

The engine projects a process into steps. A comment is not a step, so nothing written only in a comment reaches the agent or person doing the work. All 46 lines are dead at run time.

Two kinds are present.

Most are not actionable: a banner restating the description, and a line saying the activities are SIPOC rows. Others are a paragraph explaining why traced is true or false, and a paragraph explaining what a criteria block is. A reader of the projected process is not wrong without them.

A few carry a live rule that a doer needs and never gets. standard.process.yaml line 10 says "How a reviewer works is [[reviewing]]", in a header comment, while the verdict step it governs does not name it. Lines 6 to 9 of the same file say the verdict blocks nothing and is given once. They also say it turns each finding into a trivial token naming this one. note.process.yaml lines 27 to 32 draw the distinction between depends_on and ready_when. Lines 91 to 93 say deciding not to decide leaves the note noted. Each governs a step and sits outside it.

## proposed action

Move the live rules into the steps they govern, delete the rest, and leave src/processes with no comment lines.

Into the verdict activity of standard.process.yaml: the reviewer is pointed at [[reviewing]]. The reviewer is told the verdict blocks nothing, is given once, and closes the token, each finding being a trivial token naming this one.

Into the decide activity of note.process.yaml: depends_on names tokens that must close first, and the engine decides it. ready_when names anything else, and a person judges it. A note that is not ready is passed over and stays noted, with no disposition.

Everything else goes. A reason a maintainer wants goes into the commit message, not the file.

## approach

A comment is dead and a step is live. So every one of the 46 lines is sorted by one question: does a doer need this at run time?

A line that answers yes moves into the step it governs, into a field the doer reads. That field is the activity's does or a criterion's says. It does not stay in both places.

A line that answers no is deleted. A reason a maintainer would want, such as why note is untraced, goes into the commit message. That is the part a reader may disagree with: after this, knowing why reaches for git rather than the file.

No activity, state or disposition is added, removed or renamed. The shape of each process is exactly what it was, and only the words a doer reads change.
