## detail

A walker that has looked can say so, and the same hold stops being raised.

FOUND IN USE, not reasoned about. Twenty minutes after the investigation
landed, the queue answered investigate rather than work: a token was held by a
reviewer that had stopped pulling because it was reading. That is the feature
working. What it has no way of is being answered.

The walker looks, which is what the alarm asks for, finds the holder alive, and
has nothing to say. The next pull raises the same alarm, and the one after
that. Being sent once is an alarm. Being sent every pull is a wall, and this
project already knows what a wall nobody can clear does.

WHAT IT NEEDS: the walker answers the investigation, the engine records that
somebody looked, and that hold is left alone for a while. What a while means is
the same kind of number as the staleness and it belongs beside it.

AND THE NUMBER ITSELF IS WRONG FOR A REVIEWER. A reviewer between pulls looks
exactly like a reviewer that has died, because the only thing counted is pulls,
and a reviewer reading one token carefully is the commonest state there is. Ten
pulls is about right for a sub-walker and too tight for a reviewer. Either the
number depends on the role, or looking has to be answerable, or both.
