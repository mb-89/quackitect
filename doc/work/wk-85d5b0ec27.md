## detail

God mode is every refusal off, and the stop hook still fires in it.

decideStop in src/engine/hook.go reads the rung nowhere. So a stop in god still needs a claim, and a claim over open work is still argued with three times before it is granted. Every other guard is already off: decidePreToolUse returns at its god check before any of them is reached.

THE OWNER'S WORDS: in god mode, really, the stop hook doesn't fire. You are not supposed to keep running in the loop unsupervised. It is only to fix stuff.

That is the whole reason the rung exists. God is turned on when something in the engine is in the way, with a person watching. An engine that argues with that person's agent about whether it may stop is the engine in the way, in the one state built to take it out of the way.

The person's own controls are the exception and stay. The hold and the ask are enforced above the god check in decidePreToolUse, deliberately, so an override cannot silence whoever is holding the work down. The stop hook is the engine's own rule rather than theirs, so it goes.

## proposed action

decideStop asks the rung once, at its top, and returns when it is god.

1. The check goes at the top of decideStop, the way the one in decidePreToolUse sits above every guard it disables.
2. It reads NoGuardsAtAll, which is the function that answers god and today has no caller outside tests. So the god rung gains its first real behaviour through the name already written for it.
3. The comment says what god is for, and that the person's hold and ask are not affected, so a reader does not take this as everything being off.

Unbound is untouched. It keeps the claim and the argument, and only the queue stops being a reason, which is already true.
