## detail

CLASS: A KEEP LIST THAT AGES AGAINST A RECORD THAT GROWS, WHERE BEING SHORT DELETES RATHER THAN GOING RED. A destructive pass is given an exception list so it does not take what somebody still needs, and the list is typed: these fields, these folders, these names. It is complete on the day it is written. Then the record grows a field, and the field is not on the list, and nothing anywhere says so. This is the hand-list class that wk-02e17b9eb4 writes down, with the cost inverted, and the inversion is the whole of why it deserves its own name. AN ORDINARY SHORT LIST makes a check pass when it should have failed, which is a missed catch. A SHORT KEEP LIST makes a sweep take something, which is a loss with no undo and no message. It is worse still where the list guards the artefact behind an observation, because the thing deleted is the evidence somebody would have used to notice. MEASURED ON wk-339068932c: citedInOpenWork reads five fields of a token and does not read the evidence, the re-watch, a lesson or a finding's clause, and the re-watch is the field whose entire purpose is to point at the artefact the keep exists to protect. It landed the same day, hours after the list was written. INSTEAD, AND THE FIRST HALF IS WHAT STOPS IT BEING MADE: when the list is an exception to something destructive, do not write a list, ask the thing that owns the set. For a record that is the type, and a reflect walk over it costs a dozen lines and never ages. Where you must type one, write down beside it what makes it complete and what would make it short, because the next person adds a field to a struct and has no reason to look inside a sweep for a list they now belong to. AND ASK, ONCE, WHICH DIRECTION BEING WRONG GOES: a check that is short reports nothing, a keep that is short destroys, and the second earns machinery the first does not. THE SECOND HALF IS THE CHECK THAT CATCHES IT, and it runs on the producing side: enumerate every field of the record that can hold prose, and assert that the keep sees a citation in each one. That test goes red on the commit that adds the eleventh field, which is the only moment it was ever needed, and it says which field to the person who added it.
