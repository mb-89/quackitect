## detail

CLASS: A COUNT WRITTEN IN PROSE BESIDE THE LIST IT SUMMARISES, AND NEVER
RECOUNTED AGAINST IT.

A note that enumerates things -- ids, files, commits, cases -- grows a sentence
at the top saying how many of each kind there are. That sentence is written
from memory while the body is being drafted, the body then moves, and nobody
counts again. The summary is the first thing a reader reads and the last thing
anybody checks, so it is where a wrong number survives longest. It reads as
more careful than saying nothing, which is what makes it expensive: a reader
who counts ten and was told eleven concludes an entry is missing and goes
looking for one that was never there.

FOUND ON wk-61af3a054e, the backlog clearing. Its detail says "Eleven are
aborted, one is settled as a duplicate, and ten are still backlogged". Its own
body names ten aborted and eleven backlogged, and the twenty-two notes on disk
agree with the body: ten aborted, one became, eleven backlogged. The
submission repeats the error a second way -- "Twelve are settled and ten are
still backlogged", where its own enumeration adds to eleven settled -- and
drops wk-be358bd3f3 out of the backlogged list altogether.

WHY THE CHECK DID NOT CATCH IT. The check the token ships,
TestEveryBacklogItemWasRead, asks only whether each id on the list appears
somewhere else in the detail. It cannot see a verdict that is wrong, and it
cannot see a count. So the one number a reader leans on is the one number
nothing decides.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD. Do not write a count you have not derived from the list in
front of you. Derive it -- grep the section, count the lines, print the number
-- and paste what the command answered. If the count is worth writing down it
is worth a check: when a note carries both a summary and the body it summarises,
the check reads both sides and fails when they disagree, because both sides are
inside the note and neither of them moves after it closes. And when the summary
describes records that exist, count those too: a note claiming eleven aborted
is a claim about eleven files, and ls answers it.

THE CHECK, RED TODAY: extend readBacklog in src/engine/backlogread_test.go to
classify each listed id by the uppercase section its verdict sits under, and
require that the numbers written in the WHAT THIS IS paragraph equal the sizes
of those sections. Against wk-61af3a054e as it stands that is red: eleven and
ten claimed, ten and eleven found.
