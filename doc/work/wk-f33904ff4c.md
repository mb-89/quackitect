## detail

The lane holds the tool schemas as literals, and a second list maps a tool argument to a verb flag. Both are copies of what the engine already owns.

That copy is why the lane goes stale. The engine grew a tracked flag and the lane's map did not. Every non-note mint through the tool was refused while the command line worked.

The transport is already right. The lane puts the verb, its arguments and its payload to the living engine over a socket, and nothing is spawned.

So the lane holds no state and answers nothing itself. Its knowledge is the only thing worth rebuilding, and it sits in two places: the schemas it publishes, and the argument list it builds.

Two standing checks exist because of the copy. One drives the lane for its tool list, and one drives the extension's argument builders against the real binary. Both guard a duplicate rather than a rule.

## approach

The lane asks the engine what the tools are, and builds its schemas from the answer. The verb owns its flags, and the argument list the lane builds comes from that same answer rather than from a second map.

One test already holds one half of this, where the schemas are read off the structs the handlers decode. The other half is the argument map, which no test reads against the engine.

When the lane derives both, the two standing checks guard a rule rather than a duplicate, and one of them can go.

## done when

- the lane publishes no literal tool schema, decided by: search the lane for an inline schema, which answers nothing
- the argument map is derived from the engine's own answer, decided by: add a verb flag and drive the lane without editing it
- a cold session and the warm lane offer the same fields, decided by: the mcp-tools check
- one of the two standing checks is retired or renamed to the rule it now guards, decided by: the battery list
