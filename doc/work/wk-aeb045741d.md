## detail

An agent learns the stop protocol from a refusal, turn by turn, and gets it wrong first.

The chapter is right and complete. doc/guidance/driving-the-engine.md section 3 says stopping is claim then stop, that every call after the claim clears it, an answer included, and that asked is granted on the claim naming it whatever is in your hands. A Go test holds that last sentence, in src/engine/askedisgranted_test.go.

The agent never reads any of it. What reaches the session prompt is the projected actionable alone, one line: stop only with a reason named to the engine, on an interrupt claim asked. Nine sentences of protocol stay in the body, and the body is not projected.

The one measured here, in September 2026. The owner said give me the table and then wait. That is their word, so it is asked, and asked is granted at once. The agent claimed plan, because a plan was what it had just presented. It was argued with, correctly, and the owner read the argument as an engine defect. It was not one. The agent had never been told that asked outranks a reason that merely describes the moment.

This is a projection gap, not a wording defect. The chapter that matters most while a turn is ending is the one the agent has least of in front of it.

## proposed action

Carry the protocol into the projection, where the agent reads it.

1. The actionable at doc/guidance/driving-the-engine.md line 25 says the order and the clearing, not only that a reason is named: stopping is claim then stop, and every call after the claim clears it, an answer included.
2. A second actionable says asked outranks the rest. When the person's own word is what stopped you, the reason is asked, whatever else also describes the moment.
3. Measure before wording it. Whether to project the chapter body wholesale or promote these two sentences is decided by what the projection costs in prompt bytes.

Read util/checks/projections-carry-chapters.mjs first, to see whether it can catch a chapter present but thin.
