## detail

CLASS: A CRITERION THAT NAMES A PROPERTY AND NO SUBJECT, ON A TOKEN WHOSE
DELIVERABLE GOES INTO MORE THAN ONE PLACE.

The token puts one thing in two files. The first criterion names file A, the
second names file B, and every criterion after those two opens with the word
it. The pronoun has two referents, so whoever writes the check picks one, and
the one picked is the copy that is easier to read. The list is then green while
the second copy is a heading and one sentence.

IT READS AS COMPLETE, because the sentences cover every property and two lines
on the list carry a path. No line says which copy the properties are asserted
of, and a reader supplies the missing subject from the criterion above without
noticing that nothing wrote it down.

THE SECOND PLACE IS ALWAYS THE ONE THAT LOSES. It is the place with no check of
its own, so its content is decided by whoever happens to be writing the copy
that does have one.

MEASURED ON wk-0146942977, round 1. Criteria 1 and 2 name doc/guidance/specifying.md
and doc/guidance/behaviour.md. Criteria 3, 4 and 5 name neither file. I copied
both files, wrote the class in full into behaviour.md and a single line into
specifying.md, and wrote the three checks those criteria describe. All three
passed.

INSTEAD, GIVE EVERY CRITERION A SUBJECT, BY PATH, even where the criterion
above it already named one. A pronoun is not a subject, because the check is
written from the sentence and the sentence is what the worker reads.

AND WHERE THE DELIVERABLE GOES INTO SEVERAL PLACES, THE CONTENT CRITERION WALKS
THEM. It fails naming the place that is short, rather than confirming whichever
one it read first.

AND WHERE THE SENTENCE NAMES A SECTION AS WELL AS A FILE, SAY THE CHECK READS
THAT SECTION. A whole-file search is green with the text anywhere in the file,
including under a heading the sentence did not name.
