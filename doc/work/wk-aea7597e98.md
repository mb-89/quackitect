## detail

CLASS: A LIVE NUMBER MADE PERMANENT BY A CRITERION THAT REQUIRES IT INTO
STANDING GUIDANCE.

A draft wants its argument to carry weight, so it measures the record and puts
the figure in the detail. Then a criterion requires that figure to appear in a
guidance file. The criterion is checkable, it goes green when the sentence is
written, and the number stops being a measurement the moment it lands: the
record it counted goes on moving, nothing re-derives it, no projection rewrites
it, and the file is handed to every reviewer on every pull. A wrong number in a
note costs a round. A wrong number in the method is read by everybody, for as
long as the file exists, and nothing will ever say it has gone wrong.

IT IS WORSE WHEN THE FIGURE IS A RATE. A rate has two halves, and the halves
are usually taken from two instruments, so nobody can re-derive it even on the
day it was written. The numerator comes from the log and the denominator from
the notes, and the two count different populations.

MEASURED ON wk-2fb5bf5bb1. Its detail says 49 distinct tokens carry a review event and
122 rejections stand across them, a failure rate of 249 per cent, and its
criterion requires all three into doc/guidance/reviewing.md. The 106 was right
on the day and answers 121 today. The 61 answers 49 from the log and 48 from
the notes and reproduces from nothing. A round had already said so and the
redraft carried the three numbers through unchanged.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD.

WHAT WOULD HAVE STOPPED IT BEING MADE. Before writing a number into a document
somebody else will read later, ask who re-derives it. If the answer is nobody,
the document may not carry the number. Standing guidance carries the argument
and the rule. The measurement that motivated it belongs on the token, where the
token dates it and where it dies with it. Where the number really has to be in
the guidance, it goes with the command that produced it and the commit it was
taken at, so a reader can re-run it rather than believe it. And a rate is
written only when both halves come from one instrument and the rule for what
counts is a rule a command can apply.

WHAT WOULD HAVE CAUGHT IT. Run every number in the draft before submitting,
paste the command and its answer beside each, and say which commit the reading
was taken at. Where a number has no command, do not write it: say it is an
impression and drop the words that make it sound counted, which is what
doc/guidance/voice.md asks for under Claims. A count whose set is not named in
the sentence is not a measurement at all, because nobody can tell what to
count.
