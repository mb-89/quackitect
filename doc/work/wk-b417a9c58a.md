## detail

The queue hands out under the filter and the sentence beside it counts without one, so the number teaches the wrong thing about a mechanism that works.

MEASURED on 2026-09-06 with work.queue_filter set to bucket:claims. se_pull answered "143 tokens are open and workable". Twenty-two tokens carry that bucket, so no filtered count can exceed 22.

WHERE THE TWO PARTED. next() in src/engine/pull.go:844 narrows first: it wraps Tokens(r) in theQueueOffers (pull.go:862), which drops everything TheQueueTakes refuses. StaffingOf in src/engine/staffing.go:43 walks Tokens(r) raw and asks WouldHandOut per token. Its own comment at staffing.go:45 says the count is what the queue would hand out, and that WouldHandOut is the pull's own question asked here. The filter is not inside WouldHandOut, it is applied a layer up, so the count misses the narrowing the pull applies before it ever asks that question.

WHAT IT COSTS. The staffing guard sizes its demand off OpenWork, so a filtered queue of 22 asks for hands as though 143 were workable. That is the shape countiswhatthequeuehands_test.go was written against: hands spawned for work the pull passes over, each told wait.

## proposed action

ONE DOOR ANSWERS HOW MANY. The owner ruled in September 2026: there is one answer to how many tokens are open. It is the one the filter gives.

Three readings exist today. next() narrows through theQueueOffers and then walks. StaffingOf walks Tokens(r) raw. QueueDepth counts by a rule of its own, Ended and PrivateProcess. Patching staffing.go alone leaves the third behind, so the depth and the count could still disagree.

So the walk becomes a function and the other two read it. TheQueueWouldHandOut(r) walks theQueueOffers(r, "", Tokens(r)) and asks WouldHandOut per token. It answers the worker count and the verdict count. StaffingOf fills OpenWork and AwaitingVerdict from it, and QueueDepth returns its worker half.
