## detail

A bucket never draws as a group, so a drop has nowhere to land and filing rows
makes a grouping nobody can see.

WHAT HAPPENS. util/views/work.base declares a group per status under groups:,
and a declared group takes its rows before the grouping level runs. Every row
matches one of them, so the level if(bucket, bucket, status) never makes a group
of its own, and a bucket the person named is drawn nowhere.

MEASURED. se query --view work --pane left answers ten groups, every one of them
declared: true, with no by and no sets on any of them. The level itself says
sets: bucket, so the view is right and the groups it produces are not there.

AND THAT IS WHY NOTHING TAKES A DROP. A group carries data-sets only when the
engine says what a drop into it would write, which only a data-made group does.
A declared group correctly takes none: dropping onto backlogged would write a
status, and a status is moved by a pull rather than by a keystroke, which the
engine already refuses. So today there is no drop target on the page at all,
and drag and drop is dead in the editor.

WHERE THE LINE FALLS, and this is the judgment. A declared group is a filter the
view author wrote and it should keep taking its rows. A bucket is a name a
person typed and it is the one grouping a drop can write. Those two want to be
visible at the same time, and one swallowing the other is the fault.

THE SHAPE THAT MAY WORK: a row carrying a bucket goes to its bucket's group and
not to the declared group its status matches, because the person's own name for
where work belongs is a stronger statement than the status it happens to have.
That is the same ruling the engine already makes when a drop onto a derived
group clears the bucket. It wants agreeing before it is built.

THE CHECK, RED TODAY: file two rows into a bucket through se work --file, draw
the view, and require a group named after that bucket carrying both rows and
carrying sets. Today the answer has no such group.
