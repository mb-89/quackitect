## detail

limits.pulls_before_hold_is_stale is 10, and the engine counts pulls by everybody, not by the holder's peers. So the threshold is 10 pulls of the whole fleet.

With one worker, 10 pulls is 10 pieces of work, which is a long time. With twelve workers pulling, 10 pulls go past in well under a minute. Any holder deep in a token that takes twenty minutes has therefore stopped pulling by the engine's measure, and the alarm fires on healthy work.

The cost is not the wasted look. It is that the queue answers investigate instead of handing out work, so one alarm stops every worker. Twelve agents were sent to the same hold, and .se/looked.json parked five of them on it.

investigate.go says the queue is its own clock, and that a second number in seconds would be a second answer to one question. That reasoning holds for one worker. It breaks when the clock's rate is the fleet size. The thing being measured, how long a piece of work takes, does not shrink when hands are added.

## proposed action

Count what the threshold means to a holder, not to the room. Two shapes worth weighing.

Count only the pulls that could have been the holder's own, which is the fleet's pulls divided by the number of actors present. Then 10 keeps its meaning as hands are added.

Or count the holder's own missed turns. Count how many times the queue offered and this actor did not come, rather than how many pulls happened at all.

Either way, a hold is stale when its holder has skipped its own turns, not when the room was busy.
