## detail

CLASS: A SECOND SECTION UNDER A HEADING THE READER KEEPS ONE OF, ADDED BY HAND, AND THE READER KEEPS THE LAST.

A note is a markdown file a person edits. New work is added to it the way anybody adds to a document: a heading, a blank line, the new lines under it. The heading is one the engine owns. readBody in src/engine/store.go walks the sections and ASSIGNS rather than appends for a heading it reads once, so the second occurrence replaces the first, and the next SaveToken rewrites the note from the parsed token with the first one gone. Nothing is refused and nothing is reported.

WHY IT IS WORSE THAN AN ORDINARY TRUNCATION. The note reads correctly to a person right up to the save: two headings, both sets of lines, nothing out of place. The loss happens on somebody else's save, often in a commit about something else entirely, so the diff that destroys the work carries a message about another subject and no reviewer is looking at it.

MEASURED ON wk-1b7c1a2da1. Before commit 9ae952bf the note carried two done-when sections, nine criteria under the first and two under the second. The token carried two. Commit 9ae952bf, whose message says it reworded one counting criterion, wrote the note back with the nine gone and four detail paragraphs with them, because those paragraphs sat after the last lesson and belong to no section the reader keeps. The token then arrived at review with no criterion for its own subject and two criteria for a different one, and three findings from earlier rounds pointing at criteria that no longer exist.

DRIVEN RATHER THAN INFERRED. In a copy of src/engine I wrote a note with two done-when sections, three criteria under the first and one under the second. LoadToken answered one criterion, the second section's, and one SaveToken wrote the note back with the first section gone.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD. Add to the section that is there. Never open a second one under a heading the engine owns. And after editing a note by hand, load it back through the engine and compare what came back with what you wrote, because the parser is the only thing that decides what the note says.

THE CHECK, RED TODAY: refuse a note carrying two sections under a heading readBody keeps one of, naming the heading and the line the second one opens on. Against the tree as it stands nothing refuses it, and the same reader that drops the first section is the one that would have to report it.

BEFORE IT: ask, before writing a heading into a note, whether the engine already owns that heading. If it does, the new lines go under the one that is there, and a second is a silent delete rather than an addition.
