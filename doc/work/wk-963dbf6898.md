## detail

A submission piped to se pull at the command line is answered with the next token, and the caller may not be a worker at all.

Measured here: a submission typed at the shell closed a note, and the answer carried wk-6542703f66, a parked note. It stood held until a put-down by hand gave it back.

The engine cannot tell the two doors apart. The shell sends verbAsk at src/engine/client.go:27, the lane sends verbCall at src/mcp/lane.go:362, and both carry the verb, its arguments and standard input.

Handing the next token on is right for the lane, because an agent that submits is an agent asking for more. It is wrong for a person at a prompt, who asked for one thing.

## approach

The ask carries the door it came through. verbAsk at src/engine/client.go:27 gains a field, the lane's verbCall at src/mcp/lane.go:362 gains the same one, and the lane sets it.

Where the field is empty the door is the shell, so a caller that names nothing is never handed work. An unknown caller asked for one thing, and gets one thing.

runPull answers the settled submission and stops there. A pull carrying no payload is a pull for work, and it is unchanged at both doors.

The two structs are one thing written twice, which is the note wk-e80e5c912a. This change writes the field into both, and it leaves that copy where it stands.
