## detail

Describe this process with SIPOC before adding more rules to it.

THE OWNER ASKED FOR IT by name: suppliers, inputs, process, outputs, customers.
It is the frame for agreeing where work comes from and who it is for, and this
system has grown a queue, two roles, a spec state, a refusal and a nudge without
anybody drawing the whole thing once.

AND IT NAMES A GAP THE OWNER FOUND WHILE SAYING SO: a submitted SPEC is a
reviewer's workload too. The nudge counts finished work waiting for review and
does not count drafts waiting to be agreed, so a queue full of specs looks
empty. Whatever comes out of this, that count is wrong today.

WHAT SIPOC WOULD MAKE US SAY OUT LOUD:
- who supplies work. A person, an agent breaking down what it holds, and a
  reviewer handing one back.
- what the inputs are. A problem, criteria, and the method for judging them.
- the process. Draft, agree, work, run the criteria, submit, judge, close.
- the outputs. A closed token, a lesson, and a change to the tree.
- who the customers are. The owner, the next agent, and a reader in six months.

WHY IT IS WORTH DOING RATHER THAN SKIPPING: every rule in this system was added
where a failure happened, which is the right way to find them and the wrong way
to know whether they fit together. The nudge, the refusal at three, the stop
claim and the spec gate all decide what happens when work piles up, and nobody
has looked at them side by side.

RELATED: wk-d1b585b7b9, the engine nudges, which asks whether the nudge replaces
the wall or sits beside it. That question is easier to answer once the process
is drawn.
