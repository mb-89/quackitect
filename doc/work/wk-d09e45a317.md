## detail

staleWindow at src/engine/investigate.go:55 answers `return per, per, actors`. The window it hands back is the per-actor number, never multiplied by the actors present, though the comment above it says "THE NUMBER IS THE HOLDER'S OWN TURNS, so the window is multiplied by the actors present".

1. quietHold reads that window, so a holder is called stale after ten pulls by anybody rather than ten turns of its own. With fifteen actors present, one turn each is enough. Measured in session 20260904-164423: .se/arrivals.json says 78 pulls across 15 actors, worker-ada last pulled at 63, and the queue answered investigate on wk-32c0df8d1c while worker-ada was writing src/engine/hook.go under it. One alarm, one live worker.

2. TakeBackWhatWasLookedAt reads the same window in its StillPulling guard, so the walker sent to look releases the hold off that live worker on its next pull. That is the theft the guard exists to refuse, back through the number it reads.

3. The notice says "past a window of 10: 10 per actor across the 15 actors present", which reads as a window of 150 and prints 10.

## proposed action

staleWindow returns per*actors as the window, keeping per and actors as it reports them.

## approach

`staleWindow` keeps its signature `(window, per, actors int)` and answers `per * actors` for the window. `per` and `actors` come back unchanged. The notice goes on printing both halves, so a reader still sees the rate the window was taken at.

Nothing else moves. `quietHold` and `TakeBackWhatWasLookedAt` already read the window from this one function. Both are corrected by the one line, and neither learns the arithmetic for itself.

The new test asserts on `staleWindow` itself, not through `quietHold`. The take-back guard reads the same number, and a test that went only through the queue would leave that half unwatched.
