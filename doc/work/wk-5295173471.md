## detail

The tree was put in god mode from the button, and it came back to bound a moment later. Nothing was clicked.

The ruling: the only thing that ends god mode is another click on the button. Nothing else may put the rung back.

Four places could write the binding without being asked:

- a lapse or a timeout on the rung
- a projection or an engine start restoring a default
- a swap
- the extension, if it writes the rung when it redraws

This is the class where state is reset by somebody else, with nothing saying who did it or why. The binding is the most dangerous instance of it. A rung that drops itself puts guards back on a person who took them off.

## approach

The binding records the name of whatever last wrote it, beside what it says. Every writer names itself, so a drop can be attributed rather than guessed at.

Then a test drives each candidate writer over a tree in god mode, and reads the binding after. A writer that puts the rung back without a click is the defect, and the test names which one it is.

## done when

- the binding carries the name of its last writer, decided by: read the binding after a change from the button
- a swap over a tree in god mode leaves god mode standing, decided by: a test that swaps and reads the binding
- an engine start over a tree in god mode leaves god mode standing, decided by: a test that starts and reads the binding
- a projection over a tree in god mode leaves god mode standing, decided by: a test that projects and reads the binding
