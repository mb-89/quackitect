## detail

A WALKER WHO RULES A HOLDER ALIVE CANNOT SAY SO, BECAUSE THE ONLY WAY ON IS A PULL, AND A PULL IS THE RULING THAT IT IS GONE.

The notice offers two answers. Rule it gone by pulling again. Rule it alive by naming your own work and carrying on.

The second does not hold, because carrying on ends in a pull. A worker submits through pull and asks for work through pull. So the next pull after the look is read as the gone ruling, whatever the walker decided.

Measured here in September 2026 on wk-2b8f45c1e7, held by a reviewer. I looked, found no stop record and a last act reading the proxy script, which is what an agent does before retrying a push. I ruled it alive. The owner had looked earlier and ruled the same way in writing.

I then submitted an unrelated scratch note of my own through pull. The record says wk-2b8f45c1e7 was taken back from reviewer-hokusai by me. Two hands ruled the holder alive and the hold came off anyway.

The claim survived, and so did claimed_by, so a live reviewer is not robbed outright. What goes is the hold, so the queue may hand a token to a second hand while the first is still writing its verdict.

This sharpens the parent, which asks for a ruling to be kept. Ruling alive is not expressible at all, so there is nothing to keep.

## proposed action

Separate the two answers. A pull that carries a submission, or names another token, is not a ruling on the look. Rule gone with a word that says so, and let a walker submit and carry on without touching the hold.

## done when

- a pull that carries a submission for another token leaves the looked-at hold exactly where it was, decided by a test that looks, submits and then reads the hold
- a walker can say the holder is alive in a call that is not a pull, and the hold stays
- ruling gone still takes the hold back, decided by the existing take-back test
