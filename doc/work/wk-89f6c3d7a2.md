## detail

CLASS: A REMEDY THAT SWAPS A FRAGILE ADDRESS FOR A LITERAL THE PRODUCER NEVER WRITES. A record keeps a pointer that breaks when the thing under it moves: a line number, an index, an offset. The right diagnosis is reached, that the pointer is an assertion about something nobody controls, and the remedy is to keep the CONTENT instead and look it up by its words. Then the content turns out not to exist as words anywhere, because it was rendered rather than written. A test message comes out of a format string, a log line out of a template, a page out of a component, so what the record holds and what the source holds are two different strings by construction rather than two spellings of one. The remedy reads as obviously more durable than what it replaces, which is why nobody counts. IT IS WORSE THAN LEAVING THE POINTER, because the old form was merely stale and the new one cannot be produced at all, so the migration stalls on rows nobody can convert and the check written for the new form refuses the record it was written to accept. MEASURED ON wk-42adef4818: of 62 recorded observations, 32 name an address, and the message text of 26 of those 32 appears nowhere under src, because every one came out of an Errorf. INSTEAD, AND THE FIRST HALF IS WHAT STOPS IT BEING MADE: before replacing a pointer with content, go to the thing that PRODUCED the content and ask whether it holds it as text. A format string does not, a template does not, a renderer does not, so in those cases the durable handle is the name of the producer, the test, the template, the function, and not the words it emitted. Choose the handle from the producer's side rather than from the record's side, because the record shows you the output and the output is the one thing that was never written down anywhere. THE SECOND HALF IS THE CHECK THAT CATCHES IT, and it is a count you can run before writing a line of the remedy: take every row already in the record, apply the new rule to each, and count how many it can convert. Paste that count into the draft. A migration whose rule converts a minority of the rows it is about is a rule chosen from the examples somebody happened to look at, and the count is the only thing that says so before the work starts.
