## detail

CLASS: A FORBIDDEN SET NAMED IN THE DETAIL, AND CRITERIA THAT ASSERT THE ONE
MEMBER THE EASIEST INSTRUMENT CAN ALREADY SEE.

The detail says what a rule refuses and names four things. Then the criteria
are written, and one of the four is a string sitting in the payload while the
other three need something the engine has not got yet: a diff of the note
before and after, or a judgment. So the criteria assert the string, the list
reads as covered because the detail still names four, and the three that were
dropped are the three the rule was written for.

THE INSTRUMENT CHOOSES THE MEMBER, AND NOBODY NOTICES BECAUSE THE CHOICE IS
NEVER WRITTEN DOWN. Nothing in the draft says the other three were considered
and left out. They are the ones no criterion mentions, and an omission looks
exactly like an oversight because that is what it is.

THE TELL. Read the detail's forbidden list, count it, count the criteria that
decide a member of it, and see whether the two numbers agree. Then ask of each
missing member what the engine would have to look at to see it. If the answer
is harder than reading a field, that is why it is missing.

MEASURED ON wk-cae83b6e63. The detail's WHAT MAY NOT names four: a criterion, a
command, a detail's claim, and any file under src or util. One criterion
decides one of them, a path outside doc/work, and the same paragraph says the
engine holds this rather than the reviewer remembering it. A repair whose path
is doc/work and whose change rewrites a criterion satisfies every criterion on
the list.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD.

WHAT WOULD HAVE STOPPED IT BEING MADE. Write one criterion per member of the
forbidden set BEFORE choosing how any of them is checked, so the instrument is
chosen after the set rather than the set after the instrument. Where a member
needs an instrument that does not exist, the instrument is the work: say what
it has to look at, a diff of the loaded note against what was loaded, and make
that the criterion. A member left out on purpose is written into the detail
with the reason, so a reader can tell it from an oversight.

WHAT WOULD HAVE CAUGHT IT. Count the members of the forbidden list and the
criteria that decide one, and put both numbers in the draft. Then write the
case that satisfies every criterion and breaks the detail's sentence. If that
case takes one line to write, the set is covered by one member and the draft is
not finished.
