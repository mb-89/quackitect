### Why the compounding pair is recorded and not collapsed

The ceiling requirement and req-a-wrong-act-never-passes-silently measure one thing. Both grade a wrong act passing with no signal. The standing one grades it product-wide; this one grades the same failure inside a bound run, where the wrong act is a read that should never have resolved.

THEY ARE NOT MERGED, and the reason is blast radius rather than wording. A wrong act passing silently anywhere costs one bad act. A read resolving past the ceiling costs every number taken under it, and the report still looks valid.

The other nine standing candidates share no axis with anything here. They are about call latency, resumption, console-free operation and newcomer comprehension, and a benchmark run touches none of them.

### Why the order is what it is

RULED BY WHAT EACH FAILURE COSTS.

- THE CEILING FIRST. If it leaks, the run read the answers and every number under it is wrong in the flattering direction while the report still looks valid. It is the only one of the three that produces a confident wrong answer.
- THE CONDITIONS SECOND. A report missing its conditions cannot be paired with anything, so it is useless — but useless VISIBLY. Nobody acts on it wrongly.
- THE CONCEALMENT LAST. Anchoring on a previous run's numbers biases a result rather than inverting it, and the bias is one-directional and arguable.

The order follows the damage grades — fatal, crippling, corrosive — rather than being asserted beside them. Nothing was typed as a weight, and the standing set was not re-ordered.
