## anything_else

THE OWNER ASKED ONE QUESTION AND IT INVALIDATED A VERDICT.

Where does the one millisecond bound come from?

IT CAME FROM ME. I wrote it into if-benchmark-binding-to-guard at decompose-structure. It is the only millisecond bound in the corpus — every other interface declares one second, or says plainly that one second does not apply. A search of the interface folder returns seventeen bounds and exactly one of them is mine.

SO THE FIGURE WAS A THOUSAND TIMES TIGHTER THAN THE ONLY CONVENTION THIS PRODUCT HAS, and this spike returned `falls` against it, and that verdict was on its way into the fold-back as evidence that a checked ceiling cannot meet its bound.

A VERDICT AGAINST AN INVENTED THRESHOLD IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT THE DESIGN. It is evidence about the threshold.

WHAT THE REASONING GOT RIGHT. The guard sits under every resolved commit, ref and path a bound run makes, so a benchmark that slowed the walk it measures would measure itself. That constraint is real and it is why a bound belongs here at all.

WHAT IT GOT WRONG. It turned a constraint into a number without deriving it, and then measured against the number.

THIS IS THE FOURTH TIME IN THIS ITERATION that something looked like a finding and was an artefact of how it was measured. The first three were absences read as results. This one is different and worse: a threshold I authored, then tested against, then nearly reported as though the machine had told me something.

THE CONTROL THAT WOULD HAVE CAUGHT IT is the one this iteration keeps rediscovering — check what the corpus already says before asserting a figure. Seventeen bounds were one search away.
