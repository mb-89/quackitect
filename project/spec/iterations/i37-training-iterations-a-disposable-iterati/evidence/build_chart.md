## why_these

THE THREE ARE A SAMPLE ALONG ONE AXIS: how much the design pays up front to make its own guard trustworthy.

- cand-the-thin-run leans on what git already answers and on a control run beside each measurement. Cheapest, and it trusts a checked guard.
- cand-the-guarded-run pays for a history that cannot express a future commit, one visibility rule every verb derives from, and a forbidden request per run. Most expensive, and nothing about it fails quietly.
- cand-the-refusing-run sits between them. It keeps the cheap ceiling but moves the safe default earlier: a run that cannot establish its own guard never binds.

THREE ROWS ARE THE SAME CELL IN ALL THREE CANDIDATES, and that is a finding rather than laziness.

- The content export won every row it was in. It was probed, it took seconds, and the owner's engine ruling had already killed the alternatives.
- The agent is told. Ruled by the owner; the row has one live cell.
- Measuring rides an ordinary walk. The row has one cell, and it is the only route by which the honesty assumption could ever be probed.

WHAT THE SPACE HOLDS THAT THESE THREE DO NOT.

- The full cross product is well over a thousand curves. These three are a sample, not a shortlist arrived at by elimination.
- Nothing here combines the unrepresentable ceiling with taking conditions from the log. That corner is cheap-and-strict and was left out because the two ideas pull in opposite directions on cost.
- Nothing draws the replay-a-log option for the past state. It is in the box because git IS that log, but as a distinct mechanism it duplicates the export at higher cost.
- Nothing draws the ratio-against-a-reference cell. The owner ruled cross-iteration comparison out, so the cell stays in the box as a rejected option rather than as a live alternative.
