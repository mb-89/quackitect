## follow_up

THREE ACTS RAN, IN ORDER. Each is reported on its own, because each is a different kind of decision.

### One, the cut — thirty-seven struck, blind to importance

Every strike carries the same shape of reason: no pick in any of the five clusters changes the mechanism that serves it, so all five candidates meet it identically BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by resemblance.

The test is mechanical and re-runnable. Take the twenty-five picks, ask whether any touches the mechanism the axis measures, and strike only where the answer is no for all twenty-five.

THIS ACT IGNORED THE DAMAGE GRADE ENTIRELY, which is the point. Ten of the struck rows are crippling. Being important does not make an axis a differentiator.

### Two, the sort — roughed out by machine, then checked

The rough order is the engine's, read from `breaks_how_badly`: 2 fatal, then 27 crippling, then 39 corrosive, then 4 abrasive. Nobody typed it.

THE CHECK FOUND ONE ROW TO MOVE. [[req-overlay-survives-update]] is graded fatal and reads as crippling. The fatal test is that the product stops being the thing it claims to be. An overlay that does not survive an update is a failed upgrade, which is the crippling test.

IT IS MOVED RATHER THAN RE-GRADED. Re-grading the register to change my own sort is the aim the mechanism exists to prevent. A move is visible, carries its reason, and leaves the register alone.

THE MOVE CHANGES NOTHING ABOUT THE OUTCOME, and that is worth saying. It sits above the line either way. A move that happened to rescue something would deserve more suspicion than this one.

WHAT THE CHECK COULD NOT SETTLE. Twenty-seven rows are tied inside the crippling band and the machine has nothing to say about a tie. They keep their arrival order rather than being ranked by feel, because a made-up order inside a band reads exactly like a real one.

### Three, the line — drawn on the scale, not on a count

The cutoff sits at the boundary between CRIPPLING and CORROSIVE.

NINETEEN LIVE AXES STAND ABOVE IT: twenty-nine rows to the line, minus the ten struck among them.

WHY THERE AND NOT AT ELEVEN. The boundary says something a reader can check. Above it, losing the axis ends the product or stops a use case. Below it, the system still works and people route around the damage. A line at eleven would say only that somebody stopped counting.

Nineteen is above the reference of eleven, and the extra rows earn it by being crippling rather than by being liked. Sixteen live corrosive axes fall below the line, out by position and owing no reason of their own.

LAST TIME THIS STATE DREW NO LINE AT ALL. Thirty-five axes stood, the cutoff sat on the last row, and the paragraph written for it argued its way out of a decision the method had already made.
