## neutrality

Seventeen nodes. One root, sixteen beneath it. Every one of the 146 requirements is satisfied by exactly one, so the coverage closes both ways with no row counted twice.

RECUT ON THE OWNER'S RULING, from thirteen. The first cut had hold-the-work carrying the whole record lifecycle: the desk, opening, landing, closing and the archive. Twenty-three rows under one function, a sixth of the register.

WHAT hold-the-work ACTUALLY MEANS: you can leave the walk and pick it up again, and because you must be able to come back, the work has to be held somewhere while you are gone. Seven rows follow from that one sentence, including two that used to float at the root.

FOUR FUNCTIONS CAME OUT OF IT.

- route-the-work. The desk advises and does the paperwork. Nine rows.
- land-the-work. Three rows, and the strongest case of the four for standing alone: req-landing-needs-no-close makes landing something that happens DURING the record's life rather than a phase of it, so it runs on a different clock from opening and closing.
- close-a-record. Three rows. A judgment act rather than a cleanup, because every finding gets ruled on before the record can end.
- keep-the-archive. Five rows. The only function that refuses to change anything.

THE NEUTRALITY TEST, asked of each of the four new ones: could two honestly different designs both do this? All four pass. None names a mechanism. The earlier four rewrites still stand, where "serve the pull", "keep the call log", "bind a worktree" and "draw the panel" each named something this system happens to use.

WHAT I WOULD FLAG NEXT, honestly. keep-the-record now carries 18 rows and is the largest. It holds the call log and the trace graph, which I argued are one function because both answer the same question a year later and both are derived rather than written twice. That argument is the same SHAPE as the one that was wrong about hold-the-work, so it deserves the same suspicion. I have not split it, because the owner ruled on hold-the-work and not on this, and re-deriving unasked is how a structure drifts.
