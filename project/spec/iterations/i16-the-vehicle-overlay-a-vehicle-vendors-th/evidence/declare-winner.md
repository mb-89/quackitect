### What the winner does not have, and should take

REASONS. The winner's drift inventory is recomputed from the merge each time, which is why it can never be false and also why it carries no meaning. A derived inventory says a difference EXISTS and cannot say the copy's owner meant it, or why. The runner-up is the only candidate that attaches a reason, and nothing in the field does both.

THE CLOCK. Upstream movement starting a clock on every deviation, where silence is a fault rather than a default, catches a hazard the field misses: an override can apply perfectly and be wrong, because what it overrides changed underneath it for a reason the copy never saw. The winner re-cuts instead, which fixes the override without ever asking whether it should still exist.

AND THE REFUSAL ON AN UNDECLARED CHANGE, which Debian ships and no candidate here has.

### What the winner costs, said at the moment of declaring it

SOMEBODY UPSTREAM HAND-WRITES A MIGRATION FOR EVERY BREAKING CHANGE, FOREVER. That is why the mechanism is rare rather than obvious, and it is the honest reason to doubt this line.

ITS FAILURE MODE IS A MIGRATION THAT RUNS, SUCCEEDS AND PRODUCES SOMETHING WRONG. Nothing refuses; the diff is left unstaged for a person, and a person who does not read it has no signal at all. That sits directly against the criterion this iteration ranked first.

AND IT IS THE LARGEST BUILD OF THE FOUR. Identity-keyed resolution, key-level merging, a two-layer overlay, a migration runner, a recorded identity with its refusal, and a change to how the write jail is bounded.
