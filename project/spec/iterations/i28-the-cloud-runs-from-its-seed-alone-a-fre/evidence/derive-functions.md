## neutrality

ONE FUNCTION FAILED THE QUESTION AND WAS CHANGED. The other nineteen passed on the first pass.

[[fn-run-a-governed-walk.stand-up-a-product]] read: "take a computer with nothing on it to a product standing at its own front desk".

COULD TWO HONESTLY DIFFERENT DESIGNS BOTH DO THIS? No. The ending was baked in. A machine that finishes standing at a front desk is one design; a machine that finishes walking an iteration is another, and the second is exactly what this iteration builds. An unattended machine that reaches a waiting front desk has failed.

IT IS THE THIRD AND SUBTLEST TELL from the method card: a function that only makes sense given one design. No word list catches it, because "front desk" is our own vocabulary rather than a vendor's.

WHAT CHANGED: the statement now reads "to a product that is running, whether it stops at its own front desk or walks straight into work". The function is the same act with its ending left open.

HOW IT WAS FOUND, and it is worth keeping: by asking what this function would be called in a design we rejected. In the unattended design it would be called "stand up a machine", and the fact that the two names differ was the tell.

THE OTHER THREE ASSIGNMENTS NEEDED NO CHANGE. share-the-pool, hold-the-work and serve-a-step each already stated what they do without naming how.

### Both walks were run, and they disagreed once

THE REQUIREMENT WALK assigned each of the seven new rows to a function. THE USE-CASE WALK checked that every step and extension of [[uc-start-an-unattended-machine]] lands on a function.

THEY DISAGREED ON THE START. The requirement walk put the entrypoint on stand-up-a-product without complaint. The use-case walk asked where step 6 lands — the agent boots and walks — and stand-up-a-product's statement said the journey ends at a front desk. The step had nowhere to go.

THAT IS THE FOURTH HOLE from the method card, found by the walk the card says finds it: a use-case step no function covers, invisible to the requirement walk.
