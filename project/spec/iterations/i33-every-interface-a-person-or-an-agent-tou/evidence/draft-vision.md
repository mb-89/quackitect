## goal_system

THREE CONFLICTS, each between goals that already stand, each ruled here because ruling them later is expensive.

### One: the one-second rule pulls against the rich packet

THE PULL SHIPS EVERYTHING A STATE NEEDS IN ONE ANSWER — guidance, the form, the options, the legal tools, the templates. That richness is exactly why an agent can walk without asking questions, and it is a goal of the design rather than an accident.

IT IS ALSO WHY THE RULE BREAKS. Answers of 300,000 characters are routine and one submit cost 33,456 ms. The two goals meet on the same call and pull opposite ways.

RULED: COMPLETENESS WINS ON CONTENT, SPEED WINS ON DELIVERY. The packet must still carry everything the state needs — dropping content to hit a number would trade correctness for latency, which is the trade this whole system exists to refuse. What changes is WHEN it arrives. Streaming the walk the way a game streams a level keeps both halves true, and that is why note-c8e5a398b943 was pulled into this round rather than treated as a nice idea.

THE LOSER IS THE ASSUMPTION THAT ONE CALL MUST CARRY ONE STATE'S WHOLE WORLD, not the completeness itself.

### Two: the one-second rule pulls against a complete log

EVERY CALL IS LOGGED RAW AND IN FULL, and that is what makes the record trustworthy. The lane's own guidance promises that a result a host moved to disk is re-fetched by ref rather than read from the host's file.

THE PROMISE DOES NOT HOLD. Measured twice today: a survey answer re-fetched by ref came back with 56,238 characters cut. So the system has ALREADY resolved this conflict in favour of size, silently, and broke a stated promise doing it.

RULED: COMPLETENESS WINS, AND THE PROMISE IS THE REASON. A log that cuts is worse than a log that is slow, because a reader who follows the documented path lands on a value nobody checked. Either the re-fetch serves the whole record, or the guidance stops promising it. A third option — leaving the promise and the behaviour disagreeing — is the one this rules out.

### Three: transparency pulls against non-intrusiveness

THE OWNER'S FRAMING ASKS FOR BOTH IN ONE BREATH: everything over a second "needs to be non-intrusive", and must be "very transparent about how slow it is".

THOSE ARE IN TENSION AT THE EXTREMES. The most transparent surface is a loud running commentary. The least intrusive is silence.

RULED: HONESTY WINS OVER QUIET, and the owner's own wording decides it — a person is never left guessing whether it is working. Non-intrusive constrains the FORM of the telling, never whether the telling happens. A silent control is the failure mode, and the stop-at button reported this morning is exactly that: it declined, correctly, and said nothing.

### What no conflict was found between

THE MODELLING AND THE SPEED do not compete, though they look like they might. Modelling the boundaries costs one milestone and buys the enumeration that makes the check mechanical. The risk that it is ceremony is real and is handled as a red-team finding and a minted assumption, not as a goal conflict.
