## set_criteria

- complete: the five rows cover every demand in scope that can fail on its own. The scope's remaining items are the same demand at another surface, or they are mechanism rather than demand. The cubic comparison walk and the battery's tallest pole are both HOW req-surface-answers-in-one-second and the battery budget get met, and mechanism named as obligation is design frozen into a row.
- consistent: no row contradicts another, and the one place two rows touch is deliberate. req-surface-answers-in-one-second and req-call-answers-in-one-second share a number and split on the boundary where it is measured, which the sibling rule requires because they verify differently.
- affordable: four of the five are small engine changes behind interfaces that already exist. The fifth, req-surface-answers-in-one-second, is the only one whose cost is unknown, and that is exactly why req-scoped-run-records-its-timings is a must and runs first.
- bounded: every row carries a number or a state, not an adjective. 1000 ms at the mirror boundary, the response bound at the pull, shipped excluded from the open list, more than one record open at a container.
- comprehensible: each row states one condition and one response, and the detail that would have made a statement long sits in a Detail section that binds. A reader who knows nothing of this record can tell from the statement alone what would falsify it.
- no_tbd: zero. No row carries TBD, TBC, TBR or a question-mark marker, and none was written with a placeholder to be filled later.
- behaviour_modelled: one row wanted a model and got one. req-container-offers-its-records carries a three-branch model, because its fault is a MISSING BRANCH and a missing branch is invisible in prose. The other four are one condition and one response each, so a model would restate the statement in a second notation and give two things to drift apart.

## anything_else

ON WHY FIVE ROWS RATHER THAN TWELVE.

The kickoff pulled twelve items into scope. Turning each into a requirement would have produced a register that looks thorough and decides nothing.

The fan-out heuristic is the guard, and it applies in the other direction too. Most of those twelve are one of two things.

They are the same demand at another surface. Opening the state machine and opening an evidence form are both surface requests, and both are answered by one row with one pass line measured at one boundary.

Or they are MECHANISM. Caching the comparison walk and faking the git seam are how a budget gets met, not demands in their own right. Writing a mechanism into a row freezes design as obligation, and M4 is where the mechanism is chosen.

So the register is short on purpose, and every row can fail on its own in a way somebody would notice.

ON THE ROW THAT IS ALREADY MET.

req-answer-pages-never-overflows describes what the pull already does. Verification status does not belong in a requirement body, so the row states the demand and the record's evidence carries the fact that it currently holds.

It is written because the demand is load-bearing for the value prop's second metric and had no node behind it. A row that is met today is still the thing that would catch it being broken tomorrow.
