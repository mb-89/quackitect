## Mechanism

THE ABSTRACT PROBLEM. Keep a record that answers "why" years later, without
anybody maintaining it, and where the reasoning matters as much as the
outcome.

HOW LAW SOLVES IT. A judgment states the outcome AND the reasoning, in one
document, under a stable citation. A later judgment that relies on it cites
it by that name. Nobody keeps an index of which cases matter: the citation
graph emerges from the judgments themselves, and a case cited often is
thereby important.

WHAT TRANSFERS. This project already keeps decisions and derives a trace
graph, but the two are separate mechanisms. The legal shape says they are one
thing: a decision that cites what it rests on IS an edge, and the graph needs
no derivation pass.

WHAT BREAKS IN TRANSLATION, and it is the interesting half. Law has an
appeals hierarchy, so when two judgments conflict something decides which one
binds. Nothing here ranks two decisions that disagree. The trace shows both
and the reader is on their own.

Adopting the citation shape without the hierarchy would import the graph and
leave the conflict unresolved, which is worse than today: today nobody
expects the trace to adjudicate. A cell that takes this has to say what plays
the appellate court.
