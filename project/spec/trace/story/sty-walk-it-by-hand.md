## Deck

The engineer has a reason not to use an agent today. Client rules, a sensitive tree, or simply not wanting one. The reason does not matter; the system must still work.
|||
The demonstration is OWED to the owner: reports/rpt-walk-it-by-hand.md - minutes at the panel with the slider at 0.

---

They drag the slider to zero. No agent may enter any state on its own from here.
|||
req-autonomy-gates-every-hop: "Where the autonomy setting is zero, the engine shall enter zero steps on an agent's behalf."

---

The panel is unchanged. The same drawing, the same lit node, the same form under it. Nothing was an agent's view that a person now has to do without.
|||
One surface serves both hands: the panel's forms and drawing are the same machinery the agent drives through the lane.

---

They open the reading list and tick a document as read. The checkbox is theirs, and it unchecks itself if the document is edited afterwards.
|||
.se/reading.md is the reading loop as a file, for a person to open - the same proof, person-shaped.

---

They fill the evidence form in the panel, field by field. The reference fields refuse a line that names nothing, exactly as they would for an agent.
|||
The field checks (fieldProblems in engine/stateform.ts) run on the claim, blind to whose hand filled it.

---

They advance the walk themselves. The gates weigh the same, and the ones above the slider are the ones they were always going to decide anyway.
|||
req-autonomy-gates-every-hop's last line: a step above every setting completes from a person's own hand alone.

---

The record that comes out is the same shape. Same forms, same blesses, same trace. The author stamp says a person did it, and that is the only difference.
|||
The engine's actor stamp is a recorded metric with fixed vocabulary; the signed forms carry it either way.

---

The method outlived the AI. The product's claim is not that an agent walks it — the claim is that the walk is right, and an agent is one way to take it.
|||
The procedure to observe it stands: tsp-hand-walk, authored 2026-08-11; the run is the owed piece.
