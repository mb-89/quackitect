## Deck

An engineer sits down at a clone they made this morning and asks the desk what is worth doing.
|||
The desk's own method says the answer must cite the actual current state of the system, and that "what should I do next" is answered by surveying what stands ripest.

---

The desk answers from what it can see, and what it can see is one machine's worth of nothing.
|||
MEASURED ON THIS CLONE, 2026-08-18: `se_survey` reports 0 pending notes and 0 backlog. On the owner's laptop the same call reports 205 parked options. The two machines disagree about what the project is holding, and neither is wrong.

---

They pick from the twenty-four seeded iterations instead, because that is the only durable list of work there is.
|||
AND THAT IS WHY `project/spec/version-planning.md` EXISTS. It is a hand-kept plan standing in for a pool nobody else can read, and the seed says this iteration retires it.

---

Now the parked options are nodes on trunk, so the clone has them the moment it has the repository.
|||
Each carries what it is and its re-entry condition, because draining to backlog already REQUIRES a `where` naming that condition — the field the pool needs most is already demanded at the only door that mints.

---

The desk offers them beside the open iterations, and the engineer commits to one.
|||
That is the commitment point in Anderson's sense: the pool is upstream of it and holds options nobody has promised, and seeding one into an iteration is where the promise is made.
