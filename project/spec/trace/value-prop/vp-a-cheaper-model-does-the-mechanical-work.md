## Success criteria

- A RECORD WALKS TO ITS GATES ON A SMALL MODEL. The states sign, the evidence
  stands, and the gates are reached. Not "mostly" — the same states.
- THE COST OF A WALK IS DOMINATED BY THE WORK, NOT BY RECOVERY. A small model
  spends its calls on the state in its hand, not on working out what the
  machine wanted.
- THE MACHINE CARRIES THE DIFFICULTY. Where a walk needs judgment the model
  cannot give, the machine says so and stops. It never fails quietly and
  reports success.
- THE STRONG MODEL IS STILL WORTH ITS PRICE, and this proposition does not
  claim otherwise. It buys better judgment inside a state. It should not have
  to buy the ability to read the instructions.

## Unlike

[[vp-autonomy-range]] is the neighbouring range and a different axis. It asks
that the product work at every setting of HOW MUCH the agent decides. This one
asks that it work across HOW CAPABLE the agent is. A walk can sit at full
autonomy and still be driven by a model that cannot infer what an ambiguous
instruction meant.

[[vp-rigor-without-toil]] is about the engineer's time. This one is about the
engineer's budget, and the two trade against each other: the reason to reach
for a cheap model is that a cloud session's token bill is what caps how much
work gets sent, and an hour saved is worth nothing if the run was not
affordable.

## What this is not

IT IS NOT A PROMISE THAT EVERY MODEL CAN DO EVERY STATE. Judgment states —
a red team, a design comparison, a verdict — are bought with capability and
always will be. The claim is narrower and harder: THE MECHANICS SHOULD COST
NO CAPABILITY AT ALL.

WHERE THE COST ACTUALLY LANDS, and it is not where it looks. A capable model
reads a wrong instruction, ignores it, and reads the live answer instead. A
smaller one believes the instruction. So documentation that has drifted from
the engine is invisible until a smaller model is pointed at it, and then it is
the whole difference. Guidance quality IS the mechanism this proposition rests
on.
