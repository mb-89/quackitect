## Where it comes from

THE PRIOR-ART SCAN AT THIS ITERATION'S gate-motivation, run live on 2026-08-17,
and it argues AGAINST the ruling made at draft-vision two states earlier.

A 2010 University of Michigan study is reported to have found that the
slow-to-fast bar — technically the most honest representation of how the
process was actually progressing — produced the highest abandonment rate at
21.8 percent. The framing given is that a progress signal carrying discouraging
news drives people away however accurate it is.

PRIMARY NOT SEEN. This is reported through secondary write-ups found at the
scan, and the study itself was not read. Recorded that way on purpose.

## Why the ruling still stands, and what it needs

THE SAME BODY OF WORK SUPPORTS THE RULING'S CORE. People shown a moving
feedback bar were more satisfied and willing to wait around three times longer
than people shown nothing. So TELLING beats SILENCE, which is what draft-vision
ruled and what the stop-at control failed.

WHAT IS QUALIFIED IS THE WORD HONEST. Honest about WHETHER the thing is working
and whether waiting is sensible is not the same as a literal percentage that
reports discouraging progress faithfully.

SO THE RULING SURVIVES AND NARROWS: a slow interface must never leave a person
guessing. It is not thereby required to publish a faithful completion
percentage, and doing so may be the worst available choice.

## Why it is a separate entry

THIS ONE COVERS ONE SIGNAL THAT IS TOO FAITHFUL, mitigated by choosing what the
signal says. Different fix, different entry, per the register's own rule that
two things mitigated differently are two entries.

## What would settle it

A DESIGN REVIEW WITH THE OWNER AT THE SCREEN, because it is a judgment about
what a person feels and no check can make it. The owner holds this entry for
that reason.
