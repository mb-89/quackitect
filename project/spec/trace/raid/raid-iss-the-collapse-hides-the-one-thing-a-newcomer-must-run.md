## How it was found

BY WRITING THE STORY, which is what stories are for. The ramp-up deck could not
be told against the collapsed shape without inventing a step nobody had
specified, and the method says that is a hole in the design rather than a
problem with the story.

## The chicken and the egg

THE EXTENSION CANNOT BE THE ENTRY POINT ON A MACHINE THAT HAS NO EXTENSION.
Somebody arriving with nothing installed has to run the launcher once, by hand,
before opening anything is meaningful.

SO THE FIRST ACT IS STILL A SCRIPT, and the collapse moves that script out of
the first thing they see.

## What they see, before and after

BEFORE: a clone shows the launcher, the entry document, the release notes, the
built packages, two configuration folders and the inner folder. Cluttered, and
the script is unmissable.

AFTER: a clone shows one folder. Clean, and the script is inside it with
nothing at the top pointing down.

BOTH ARE DEFENSIBLE. That is why this is an open issue rather than an argument
against the collapse.

## Candidate answers, none chosen

- LEAVE A POINTER AT THE TOP. One short entry document whose whole job is to
  say which folder to open and what to run once. It costs one file at the level
  the collapse was meant to empty.
- SAY IT IN THE CLONE INSTRUCTION instead, and accept that somebody who lands
  in the folder without reading gets no signal.
- LET THE EDITOR ANSWER IT. Opening the outer folder could be made to say
  plainly that the project is one level down. That is a behaviour rather than a
  file, and it belongs to the entry-point goal.

CHOOSING IS THE DESIGN MILESTONE'S. All three are small, and the first is the
one that costs the collapse something.

## Why it is expected rather than plausible

EVERY FIRST-TIME ARRIVAL MEETS IT. There is no path around it for somebody who
has never installed the runtime, and that is precisely the population the first
story is about.
