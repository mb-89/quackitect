FOUR LANDED BEFORE THE GATE PRICED THEM, built on trunk the same day
this record opened.

- The entry-time tree levelling, which commits a record's method mirror
  before any work starts in it.
- The claim ledger's terminal state, so a shipped record refuses entry.
- The reading credit keyed on content, so a reload re-owes nothing
  unchanged.
- The dependency graph, filled in and rendered.

TWO WERE PULLED FORWARD AFTER THE BLESS, on the owner's ruling: the
pull must never overflow, and the fill answer must stop returning the
whole form twice.

WHY IT IS AN ISSUE RATHER THAN A RISK. It is not something that might
happen. The gate is signed, the scope field does not describe the work,
and both statements are already true.

## Mitigation

THE SCOPE ADDITION IS RECORDED IN THE RECORD'S OWN VISION, because
se_amend is not legal at the state the walk stood in when the owner
ruled. That is honest but it is the wrong home: a gate's scope should
live on the gate.

WHAT ACTUALLY CLOSES IT: write-requirements mints a row for each of the
six, so the register describes the behaviour and verification has
something to check against. Until then the gap is named here.

THE STRUCTURAL FIX IS NOT OURS. A new demand appended to a signed scope
has no verb - amend covers wording that changes no claim, reopen covers
a claim that was wrong, and this is neither. That gap is i18's subject.
It has now cost this record twice in one day.
