# Requirements management, 2026

THE THREE THAT MATTER, by mindshare in the application requirements-management
category as of 2026-05.

- POLARION. 16.0%, up from 14.7% year on year. Traceability rated 9.7. The
  default where Siemens PLM already runs.
- JAMA CONNECT. 11.1%, DOWN from 15.8%. Sells "Living Requirements" and Live
  Traceability, plus an NLP advisor that grades requirement TEXT quality.
  Ease of use rated 8.3.
- IBM DOORS NEXT. The incumbent. Ease of use rated 7.4, the lowest of the
  three, and the reason teams migrate off it.

WHAT IS EVIDENCED, AND WHAT IS NOT.

EVIDENCED: all three have decades in safety-critical and regulated work, with
the certifications that implies, and real customers under audit. This product
has none of that.

CLAIMED BY THE VENDOR, NOT EVIDENCED: Jama markets an Advisor NLP engine that
grades requirement quality. That a feature is marketed is evidence the feature
is CLAIMED. It is not evidence the feature is good, and it is not evidence it
beats anything.

WITHDRAWN, 2026-08-06: this note previously said Jama grades requirement
quality "better than us". That was fabricated. Nobody here has run it, our own
checker does not exist because we have no requirements at M2, and the owner -
who uses these tools daily - reports the opposite in practice. A comparison
needs evidence on BOTH sides, and where one side does not exist it is not a
weak comparison, it is an impossible one.

WHAT THEY SHED. None of them drives an agent. They record requirements that
people wrote and trace them afterwards. The method around them is a convention
their users follow, or quietly do not — nothing in the tool refuses.

WHAT WE TOOK. The trace shape itself: typed nodes, semantic edges, coverage
computed across levels rather than attested. That idea is theirs and it is
sound. What is added is the refusal.

A NOTE ON THE NUMBERS. Mindshare figures come from a comparison aggregator and
move with its sampling. The direction is the signal — Polarion rising, Jama
falling — not the decimal.
