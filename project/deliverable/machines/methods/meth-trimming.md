## Situation

M4 enumerate-space, as one of the parallel finders.

Every other finder adds options. This one removes, and the removal IS the
option.

## THE QUESTION

For each function cluster: what if it does not exist?

Then the follow-up that makes it real. Who does its job instead?

- Another cluster.
- The environment.
- The user.
- Nobody, because nobody needed it.

## WHY IT HAS TO BE ITS OWN FINDER

Nobody proposes it. Asked for options, an agent and a person both produce
things to BUILD, because that is what an option sounds like.

The null option arrives only when something asks for it by name, and it is
regularly the best one on the chart.

## THE FOUR OUTCOMES

- IT GOES, and nothing takes over. The cluster served a requirement nobody
  holds any more. That is a finding for the register, not just a cheaper
  design.
- IT GOES, and another cluster absorbs it. Fewer parts, fewer interfaces.
- IT GOES, and the ENVIRONMENT does it. Somebody else's system already
  solves this, and we were reimplementing.
- IT STAYS, and now you can say why. A cluster that survived the question is
  better justified than one nobody asked about.

## THE ADJACENT MOVE: SUBTRACTION

SIT's pattern, and it is a step short of trimming. Remove a component, then
ask what the remainder is now good for.

The difference matters. Trimming asks who takes over the job. Subtraction
asks what the mutilated thing has become, and sometimes the answer is a
different product.

## THE FAILURE MODE

Trimming what is cheap instead of what is expensive.

The interesting question is always about the biggest cluster, the one that
looks structural. Removing a small support function saves nothing and feels
productive, which is why it is what people do.
