## Situation

Every register entry carries two grades: `breaks_how_badly` says what it
costs ([[meth-damage-scale]]), and `how_likely` — this scale — says how
probable the paying is. Both are ordinal words with tests, so a grade can be
argued with, and neither wears a number nobody measured.

## THE THREE LEVELS

- **expected** — it happens on the current course; only the timing is open.
- **plausible** — one ordinary, nameable event produces it.
- **conceivable** — it takes an unusual combination of independent things.

## The test for each

- EXPECTED. Name the standing condition that produces it. If that condition
  already holds today, the grade is expected.
- PLAUSIBLE. Tell the story in one sentence with no coincidence in it. If
  the sentence needs an "and also", the grade is conceivable.
- CONCEIVABLE. The story needs two or more independent things to go wrong
  at once.

## What the grade means per kind

- risk — how likely it comes true.
- assumption — how likely it is false.
- issue — how likely it stays unresolved and bites again.
- dependency — how likely it fails to arrive as promised.
- decision — how likely it proves wrong.

## NEVER A NUMBER

A percentage nobody measured reads exactly like one somebody did — the
register's standing rule, kept. The corpus works the same way: FMEA rates
Occurrence and Severity as graded levels and multiplies ranks, and risk
management estimates probability times damage through graded judgment,
never through invented precision.

CONCEIVABLE IS NOT IGNORABLE. The corpus's compounding case: every single
failure had its mitigation, and a compound of two conceivable events
defeated them all. A conceivable likelihood on a fatal damage grade is
exactly the entry a gate must read.
