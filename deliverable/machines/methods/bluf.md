# BLUF and the inverted pyramid — the bottom line comes first

**What.** Two names, one discipline. BLUF (bottom line up front, from
military writing): the answer, decision, or request stands in the first
sentences; justification follows. The INVERTED PYRAMID (journalism):
the most important facts first, detail in descending order — so the
text survives being cut from the bottom, and the reader can stop
anywhere without missing the point.

**When.** Every answer, report, summary, and email. Especially when the
reader is busy, the message is long, or the harness might eat the tail.
Progressive disclosure (deliverable/machines/methods/progressive-disclosure.md) is the same
idea for whole documents and interfaces.

**Steps.**
1. Write the bottom line as one or two sentences: #work

   what happened, what
   is needed, or what was decided.
2. Put it FIRST — before context, before narrative, before caveats. #work
3. Order the rest by falling importance #work

   Not by the order you did the
   work or thought the thoughts.
4. Cut test: #work

   delete the last paragraph. If the text is still complete,
   the order is right, so repeat.

**Output.** A text whose first sentence could stand alone — and whose
tail is detail, never the point.
