# Analogy transfer — steal the shape from another domain

**What.** Ask how OTHER domains solve the abstract version of the
problem — other industries, other sciences, nature (bionics). Transfer
the mechanism, not the surface.

**When.** The domain's own conventions are exhausted, or the problem
smells like something with a famous solution elsewhere.

**Steps.**
1. Abstract the problem one level: #work

   not "archive rendering" but "showing
   a large history without drowning the reader".
2. Name EVERY domain that faces the abstract problem. #work
   - libraries
   - air traffic
   - forestry
   - immune systems
   - logistics
   - epidemiology
   - finance
3. Describe each domain's mechanism in ITS terms, honestly. #work
4. Transfer: #work

   what is the mechanism's equivalent here? What breaks in
   translation?

## NAME MANY DOMAINS, NOT THREE #work

This card said "name three domains", and three was never the method. It was
the number one person could speak to.

The bottleneck was always the searcher's own reading, and that bottleneck is
the thing that moved. Name the domains that genuinely face the abstract
problem, then say which ones you could not describe honestly — an admitted
gap beats a confident paragraph about a field nobody knows.

## NATURE IS A DOMAIN, AND IT HAS ITS OWN NAME #work

BIOMIMETIC DESIGN IS THIS METHOD with biology as the source. It is named
separately in the current literature — iDesignGPT (Nature Communications,
2026) runs "biomimetic design" beside SCAMPER, TRIZ and morphological
analysis — and it is not a separate finder here, because abstracting one
level and transferring a mechanism is the identical move.

Sweep it deliberately anyway. Immune systems, foraging, thermoregulation and
swarm behaviour solve coordination and scale problems that software keeps
rediscovering.

**Output.** One [[option]] node per transferred mechanism, each naming its
source domain and what survived the translation — and what deliberately did
not. A transfer with no losses was not a transfer.
