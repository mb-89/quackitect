statement: THE CENTRAL RISK OF THIS ITERATION, and it is the goal system's G1-vs-G2 conflict made concrete. Every check moved from the agent's prose to a lint is a new way for the machine to refuse. If a lint fires wrongly - blocking a correct build over a rule it misunderstands - the agent's rational response is to work around it, and the iteration will have replaced 'the tools are too weak to use' with 'the checks are too dumb to satisfy'. That is the same failure with better branding, and it would arrive with the authority of a mechanical verdict, which is harder to argue with than a missing feature.

trigger: "The FIRST time a lint refuses work that was correct. Ruled response, decided now so it is not negotiated under pressure: a lint that fires wrongly is a DEFECT TO FIX OR REMOVE, never a cost to absorb and never something to route around - and the routing-around is itself reportable under se.law-workaround-then-record-the-gap. Design consequence carried into M4: prefer lints that compute a FACT the evidence must match (did these tests run red, does this requirement name a verify_method) over lints that judge INTENT, because the first kind cannot be wrong about a correct build."
