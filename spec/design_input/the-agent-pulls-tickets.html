The agent pulls tickets

Level one, drawn. The record is the design input beside this page, spec/design_input/the-agent-pulls-tickets.md, on claude/relaxed-knuth-f0uk4d. This page draws that note and adds the one thing the tree gives no home to yet: what the field does with the same problem.

A red team, a steel-man and a survey of the field read the first draft, and what they earn is folded in. Where a sentence here and the note disagree, the note stands and this page is stale.

1The shape

Level zero is agent control, and it stands. Level one is the ticket system. The agent pulls tickets, works the ticket in hand, and pulls again. The ticket system decides which ticket comes back. A person works the same tickets, in the same files, with the same verbs. Processes mint the tickets, and the state machines of level two read them and see where the work stands. The level pays for the work the tree keeps putting off: the design input and design output phases come back as other than the ask, and the retro holds only where a hand produces defined evidence at every step, under a schema.

The agent pulls.

One verb hands it a ticket at a step, and the same verb hands the ticket back. It chooses no ticket, edits no state, and reads no process.

The engine decides.

It hands out the next ticket by rules a person reads as data, checks every hand-back mechanically, and moves the ticket on.

A person works the same files.

Every ticket is a markdown note a person opens, edits and pulls, under the same rules the agent meets, with no refusal.

2A ticket, as a file

A ticket is one markdown note under a schema, kind: [[ticket]]. The file name is its id and holds five words at most, which is the cap the tree already sets on every name. The frontmatter holds what a program reads. The body holds the ask, a chapter per step nested as the steps nest, and a discussion. Shaded fields are the verbs' to write; a hand edit by an agent to any of them is refused.

3The lifecycle and the route

The lifecycle is generic and small. It answers whether a hand may pull the ticket and whether it is finished, and nothing about what kind of work it is. In work and parked are derived, not stored.

draft→ open→ closed · reason derived: in work = a group holds it and a hand holds it parked = open at a person step with a condition

The kind of work lives in the route, which the ticket carries as its own tree of steps. A step with steps under it is a phase, and it carries what its steps share. A step with none is a leaf, and the pull hands out leaves alone. A leaf carries what differs: its own reads, its own hand rule, and the form of its evidence. A review is a step, not a state. So is a test, an approval, a translation or a deploy. Adding a kind of work adds a leaf to a route and moves nothing in the schema.

nota step whose hand this step's hand may not be, as draft or implement

A step is a form with six slots

That is v4's SIPOC ruling, and the route says each slot in one field or derives it. An author writes does on every leaf, and the other four where the default is wrong.

  • A leaf inherits by, reads, on_fail, input, needs, from and to from the phases above it. The reads and the needs add up, from reaches the first leaf, to reaches the last, and the rest take the nearest value.
  • A name refers to a sibling first, and to a step from the top second. A path with a slash, as design/review, says exactly.
  • not: implement excludes the hand of every leaf the mint puts under implement, and on_fail: implement sends the ticket to that phase's first leaf.
  • step names a leaf by its path, as implement/tests-red. The chapters follow the tree: a phase's chapter holds its steps' chapters, a leaf's chapter holds its evidence, and nothing else.
  • A leaf with no on_fail fails back to itself, and an inserted step counts for no not.
  • A checklist adds up from the phases above, the way reads do, and the leaf answers it in a field it takes without naming one, checked. The pull skips a leaf whose when fails to hold and writes skipped and the reason into the record.

Conditions and checklists

The list of conditions is the engine's, and a route names one or none. A checklist is what a hand has to do at this step, and it stands in the process, where guidance stays general. The work answer carries it at the hand-out, so the hand knows what it has to do; the hand-back asks for one line per item on how the hand takes it into account; and the retro grows it, so the items are the tree's memory of what goes wrong. A hand reads them as institutional knowledge, and no tick answers one. The first process the tree writes shows how they bake in, and until the judge reads the lines, the tree trusts the hand.

Red for the right reason

stepthe hand doesthe engine checks at the hand-back
tests-redwrites the tests the ask calls forthe tests the branch adds or changes since the ticket's first take fail, and the failure is an assertion, with no build fault
changemakes the changethe tree builds and lints
tests-greenmakes the tests passthe same tests pass, and the check is green on the commit

The right reason splits in two. The class of the failure is mechanical, and the test verb prints it as the one word expects matches: green, assertion, build or missing, so a build fault or a missing test passes for no red. Whether the assertion names the behaviour the ask describes is a judgement, and the judge reads the failing assertion against the ask where judge.enabled says so. A child ticket stays the unit that changes hands or runs beside another; a sub-step is a leaf, because a leaf costs a chapter and a ticket costs a file, a group and a landing.

Three keywords join the schema checker so the shape holds mechanically: x-one-per, so a chapter stands for every step, nested as the steps nest, under the step's name; x-names, so step names an entry of steps; and x-earlier, so on_fail names a step that stands before its own. The write door, the sweep and the language server read one rule. The checker today reads top-level scalars alone; before the keywords land it learns to recurse into steps and evidence, to keep a line per nested key, and to resolve a path. That is the first branch's real size.

Everything else on the ticket is the engine's. The write door weighs a ticket write against the render of the ticket's own frontmatter, which is the route and the record drawn as chapters. The test verb

work test runs the tests the branch adds or changes since the ticket's first take, or the ones a step names, and answers one word and a reason. A command field names it with the answer it wants, as expects: assertion or expects: green. The verb reads the delta off git, which is the half of v4's test map that a branch already carries, and the record holds the first take's hash, so ticket three of a group runs its own tests and no other ticket's.

answermeans
greenevery test it runs passes

buildthe file loads no test, because the tree fails to build or to parse
missingthe branch changes no test, so there is nothing to run

The diff of the work is the group's branch, and git log on the ticket file is its history. So a ticket carries no time and no line number, and a reader finds both in git. The tickets folder stands beside the rationales in .vale.ini, so an evidence chapter and a discussion may say what happened.

5Processes are routes

A process is a route the mint copies onto a ticket. Level one ships four for tickets and one for a group, as YAML files under spec/processes/, six of them and in the tree already, each under process.schema.yaml and holding a steps list beside the ask's fields, and nothing more. The ask is a form too: the process names its fields with the same forms the evidence takes, the mint renders them under Ask, and the verb that opens a ticket refuses open while one stands empty. The mint takes --process <name>, copies the route, and writes the process file's hash beside it, so the ticket depends on nothing outside itself after that, and a process file changes without reaching a ticket in flight. A fix to a process reaches a ticket through work reroute, which copies the current route over the leaves the ticket has yet to reach, refuses where step names a leaf the new route lacks, and the board flags a ticket whose hash trails its process.

A note is the smallest ticket, and it is where most work starts. A hand that meets an idea, a bug or a doubt mid-work writes one with work note and carries on; its hold stays where it is, and the note carries from, the ticket and step in hand. The note is private and stays on the box, and its ask is one field, line, whose says carries three hints and nothing else: the smallest case that shows it, why it matters, and what a stranger needs in order to act on it. Its one step, decide, is where a retro reads it later and closes it as dropped, as done where the answer is small enough to do on the spot, or as became, naming the tracked ticket the mint cuts from it. Anyone mints a note and a retro decides it: the step says by: retro, and a hand at a retro step is the one hand the pull hands a note to. A group's retro decides the notes its box minted, the tree's retro decides the rest, and a note that stays behind on a cloud box dies with it.

A note also answers. The answer door holds every tool call until the owner's prompt has an answer, and a prompt that says "make a note of this" wants one thing back: the note. So work note writes a note line to the log, the answer door reads that line off the log and counts it where the prompt names a note, and the viewer draws it pink, right under the prompt. The owner reads that the agent understood, and the agent carries on. A note answers no other prompt, so the readback stays owed everywhere else.

The drawing is a projection

A process file is data, and its drawing derives from it. One emitter reads a route, and a record where a ticket carries one, into a graph: a node per phase and per leaf, a pass edge between neighbours, a fail edge back with its label, a dotted node for when, a marked node for a person step, and the pointer, the skips and the returns off the record. One reader draws that graph: the editor, live, for a process and for a ticket with its position. It draws from the file at every open, so no second copy stands anywhere to drift, and nobody else needs the picture: an agent reads the YAML, and a person reads the editor.

Every earlier version wanted that property under the name executable diagram: v1 as a lint-pinned Mermaid subset, v2 and v3 as an Obsidian canvas the engine compiled, v4 as a React Flow canvas fed by a narrating runtime. The tree reaches it from the other side: the data runs, and the picture derives. v1's rules carry over whole: nodes declared before edges, every edge labelled, no coordinates, and the hash over the graph and not the bytes.

The drawing carries no style of its own. The extension holds one common style sheet, and it takes its colours and its type from the editor's theme, which is VS Code's. A cloud box prepares the emitter and the webview's wiring, and spends nothing on graphical design, which is desk work. A ticket's drawing animates nothing, because the record is the run and every hand-back is a push; a process may animate a run later, and since the emitter reads a graph, a feed that moves the pointer adds that and rewrites nothing.

the editor gains, on a desk with the ownerwhat a person does with it

One reader serves both files. The tree's YAML reader reads a process file whole and a ticket's frontmatter; the section reader reads the ticket's body, which holds the prose places. The editor holds those two and no third.

6A group is a branch

A ticket is too small for a branch, and a branch is too heavy for every ticket. So the unit a box works and the unit that lands is a group: a ticket under the group process, and a branch named work/<group> after it. Its children are the tickets naming it under group, and a group inside a group runs on the outer branch, so one schema covers everything that moves and one relation says where work lands. The route is what a cloud run does step by step: sync under when: cloud, then split, where the hand mints the children or assigns standing tickets and where the editor lets a person drag them in, then the children, then a retro phase of three leaves. The children step has two ends: the box leaves it when no child stands at an agent step, and it ends when every child closes. A ticket names its group in one field, and a person writes that field. A group of one ticket is the ordinary case, and a group of five is the same shape.

A hand mints where it means to work. A ticket it means to do itself goes into its own group and lands with it. A ticket it leaves for others carries no group, and a person sorts it later. On a cloud box a loose ticket rides the group's branch and lands at the merge, since the box commits on no trunk. There is no lease: a branch held for a long time is something a person has to look at in any case, so the age of its tip is the signal, and past work.staleAfter the group is implicitly yours. The rule derives from the tip, so nothing writes to a branch nobody holds.

The group's retro, and a cloud box

A cloud box differs from a desk in one leaf, sync, which takes trunk into the branch first, and in the retro's cloud leaf. The retro phase is the group's own retro, and on a cloud box it is the only retro that box ever runs.

A private note stays on its box, so the drain closes each one as dropped with a reason, as done on the spot, or as became, a tracked ticket with its ask filled from the note. A ticket the drain mints names this group, and then the box works it before the group ends, since children has two ends. Or it names no group, and then it rides the branch and lands on trunk at the merge, where a person sorts it. It is open where the drain fills its ask and draft where it cannot. The six lines work new appends to a brief today are this route one for one, so the first cloud run under the pull is the first process running as data.

The retro

The tree's retro turns a window of the record into changes to the machinery. It is blameless, and it repairs the generator of a bad output: a prompt, a guidance line, a form, a refusal, a tool. The route stands in spec/processes/retro.yaml, and a retro is a ticket, so its evidence is the report, in git. The retro keeps missing things because nothing refuses a retro that skips one, so every question is a field on a step, and the engine hands nothing back with a field empty. It reuses the group's shape: collect mints the readers, a children step waits for them, and the retro's own hand mines what collect lays out, leaf by leaf.

work collect drains everything under .se into the retro folder: the log after a rotation, every script anyone writes, a copy of every private note, and the transcripts with their thoughts. It lists the tickets that close in the window with their records, the retro leaves of every group that merges in it, and the earlier retros. The window runs from the last retro's close commit to now, and the verb cuts it into chapters of six hours that hold activity, with the counts per chapter written before anyone reads a word: prompts, tool calls, shell commands, refusals by kind, errors, tickets that move, notes that appear, and the median length of a thought. So the worker's thinning over a long window, which v4 measured by fifths, stands per chapter for free.

Each chapter is a private ticket under chapter, with the counts as its ask and one hand the engine spawns for it, which by: helper names. Its reader answers five things: the work of these hours; what goes well, and what makes it go well; what goes badly, each with its moment in the log or the transcript; how each bad line stops happening, by its home; and what the thoughts say that the actions do not. The rest of the window is one reading, so the retro's own hand does it, leaf by leaf under mine, and collect lays each leaf's material out as a file in the retro folder.

7The pull

The agent holds three verbs: pull, guidance and escalate. Every other verb belongs to a person or the engine. The shell is the verb, under ./RUNME.sh work, and a plugin tool wraps it for a session that holds the plugin. The engine is these verbs and the beat, and it runs with nobody on the box, which is why it stands in level one and outside level zero. A pull carries at most two things: the ticket it hands back and a verdict. The file is the payload, because the hand wrote its evidence through the write door before it pulled. A leaf holding a verdict field takes the verdict from the field, and the flag is refused there.

On pass the engine writes the leaf's entry into record, moves the step, commits the step's work and the ticket as one commit named by ticket and step, pushes, and answers the next ticket, or done to a hand it spawned for one step. A push that fails fetches, rebases that one commit and tries once more, or answers refused and says the branch moved. A hand-back the record already answers gets the recorded answer, and one whose take hash trails the branch is refused as stale, so a retry and a dead box's late push both land nothing twice. On fail it sets step to the row's on_fail, or to the step itself where none stands, counts the return, and at work.failsBeforePerson returns inserts a person step carrying the last findings. The judge runs in the plugin wrapper, since a shell verb reaches no model: a person's hand-back from the shell meets the four mechanical checks, and the judge reads it at the next agent pull on that ticket.

A work answer says one thing to do, and it hands the leaf, not the whole ticket. It carries the ask and the leaf alone: its fields, their forms and what each says, the guidance the leaf and its phases name, and the position: which leaf of how many, under which phase. It names the file and the chapter to write in. The whole file stands on disk and nothing hides it, so a hand that wants the picture reads the file.

8Hands and escalation

A hand is a box, a session on it, and an agent inside it where the harness names one. A helper the session spawns is the session's own hand, and the spawn hook tags it so; only a hand the engine spawns for a step is a hand of its own, so a session's own helper reviews nothing of the session's work. A person's hand is their git author name; the verb refuses by: person where the environment names an agent's harness, and work.personSigns asks for a signed commit as the stronger door. A role is a property of a step, and no agent holds one for life. When the next step's by excludes the hand that pulls, the pull spawns a hand for it through the hook, with a prompt the engine writes from the ticket and the step's guidance. The session sees the outcome and its next ticket, and shapes nothing of the spawn. That answers v4's objection to a session spawning its own reviewer.

A hand that cannot go on without a person calls escalate with the question. The engine inserts a step in front of the current one, by: person, with the question under asks, mints its chapter, and points step at it. The ticket stays open and is now a person's to pull. The inserted step carries one field, answer, of form text, or of form choice where asks carries options. The person fills it and hands the step back, and the engine advances into the step behind it. A gate the mint writes and an escalation the engine inserts are one mechanism; a park is the same, with the condition as the question. The box works the group's other tickets while a person step waits, and when none stands at an agent's step it leaves the group at todo, so a box returns once the person answers on the branch.

9Guidance rides the step

Each step names the guidance it reads. The pull's work answer carries those notes' actionables inline, so the hand holds them the moment it holds the step. The guidance verb answers a named note, or, unnamed, the notes the current step lists and the always-on ones.

The pull is the one road to a hand-back, and the pull hands the guidance. So a step with reads is a step no hand works before the notes reach it, and the engine keeps no quiz to prove it. What it keeps is the arrival, and the arrival says when to hand the notes again.

10What a person sees

Three surfaces draw the folder and the branches, and none keeps a second record: the work editor, which a button in the sidebar opens; work list in a terminal; and the ticket file itself. The board draws what the machine holds, and nothing more: a ticket in a held group shows its group's state and the age of the branch's tip, and no state of its own, since the branch is responsible for it and nobody on trunk touches it. The board fetches on the schedule the tree already has, so a tip nobody fetches yet stays unknown to it, as any reader of git expects. The editor stands outside the sidebar, in a window of its own, and its shape waits for a design note of its own once the back end stands. v4's work editor is the starting point, and it may end as a Kanban board or as something else. 14The order of work

Nine branches, and the dependencies make the order binding. Eight briefs stand on their work/ branches, cut from the note, and each names the chapters it implements; the ninth, the retro as a ticket, waits for the retro's shape, which the note now carries. The editor's live drawing, its block library and the evidence form wait for branch 2 and are desk work with the owner. The five work branches standing today finish under the verbs they carry. A brief branch is one whose tip carries HANDOVER.md, and work list tells the two kinds apart by that; take keeps serving a brief until work list names none; adopt turns a brief into a group ticket and one child for the ones a person wants moved, and the old verbs go once the last brief merges.

15What the field does

Ten systems that organize work for agents, read in September 2026. The mechanics level one keeps are the ones they converge on: a store the person reads, an atomic or arbitrated claim, isolation per unit, one unit per fresh session, a state that means a person must look, and something that recovers a dead agent. None of them makes every write name its ticket, runs processes as data, or forces a reviewer's verdict before close; the traveler on the ticket is where this design stands apart. The review after the first draft took three things the field keys on and the draft lacked: an idempotency key for a retry, which is the take hash; a bound on a fail loop, which is the returns count; and a verdict tied to the diff it read, which is the read list.

16What this leaves open

  • whether a person step may go to a spawned strong model at high autonomy
  • whether yours drowns in parked conditions, and a parked state returns
  • a heartbeat ref per held group, if a tip's age proves too coarse a sign of a dead box
  • two hands on one verdict, which a process may ask for as data once one hand's verdict is measured
  • a WIP limit per step, which is Kanban's one knob and stands outside the note
  • whether a guidance card's own items become steps at the mint, which v3 tried as card marking and level two may take up
  • more forms, such as a number or a date, as the routes come to ask for them
  • animation of a process run, which a feed into the emitter's graph adds later
  • the pull handing a cloud box its retro early when the cap is near, if the platform names the cap
  • the form of a review report, which may take a schema of its own, and what a passing verdict's open points become
  • a plainer word than says on a field

Confidence. The design is the owner's, argued over one afternoon against v3, v4 and the field, and it is a design input: what to build, why, and in which order. Nothing in it is measured yet. The first branch that lands, the schema, is where the measuring starts.
