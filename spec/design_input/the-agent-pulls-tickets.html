The agent pulls tickets

Level one, drawn. The record is the design input beside this page, spec/design_input/the-agent-pulls-tickets.md, on claude/relaxed-knuth-f0uk4d. This page draws that note and adds the one thing the tree gives no home to yet: what the field does with the same problem.

What this page is. The drawings of the level one design, the example ticket as a file, and the comparison with ten systems that organize work for agents. Where a sentence here and the note disagree, the note stands and this page is stale.

What this page is not. A second copy of the note. The rulings, the field table and the order of work live there, and this page points at them where it draws them.

1The shape

Level zero is agent control, and it stands. Level one is the ticket system. The agent pulls tickets, works the ticket in hand, and pulls again. The ticket system decides which ticket comes back. A person works the same tickets, in the same files, with the same verbs. Processes mint the tickets, and the state machines of level two read them and see where the work stands.

The agent pulls.

One verb hands it a ticket at a step, and the same verb hands the ticket back. It chooses no ticket, edits no state, and reads no process.

The engine decides.

It hands out the next ticket by rules a person reads as data, checks every hand-back mechanically, and moves the ticket on.

A person works the same files.

Every ticket is a markdown note a person opens, edits and pulls, under the same rules the agent meets, with no refusal.

2A ticket, as a file

A ticket is one markdown note under a schema, kind: [[ticket]]. The file name is its id and holds five words at most, which is the cap the tree already sets on every name. The frontmatter holds what a program reads. The body holds the ask, a chapter per step nested as the steps nest, and a discussion. Shaded fields are the verbs' to write; a hand edit by an agent to any of them is refused.

3The lifecycle and the route

The lifecycle is generic and small. It answers whether a hand may pull the ticket and whether it is finished, and nothing about what kind of work it is. In work and parked are derived, not stored.

draft→ open→ closed · reason derived: in work = a group holds it and a hand holds it parked = open at a person step with a condition

The kind of work lives in the route, which the ticket carries as its own tree of steps. A step with steps under it is a phase, and it carries what its steps share. A step with none is a leaf, and the pull hands out leaves alone. A leaf carries what differs: its own reads, its own hand rule, and the form of its evidence. A review is a step, not a state. So is a test, an approval, a translation or a deploy. Adding a kind of work adds a leaf to a route and moves nothing in the schema.

step fieldholds
nameone word or a hyphenated pair, unique among its siblings
stepsthe steps under a phase, in order
byanyone, person, agent, children, or not <step>
readslinks to the guidance notes the step's hand reads
on_failan earlier step, where a failed hand-back sends the ticket
asksthe question a person answers, on a step a person takes
evidencethe fields a leaf's hand fills, each with a name, a form and a says
  • A leaf inherits by, reads and on_fail from the phases above it. The reads add up, and the other two take the nearest value.
  • A name refers to a sibling first, and to a step from the top second. A path with a slash, as design/review, says exactly.
  • not implement excludes the hand of every leaf under implement, and on_fail: implement sends the ticket to that phase's first leaf.
  • step names a leaf by its path, as implement/tests-red. The chapters follow the tree: a phase's chapter holds its steps' chapters, a leaf's chapter holds its evidence, and nothing else.

Red for the right reason

stepthe hand doesthe engine checks at the hand-back
tests-redwrites the tests the ask calls forthe tests the branch adds or changes fail, and the failure is an assertion, with no build fault
changemakes the changethe tree builds and lints
tests-greenmakes the tests passthe same tests pass, and the check is green on the commit

The right reason splits in two. The class of the failure is mechanical, and the test verb names it: green, or red with assertion, build or missing. Whether the assertion names the behaviour the ask describes is a judgement, and the judge reads the failing assertion against the ask where judge.enabled says so. A child ticket stays the unit that changes hands or runs beside another; a sub-step is a leaf, because a leaf costs a chapter and a ticket costs a file, a group and a landing.

Three keywords join the schema checker so the shape holds mechanically: x-one-per, so a chapter stands for every step, nested as the steps nest, under the step's name; x-names, so step names an entry of steps; and x-earlier, so on_fail names a step that stands before its own. The write door, the sweep and the language server read one rule.

4Evidence per step

A leaf names its evidence as fields, and each field has a form. The mint renders the fields into the leaf's chapter with a comment each, so a person sees what goes where and an agent meets the same slots. A criterion with no evidence behind it is guidance, so no form takes a tick.

Everything else on the ticket is the engine's. The write door weighs a ticket write against the render of the ticket's own frontmatter and refuses a write that changes anything outside those places, naming the line. A person and an agent meet one form through two doors: the person fills the slots in the editor, and the agent fills them through the write door or hands the fields back as the pull's payload, which the engine writes into the same slots.

The test verb

work test runs the tests the branch adds or changes since the take, or the ones a step names, and answers one word and a reason. A command field names it with the answer it wants, as expects: red on a command field. The verb reads the delta off git, which is the half of v4's test map that a branch already carries.

answermeans
greenevery test it runs passes
red · assertiona test fails on its own assertion, which is the red a tests-red step wants
red · buildthe file loads no test, because the tree fails to build or to parse
red · missingthe branch changes no test, so there is nothing to run

The diff of the work is the group's branch, and git log on the ticket file is its history. So a ticket carries no time and no line number, and a reader finds both in git. The tickets folder stands beside the rationales in .vale.ini, so an evidence chapter and a discussion may say what happened.

5Processes are routes

A process is a route the mint copies onto a ticket. Level one ships three, under spec/processes/, each a note holding a steps list and nothing more. The mint takes --process <name> and copies the route, so the ticket depends on nothing outside itself after that, and a process file changes without reaching a ticket in flight.

processroutefor
notedecidea thing to look at later, with no work in it yet
trivialdoa fix small enough that the ask is the design
standarddesign (draft, review) · implement (tests-red, change, tests-green) · verdicta change that wants an approach first, tests before code, and a second pair of eyes after
complexsplit · children · verdicta change too large to review whole

A note is the smallest ticket, and it is where most work starts. A hand that meets an idea, a bug or a doubt mid-work writes one with work note and carries on; its hold stays where it is. The note is private and stays on the box, and the mint's comment on its ask carries three hints and nothing else: the smallest case that shows it, why it matters, and what a stranger needs in order to act on it. Its one step, decide, is where somebody reads it later and closes it as dropped, as done where the answer is small enough to do on the spot, or as became, naming the tracked ticket the mint cuts from it. On a desk that reading is the retro's. On a cloud box the pull hands a hand its own notes once its group's tickets run out, because a note that stays behind dies with the box.

A note also answers. The answer door holds every tool call until the owner's prompt has an answer, and a prompt that says "make a note of this" wants one thing back: the note. So work note writes a note line to the log, the answer door counts that line as the answer, and the viewer draws it pink, right under the prompt. The owner reads that the agent understood, and the agent carries on.

6A group is a branch

A ticket is too small for a branch, and a branch is too heavy for every ticket. So the unit a box works and the unit that lands is a group: a branch named work/<group>, and a note under spec/groups/ holding its state and its ask. A ticket names its group in one field, and a person writes that field. A group of one ticket is the ordinary case, and a group of five is the same shape.

rulewhat it says
the branch is the claimtaking a group pushes state: held on the group note, and the push arbitrates two boxes
a stale branch is a person'swork list says how long each held branch has stood still, and a person releases or closes it
one box, one groupa box works one group at a time, and the group's tickets one at a time
the branch is the filteron a group's branch the pull sees that group's tickets alone, and widens nothing; a filter a person sets in config outranks the branch
trunk hands out no group's ticketon trunk the pull offers a box a group to take, and a person the tickets of no group; a group's tickets are worked on its branch alone
the branch holds the truthwhile the branch stands, its copy of the group note and of its tickets is the record
a group endswhen no ticket in it stands at a step an agent may take
the merge is a person'swork merge takes an ended group into trunk, and work close drops the branch

A hand mints where it means to work. A ticket it means to do itself goes into its own group and lands with it. A ticket it leaves for others carries no group, and a person sorts it later. There is no lease: a branch held for a long time is something a person has to look at in any case, so the age of its tip is the signal and work list names it.

v4 ran this shape under two words, bucket on the token and group/ on the branch. Now the two coincide, so one word stays: group.

7The pull

The agent holds three verbs: pull, guidance and escalate. Every other verb belongs to a person or the engine. The shell is the verb, under ./RUNME.sh work, and a plugin tool wraps it for a session that holds the plugin. A pull carries at most two things: the ticket it hands back and a verdict. The file is the payload, because the hand wrote its evidence through the write door before it pulled.

A work answer says one thing to do, and it hands the leaf, not the whole ticket. It carries the ask and the leaf alone: its fields, their forms and what each says, the guidance the leaf and its phases name, and the position: which leaf of how many, under which phase. It names the file and the chapter to write in. The whole file stands on disk and nothing hides it, so a hand that wants the picture reads the file.

The pull runs at two levels

who pullswhatthe rule
the beata box per free groupgroups at todo, none of whose tickets wait on an unmerged group, by urgency
the boxthe next ticket of its grouptickets at open with no open dependency, children before parents, a step this hand may take, by urgency then name
a personanythingthe same two lists on the board, and no refusal

The pull writes .se/hold.json naming the ticket, the step, the group and the hand. The tooth's work-waiting check reads it, so a turn ends on a fact where a ticket stands in hand.

8Hands and escalation

A hand is a box, a session on it, and an agent inside it where the harness names one. A person's hand is their git author name. A role is a property of a step, and no agent holds one for life. When the next step's by excludes the hand that pulls, the pull spawns a hand for it through the hook, with a prompt the engine writes from the ticket and the step's guidance. The session sees the outcome and its next ticket, and shapes nothing of the spawn. That answers v4's objection to a session spawning its own reviewer.

A hand that cannot go on without a person calls escalate with the question. The engine inserts a step in front of the current one, by: person, with the question under asks, mints its chapter, and points step at it. The ticket stays open and is now a person's to pull. The person answers under the step's evidence and hands it back, and the engine advances into the step behind it. A gate the mint writes and an escalation the engine inserts are one mechanism; a park is the same, with the condition as the question.

triggerwho insertsthe knob
the hand needs a personthe hand, through the verbnone
the same hand-back meets refused N timesthe enginework.refusalsBeforePerson
a step a person takes, from the mintthe mintnone

A ticket that gathers more inserted steps than work.stepsBeforeSplit is the wrong size. The engine refuses another insertion and asks for a split into sub-tickets, closing the ticket with reason became. v3 measured the identical refusal twenty-seven times in nine seconds; this is the count that ends that.

9Guidance rides the step

Each step names the guidance it reads. The pull's work answer carries those notes' actionables inline, so the hand holds them the moment it holds the step. The guidance verb answers a named note, or, unnamed, the notes the current step lists and the always-on ones.

The pull is the one road to a hand-back, and the pull hands the guidance. So a step with reads is a step no hand works before the notes reach it, and the engine keeps no quiz to prove it. What it keeps is the arrival, and the arrival says when to hand the notes again.

the engine writeswhereit lasts
one log line per note handed overthe logfor the record
the notes this hand holds for this step, by name and hash.se/hold.jsonthe session

The hold says which notes reach this session, so the pull hands them once per step and session, and again on three things: a refused answer, which quotes the rules its findings cite; a compaction, which level zero already notices; and a note whose hash moves. A refresher on every pull costs tokens and buys nothing the hold does not know. The proof of application is the hand-back: the commands, the evidence and the judge.

measurementsaysso
v3, on its own reading probesevidence, not enforcement; a model that skims and guesses passes; boot paid about fourteen reads for themno probe at level one
v5, on the fielda door holds a rule in every run, a prompt in about four runs of fivethe door and the hand-back carry the rule

The standing layer shrinks to the notes that bind every session. A note a step names leaves the layer and rides the step.

Level two is processes as machines, and it reaches the agent by no road. It reads the folder of tickets, derives where each machine stands from the states and steps it finds there, mints tickets with routes, sets parent on them, and reacts to a close by minting the next. The agent still pulls, and the only fact it meets is a route.

14The order of work

Eight branches, and the dependencies make the order binding. The eleven work branches standing today finish under the verbs they carry; adopt turns a brief into a group note and one ticket for the ones a person wants moved, and the old verbs go once the last brief merges.

15What the field does

Ten systems that organize work for agents, read in September 2026. The mechanics level one keeps are the ones they converge on: a store the person reads, an atomic or arbitrated claim, isolation per unit, one unit per fresh session, a state that means a person must look, and something that recovers a dead agent. None of them makes every write name its ticket, runs processes as data, or forces a reviewer's verdict before close; the traveler on the ticket is where this design stands apart.

Two of them are worth naming for what they settle. Symphony's rule that only the orchestrator mutates scheduling state is the pull's rule here: the agent mutates the work, the engine mutates state and step. Azure DevOps and Jira model a multi-hand flow as a custom workflow per work item type, which is a schema change per process; the traveler puts the same flow on the ticket as data, so level one stays still while level two invents flows.

16What this leaves open

  • whether a person step may go to a spawned strong model at high autonomy
  • whether yours drowns in parked conditions, and a parked state returns
  • a WIP limit per step, which is Kanban's one knob and stands outside the note
  • whether a guidance card's own items become steps at the mint, which v3 tried as card marking and level two may take up
  • more forms, such as a number or a date, as the routes come to ask for them

Confidence. The design is the owner's, argued over one afternoon against v3, v4 and the field, and it is a design input: what to build, why, and in which order. Nothing in it is measured yet. The first branch that lands, the schema, is where the measuring starts.
