## decided

The floor is look and shout. Read enough to understand the situation and get a message out, and nothing that changes the world. No policy may deny a floor entry. Every floor use a policy would have denied is recorded. Emergency mode widens the floor, and only the owner may enter it.

## why

A floor a policy can override is a default, and the bug case walks straight back in. So no policy may deny a floor entry, because the one case the floor exists for is the case a policy would not cover.

The floor was kept to looking and reporting. Anything that changes the world would grow the floor into the real permitted set, and then the exception would be the rule.

Every floor use that policy would have denied is recorded. A permanent bypass nobody counts is a bypass nobody notices.

Repair needs powers the floor deliberately lacks, so emergency mode exists and only the owner may enter it. It is bounded, announced and marked. Break-glass is legitimate when it is time-bound, visible while active, and every act under it carries a mark.

## costs

The floor cannot repair anything, so the recovery path is a person, and an agent that cannot reach the owner is stuck. Emergency mode is a real bypass, and its safety rests on being disarmed, which nothing forces. The recorded floor uses are only worth their cost if somebody reads them.

## revisit when

- an agent has to repair the engine with nobody watching
- emergency mode is found armed and forgotten
- the recorded floor uses go unread for long enough to stop earning their cost
