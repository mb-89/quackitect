## decided

A refusal names what is open for that agent and the call that takes one.
It asks over every name the caller answers to.
A list past ten lines says how many more there are and sends the reader to the queue.

## why

A wall that says no and stops there sent the agent looking for a way round.
The refusal is the whole product of this guard, so it carries the next move.

No token is held under a harness name.
A menu built over the raw name told a subagent nothing was open, while the token it had pulled stood open under its pulled-with name.

A list of everything open is not a menu once it runs past the screen.
The queue hands one out, and the refusal says so rather than printing the whole folder.

## costs

The refusal is long, and a reader pays for the menu on every refused call.
It reads the whole token folder on a call that is being refused anyway.
A truncated list hides tokens that may be the right ones.

## revisit when

- the menu costs more to build than the refusal saves, on a tree carrying thousands of tokens
- the panel is always in front of the reader, so a refusal can point at it instead
