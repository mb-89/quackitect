## detail

An agent learns the stop protocol from a refusal, turn by turn, and gets it wrong first.

The chapter is right and complete. doc/guidance/driving-the-engine.md section 3 says stopping is claim then stop, that every call after the claim clears it, an answer included, and that asked is granted on the claim naming it whatever is in your hands. A Go test holds that last sentence, in src/engine/askedisgranted_test.go.

The agent never reads any of it. What reaches the session prompt is the projected actionable alone, one line: stop only with a reason named to the engine, on an interrupt claim asked. Nine sentences of protocol stay in the body, and the body is not projected.

AND THE CHAPTER IS WRONG WHERE IT DOES SPEAK. Line 56 says a second refusal says the claim was cleared, never that it was refused. That is false whenever the agent holds work. The second refusal is the claim being argued with, and the notice says claim two of three. The same sentence is in the refusal itself, in stop.go, in capitals.

No guidance anywhere describes the argument. Every file under doc/guidance was searched for argued, three times and granted. The rule an agent is held to lives in challenge.go, and nowhere a reader would look.

So an agent reads one projected line. It meets a notice saying a repeat means its claim was cleared. Then it is argued with twice, for a reason it was told never happens.

## proposed action

Say the true rule, in the chapter, the actionables and the notice.

1. Correct line 56. A second refusal has two causes, and the notice says which. Either a call cleared the claim, or it was argued with over open work.
2. The chapter gains the argument. A claim over open work is pushed back on twice and granted the third time.
3. The actionable says the order and the clearing, not only that a reason is named.
4. A second actionable says asked outranks the rest, and is granted at once.
5. The same correction goes into the notice in stop.go, which carries the false sentence in capitals.

## approach

The actionables are what the projection carries, so the two sentences become actionables rather than the body becoming projected.

That is the choice this makes. Projecting the chapter body wholesale would put nine sentences into every prompt, and the standing layer is measured in bytes. Two lines carry what an agent gets wrong.

The first names the order and the clearing. Stopping is claim then stop, and every call after the claim clears it, an answer included. The second says asked outranks the rest, when the person's own word is what stopped you.

The check is read before either is written. projections-carry-chapters counts what the output style declares against the chapters, so adding an actionable moves a count it already holds.

The red is a row in askedisgranted_test.go: a plan presented, the person saying wait, and decideStop granting asked rather than plan.
