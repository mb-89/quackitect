## Deck

An iteration is running and I can see something it should pick up. I have
no way to put it there. I can tell the agent in chat, or write a note and
hope, or wait for the next round. None of those lands the work on a step.
|||
THE PROBLEM WAS LIVE ON THIS RECORD'S OWN WALK. The owner saw work that
belonged in the round and said so in chat, five separate times. Each time the
only thing that made it real was the agent opening a token in the same breath —
which is the workaround this slide describes, not the gesture it wants.

---

The round is three states in. Its machine is on screen with counts on every
state. The backlog table is open beside it, filtered to the part of the
system this round is touching.
|||
THE MACHINE AND THE COUNTS ARE SERVED, AND THE TABLE IS MEASURED. 395 rows in
one pane, four top-level groups, the backlog among them with its own count.
`reports/rpt-see-every-open-thing-at-one-glance.md` carries the measurement.

THE FILTER WAS NOT APPLIED, and no screen was looked at. The agent does not
look at one unasked, so the arrangement this slide describes is the owner's to
confirm.

---

I see a backlog item that fits this round exactly. I drag its row out of the
table and onto the machine. While the row is in the air, the states show
which of their buckets will take it — including buckets that were hidden a
moment ago because they held nothing.
|||
NOT OBSERVED. No drag was performed and the in-flight highlighting was not
seen. The drag surface has its own spike in this record —
`run-spikes/cross-panel-drag` settled whether a drag crossing two panels is
buildable here — but the gesture itself is unexercised.

---

I drop it on the state that will do the work. Its count goes up by one. The
item is now owed by that state, and the state cannot be left until it is
settled or moved on.
|||
OBSERVED, and it is the heart of the story. Three tokens standing at the
backlog were moved onto `iterations/i63/fix-findings` mid-round. From that
moment the state carried them, and the walk could not leave until two were
settled as fixed and one was settled `carried` with its finding attached.

THE MOVE WENT THROUGH THE LANE RATHER THAN THROUGH A DROP. The mechanism it
exercises is the one a drop would use, and the hold that follows is identical.
`reports/rpt-steer-a-running-iteration-by-moving-work.md` carries it.

---

Later the agent finds that something further up the round needs rework. It
does not stop and it does not silently patch. It writes an item saying what
must change, and pushes it UP to the state that owns that work.
|||
HALF OBSERVED, AND THE HALF THAT WORKS IS THE HONEST ONE. The agent found
several things needing rework tonight and neither stopped nor silently patched:
each became a token or a note carrying what must change.

THE UPWARD PUSH IS NOT BUILT. A token can be opened at another position with
`at`, which is the mechanism, but no agent pushed rework up a round tonight and
nothing weighs such a push. The story's own closing note says this half needs
the orchestrator round.

---

That push is a signal rather than an order. The item sits on the earlier
state as an open point, visible to everyone, and nothing is reopened yet.
|||
NOT BUILT YET. A token placed at an earlier state would sit there and hold it,
which is the visible half. What does not exist is the distinction between a
SIGNAL and an ORDER: today an open token at a state simply holds that state,
with no weighing step between.

---

At the next gate the open point is on the table. The gate decides: accept
the move and carry on, or refuse and send the earlier state back. Either
way somebody signed for it.
|||
PARTLY BUILT, AND IT MOVED TONIGHT. A gate now refuses its thumb while any
state inside the record still holds open work, and emergency does not lift it.
So an open point genuinely reaches the gate and genuinely stops it.

WHAT IS MISSING IS THE CHOICE. The gate today has one answer — settle it or
move it — rather than the two this slide describes. Accepting the move and
carrying on is not a thing the gate can do.

---

I steered a running round by moving work rather than by talking about it,
and the round can steer itself the same way. Nothing was skipped, and
nothing was reopened without a decision.
|||
THE FIRST HALF HAPPENED AND THE SECOND DID NOT. Work was moved onto a running
round and the round could not leave it. Nothing was skipped: four refusals were
earned tonight by trying to move on.

THE ROUND DID NOT STEER ITSELF. Every move was made by a hand, and the
self-steering half rests on the upward push that is not built.

NOTE, CARRIED FROM WHEN THIS STORY WAS WRITTEN: the second half — the agent
pushing rework upward and an orchestrator weighing it before the gate — needs
the orchestrator round. The gesture and the open point are this round's; the
impact analysis behind them is not. The story was written whole so that half
is not forgotten, and this round did not close it.
