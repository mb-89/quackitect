## Deck

Deciding what to do next means reading a list I cannot sort, cannot filter,
and cannot act on. Seeding a record from something I find there is a
separate act, in a separate place, from a standing start.
|||
THE TABLE NOW SORTS, FILTERS AND ACTS. It is the database card, twice, side by
side, so the sort control, the properties control, grouping and filtering all
come from a surface that already existed rather than from a second one built
for this.

THE SEEDING HALF IS STILL SEPARATE. Nothing was seeded from the table tonight
and nothing in this round joined the two acts.

---

The backlog holds over a hundred and fifty items. Each is a file with a
statement and a re-entry condition. I can list them and I can read them one
at a time. I cannot ask which of them are about the surface, or which are
small, or which are mine.
|||
THE 154 ARE IN THE TABLE NOW, AND THEY WERE NOT BEFORE. The pool's nodes
carried a type the editor's filter did not admit, so a reader saw one row where
154 stood. The rename fixed it and the mint writes the admitted type.

THE COLUMNS TO ASK WITH ARE THERE — statement, place, bucket, slot, status,
source. Whether those are the right questions is untested, because nobody asked
one tonight.

---

I click the backlog's count on the front desk. A table opens: one row per
item, columns holding whatever the items carry — statement, complexity,
priority, the condition that would bring it back.
|||
THE COUNT IS DRAWN AND THE TABLE IS SERVED. The backlog stands as its own
top-level group with its own count, and the pill opens the editor.

THE CLICK WAS NOT PERFORMED. The card was measured by rendering it, not by
pressing anything, because the agent does not look at a screen unasked.

---

I filter. Complexity low and priority high gives me eleven rows, and every
one of them is something I could finish today.
|||
NOT OBSERVED, AND THIS IS THE SLIDE THE STORY TURNS ON. The filter control
ships with the card and was never run. Neither `complexity` nor `priority`
stands as a column on a work token today, so the exact filter this slide
describes could not be applied even by somebody trying.

THAT IS A REAL GAP rather than an untested feature, and it is the strongest
argument that this story is only partly served.

---

I find three that belong together. I drag them onto the plus at the top of
the list. A bucket appears holding exactly those three, and I name it.
|||
THE MECHANISM IS BUILT AND THE GESTURE IS UNEXERCISED. Filing rows under a
bucket changes the grouping and moves nothing; renaming a bucket carries every
row filed under it; a bucket nobody set is absent rather than empty. All three
have cases.

NOBODY DRAGGED ANYTHING. The bucket half of this slide is proven at the seam
and unproven at the surface.

---

I seed an iteration from that bucket. Its three items arrive as the new
record's scope, at its kickoff, without being retyped anywhere.
|||
NOT BUILT YET. Seeding takes a goal and a vision; it does not take a bucket.
Nothing carries a bucket's rows into a new record's scope, and nothing was
seeded at all tonight.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD, today: drain a note to `backlog`, which mints a token into
the pool, and pull it into a record at the next seeding by hand.

---

The round's build steps are seeded, and one of them is always debt reduction.
Small repairs are pulled from the backlog into it, so they get done while the
hands are already in that part of the system.
|||
NOT BUILT YET. No build-step drawing carries a standing debt-reduction step,
and this record's own did not. Small repairs reached this round the other way
round: the owner named them and a token was opened in the same breath.

---

I think of something the backlog does not have. I add a row and write it,
in the same table, and it is an item like any other from that moment.
|||
OBSERVED, THOUGH NOT FROM THE TABLE. The mint route accepts a statement and
places it in the backlog, and the item it writes is indistinguishable from any
other — same file shape, same fields, same store. Its own case proves the
payload lands and the item reads back from the sources the editor reads.

THE ENTRY CONTROL ON THE TABLE was not used tonight.

---

I decided what happens next and did it in one place. The browsing, the
choosing, the grouping and the seeding were four gestures in one table
rather than four tools.
|||
NOT ACHIEVED, AND THIS IS THE HONEST HEADLINE FOR THE WHOLE DECK. Two of the
four gestures are built and unexercised, one is missing a column it needs, and
seeding from the table does not exist.

WHAT THIS ROUND ACTUALLY DELIVERED for this story is the TABLE: everything
outstanding is now in one place, correct, filtered to the type that means
something, and not burying the page. The deciding is still done somewhere else.
