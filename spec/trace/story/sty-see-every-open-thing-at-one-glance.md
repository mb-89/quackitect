## Deck

I cannot see what this system owes me. What stands open is spread across
the notes inbox, the backlog pool and whatever forms happen to be unfilled,
and no screen holds them together.
|||
THE THREE STORES ARE NOW IN ONE TABLE. The work editor reads the work store,
the pool and the pending notes together, and the measurement is 395 rows in a
single pane with four top-level groups.

THE SPREAD WAS REAL. Before the type rename, 154 pool nodes carried a type the
editor's filter did not admit, so a reader saw an empty backlog while the
drawing counted 154. That is fixed and the count now agrees with itself.

---

The machine is drawn on screen, states coloured by whether they stand. That
colour says whether a state is finished. It says nothing about how much work
is inside one, and nothing at all about the two stores off to the side.
|||
THE COLOUR AND THE COUNT ARE NOW DIFFERENT FACTS AND BOTH ARE SERVED. Every
state answers green or not, and separately carries `open` when work stands
there.

TONIGHT THE DIFFERENCE MATTERED. `fix-findings` read green AND carried no open
mark while four tokens were genuinely open — because they were parked at the
backlog rather than at the position. The colour was right and the count was
right; the placement was wrong, and only having both facts made that legible.

---

I open the machine. Every state now carries small counts along its edges:
what it must take in on the left, what it must produce on the right.
|||
THE DATA IS THERE AND THE DRAWING WAS NOT LOOKED AT. `slotOf` derives take-in
from produce off the item's source, and `owedFrom` returns a figure per slot
for any position, live, computed on every look rather than stored.

WHAT IS UNOBSERVED HERE is the drawing itself. The agent does not look at a
screen unasked, so whether the counts appear along the edges is the owner's to
confirm.

---

I read one state without clicking it. Two on the left, three on the right.
That is five things owed before this state can be left, and I knew it in a
glance.
|||
THE ARITHMETIC HOLDS AND THE GLANCE IS UNTESTED. The two slot figures are what
the leaving guard reads, so the sum genuinely is what must be settled before
the state can be left — that was exercised four times tonight.

WHETHER IT READS IN A GLANCE cannot be answered from here. It needs somebody
looking, and it is named in the report rather than claimed.

---

I look across the whole machine. Several states carry nothing along the top
at all. A state with an empty top row is finished, and there is nothing to
interpret.
|||
OBSERVED IN TEXT, ACROSS ALL 64 STATES AT ONCE. `se_surface` printed every
state of this record with its colour and its marks, several times tonight. Five
carried `open` early in the evening; by the validation gate the picture had
narrowed to the states genuinely still owing something.

NOTHING HAD TO BE INTERPRETED. A state either carried the mark or did not.

---

The backlog is on the front desk as its own bucket, with its own count. It
sits beside the states rather than inside one, because nothing in it is owed
by any state yet.
|||
OBSERVED, AND IT IS LOad-BEARING RATHER THAN COSMETIC. The backlog is drawn at
the front desk and is NOT a position, which is exactly why tonight's
record-wide gate rule cannot be held hostage by it: the backlog sits outside
every record's prefix by construction rather than by a list.

IT SHIPS FOLDED so it does not bury the page, and every row under it arrives
hidden rather than being taken back by a script.

---

One screen answers where the work is. I can see what each state owes, what
is finished, and how much is waiting with no home — without a call, a
folder, or asking the agent.
|||
THE ANSWER IS ASSEMBLED AND THE SCREEN IS THE OWNER'S TO JUDGE. Everything this
slide asks for is served from one place: the states with their marks, the two
side stores with their counts, and the rows behind each.

AND THE LAST CLAUSE WAS TESTED FOR REAL TONIGHT — twice the owner corrected the
walk from what they read rather than by asking me what I was doing. That is the
slide's promise working, from the person's side rather than mine.
