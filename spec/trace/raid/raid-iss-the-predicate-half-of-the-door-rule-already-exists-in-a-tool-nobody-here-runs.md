## What was wrong

The pressure test answered its own hostile question, why not just use dependency-cruiser, with DIFFERENT SUBJECT. It said the tool governs module dependencies rather than who may call the filesystem.

That is false against the tool's own rules reference, read at its primary on 2026-08-26.

- Every rule carries a `from` as well as a `to`, so the importer axis exists.
- `not-to-core` is the reference's own example of a rule forbidding dependencies on core modules.
- `fs` and `node:test` are the reference's own examples of the `core` dependency type.

So `from.pathNot` plus `to.dependencyTypes: [core]` is exactly a door rule saying who may reach the filesystem.

## What is still true

The reason half stands, and it is the record's actual thesis.

- dependency-cruiser's baseline is GENERATED. `depcruise-baseline` writes every current violation to a file and lowers each to ignore, so nobody typed a line and nobody can be asked about one.
- Its `comment` field is documented as not used in any rule logic, and it sits on the RULE rather than on the exception.
- It ships a blanket off-switch, `severity: "ignore"`.

## Why this is an issue rather than a note

The record made a comparative claim, the comparison was wrong on one side, and a signed form carried it past two gates.

THE ANSWER WAS ALSO THE ONE PLACE THE OWNER WOULD LOOK. A hostile question exists so a reader can check the weakest point, and this one returned a wrong reassurance.

## What it does not do

IT DOES NOT REOPEN THE WINNER. Choosing to build is the owner's call and this record does not take it back on its own.

What it does is put the true premise in front of that call: the predicate is buyable, the write-time refusal and the reasoned departure list are not.
