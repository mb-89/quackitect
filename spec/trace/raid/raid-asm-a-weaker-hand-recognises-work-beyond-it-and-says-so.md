## Probe

PLANT TWO AND COUNT. Give a cheaper hand a set of work tokens in which two
are deliberately marked below their real difficulty.

COUNT WHAT IT DOES WITH THEM. Raising the mark is the pass. Answering is the
failure, and answering confidently is the failure this entry is written
against.

IT NEEDS THE ROUTING BUILT, so this is a before-and-after probe rather than
one that can run today.

## The shape of the problem

THE MARK IS THE FIRST DEFENCE AND IT IS AUTHORED IN ADVANCE. Somebody writing
a method card decides how hard each step is, before knowing which hand will
meet it.

WHERE THAT MARK IS WRONG, THE ONLY THING LEFT IS THE HAND'S OWN JUDGMENT, and
the whole reason the hand is cheap is that its judgment is weaker.

## Why it is graded plausible

ONE ORDINARY EVENT PRODUCES IT: a step marked low because it reads simple,
which turns out to need a call nobody wrote down. That is the ordinary case
for a card written by somebody who already knew the answer.

MEASURED, ON THE SHAPE THIS REPLACES. A cheaper hand filling a long form
incorrectly is the failure the actual reports, and nothing objected then
either.

## What falsifies it

A CHEAPER HAND THAT ANSWERS a deliberately under-marked work token rather
than raising it. One run settles it.

## What is NOT assumed here

NOTHING SAYS THE MARK IS RIGHT. That is the other half and it belongs with
whoever authors the cards. This entry is only about what happens when it is
wrong.
