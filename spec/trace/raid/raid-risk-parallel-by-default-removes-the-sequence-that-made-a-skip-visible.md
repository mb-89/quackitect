## The ruling this sits under, stated fairly

THE OWNER RULED THAT NUMBERING IS NOT A TECHNICAL DEPENDENCY. Where a step
genuinely needs another finished first, that edge is written. Where it does not,
the steps may run in parallel, and deriving an edge from document position would
serialise work with no reason to be serial.

THAT REASONING IS SOUND AND THIS ENTRY DOES NOT CONTEST IT. What it records is
the cost of applying it to cards written before the rule existed.

## OWNER RULING — the same mechanism as the machines, and the second-order fear is answered

USE THE RULE THE STATE MACHINES ALREADY USE. Things that do not depend on each
other are parallel. Things that do are in sequence. There is no third rule to
invent for tokens.

AND THE ORDER LOSS MATTERS LESS THAN THE ATTACK CLAIMED. Even where tokens are
parallel, one hand still works them one after another. Parallel is a statement
that nothing forbids another order, not an instruction to interleave.

SO THE SKIP-VISIBILITY ARGUMENT LARGELY FALLS. What makes a skip visible is an
open token holding its state, which is the mechanism, rather than a reader
noticing an unexpected sequence.

WHAT SURVIVES IS ONE NARROW THING, kept because it costs a judgment at
conversion and nothing afterwards.

## The narrow risk

CONVERSION IS WHERE IT BITES. Every numbered card was written when numbering was
the only way to say order. At conversion each one becomes a set of tokens with no
edges, and the author's intent is gone with no record that it was ever there.

NOBODY IS NAMED TO RESTATE IT. The rule puts the burden on whoever writes an
edge, and at conversion time nobody is writing edges.

## The second-order effect, and it is why this is not merely tidiness

AN EXPECTED SEQUENCE IS WHAT MAKES A SKIP VISIBLE. Step nine arriving before
step two is noticeable. With no expected order, a missing step and a step taken
early look the same to whoever is reading.

## What closes it

A PER-CARD RULING AT CONVERSION, recorded on the card. Either the numbering
carried intent and becomes edges, or it did not and the card says so.

THE CHEAPEST FIRST MOVE is to count the numbered marked headings across the
existing cards, so the size of the judgment is known before it is made.
