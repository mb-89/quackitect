## Rejected options

SPLIT THE BOOTSTRAP INSTEAD, by whether the act stands inside the tree it
touches. [[opt-split-the-bootstrap-by-whether-the-tree-is-the-one-you-stand-in]].
What the runner-up takes, and it buys the open path its own clock. Rejected at
the graft state as incompatible: the merge IS this line's argument on drift, and
splitting to buy a clock trades a guarantee for two points on the lowest-graded
axis here.

KEEP BOTH CLUSTERS AS THEY STAND. Rejected because the two paths have already
drifted, and nothing forces a fix to one to reach the other.

## Consequences

SEVEN FUNCTIONS AND A CLUSTER BOUNDARY DISAPPEAR, and the two paths stop being
able to drift apart. That is the whole purchase.

THE ARRIVAL'S SECOND GUARANTEE IS AT RISK, and this is the most dangerous
consequence on the chart. That guarantee is two-sided and loud: either the agent
stands on a caged lane, or it is told plainly what failed. The second half exists
BECAUSE nobody is watching, and folding it into a path built for a present person
is exactly where it would quietly go.

AN AGENT BELIEVING IT IS CAGED WHEN IT IS NOT is the failure this risks, and it
is silent. That is worse than anything else this decision touches.

A MACHINE WITH NO EDITOR NEEDS A WAY IN THAT THE OPEN ROW DOES NOT DESCRIBE. The
winner takes a host pattern test as its way in, and the merged path serves hosts
that have no such feature. That gap is real and unfilled.

## What this decision owes before it is built

A SPIKE, NAMED AT M6 RATHER THAN HOPED FOR. Whether the two-sided guarantee
survives being folded into a path built for a present person cannot be settled by
reading, and both the winner and the third candidate depend on it.

### The prior-art back-check

NO ANCESTOR TO STUDY. This is a partition of our own functions rather than a
re-derivation of anybody's design, and no comparable product publishes its
internal cluster boundaries. Saying so is the honest answer rather than reaching
for a loose analogy.
