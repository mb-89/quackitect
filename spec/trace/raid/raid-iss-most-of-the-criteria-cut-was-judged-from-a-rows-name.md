## What was measured

THE SAMPLE WAS ADVERSARIAL, NOT RANDOM. The gate picked the ten cuts most likely
to be wrong — the rows a candidate that moves records into branches would touch.
Two of those ten failed. That rate cannot be extrapolated to the seventy-nine
nobody read, in either direction.

BOTH FAILURES SHARED A SHAPE. The row's NAME suggested a subject no candidate
touches. The row's STATEMENT said something narrower and live.

- `req-setup-stops-before-partial` reads as install discipline. Its statement
  stops the script when a tool it NEEDS is missing, and one candidate adds a
  required tool.
- `req-close-leaves-trunk-clean` reads as record closing. Its statement demands
  zero uncommitted changes on the trunk, and one candidate exists to hold engine
  writes to four named mutations.

## Why the cut is cheap and the check is not

STRIKING A ROW COSTS ONE LINE. Verifying it costs reading a statement and
holding it against three candidate configurations.

SO THE ECONOMICS RUN THE WRONG WAY, and the state's own guidance names the
failure it produces: "What is NOT a legal reason to cut: it looks like they are
all about the same." A name-based judgment is that guess wearing a reason's
clothes.

## What would have prevented it

READING EVERY STATEMENT. Ninety-nine rows is one script and one pass, not a
hundred calls, and the statements are one frontmatter key.

A CHEAPER GUARD WOULD ALSO HAVE CAUGHT BOTH. Any cut whose reason does not quote
the row's own statement is unverified by construction, and could be marked as
such without anybody judging it.

## What it does not undermine

THE FRONT DID NOT MOVE. The restored axis was scored and domination recomputed
over all twelve. Nothing is eliminated, and the candidate the two errors would
have affected holds a strict advantage elsewhere that keeps it non-dominated
whatever happens on those rows.

## What the spike found, 2026-08-19

NINETY-NINE OF NINETY-NINE STATEMENTS WERE READ. Not sampled. The statements
were pulled as one frontmatter key across the whole requirement folder, joined
to each row's number and its quoted cut reason, then judged against what the
three designs commit to.

FIVE MORE ROWS WERE STRUCK WRONGLY, on top of the two the gate already found.

- `req-purpose-recorded-at-begin`. The third line's own text says a clone
  "loses the purpose the scaffold used to record". The cut called it untouched.
- `req-scaffold-from-template`. Two designs scaffold from the product template.
  The third has no scaffold at all, only a clone.
- `req-extension-replaced-reported`. The statement names the install script.
  Two designs have one and must replace and report; the third deletes it.
- `req-begin-says-own-window`. Two designs have a completion moment where the
  engine speaks. The third has no engine-run begin.
- `req-method-reuse-is-vendoring`. Weakest of the five, and named as weaker.

FOUR MORE ARE UNSURE AND EACH SAYS WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT.
`req-setup-serves-shipped-method`, `req-acts-carry-role-and-channel`,
`req-ideation-opens-no-record`, `req-second-product-reuses-install`.

## The reason itself was pasted across a block

THREE ROWS CARRY "[cut: vehicles, untouched]" AND NONE OF THE THREE IS ABOUT A
VEHICLE. Three genuine vehicle rows sit nearby with the same reason.

THAT IS A DIFFERENT DEFECT FROM JUDGING BY NAME, and it is worse. A name-based
judgment at least looked at the row. A reason copied down a block did not.

## What it does NOT change

ALL FIVE SEPARATE THE THIRD LINE ONLY. The two leading designs both keep the
installer and the scaffold, so they tie on every one of the five.

SO THE SEAT CANNOT MOVE ON THESE ROWS, and the finding makes the larger problem
worse rather than better: the surviving axes separated the two leaders on two of
twelve, and restoring five more that they tie on takes it to two of seventeen.
