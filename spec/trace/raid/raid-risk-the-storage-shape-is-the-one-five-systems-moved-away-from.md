## The evidence on the other side

FIVE SYSTEMS, OVER EIGHTEEN YEARS, IN FOUR LANGUAGES converged on an
append-only log with state derived from it.

ONE MATURE PROJECT REJECTED FILES IN THE TREE BY DESIGN and published its
reasoning. Another used the approach for fifteen years and abandoned it.

THE CLOSEST ANALOGUE BUILT FOR AGENTS moved to a version-controlled database
with cell-level merging, and tells agents not to keep work in markdown lists.

That is not a majority to outvote lightly.

## The argument on our side

THE READERS HERE ARE THE COMMITTERS. There is no separate audience reading a
rendered view of somebody else's log.

ONE ENGINE WALKS ONE RECORD, and a second machine wanting the same record is a
second checkout. So the concurrent-write pressure that drove the others is
weaker here than it was for them.

A PERSON CAN OPEN THE WORK WITHOUT A TOOL. That is the whole purchase, and it
is the property the log shape gives up.

## Why it is graded rather than closed

IT IS A TRADEOFF, NOT A WIN. Readability and direct editing against mechanical
mergeability. The weight of the field sits on the other side, so the argument
has to be made out loud rather than assumed.

## What closes it

THE OWNER RULING ON THE STORAGE SHAPE, with the counter-evidence above cited in
the same breath. Until then this entry is the dissent, kept where a gate reads
it.
