## Closed the same day it was raised, 2026-08-19

THE OWNER OVERRULED THE REASONING AND WAS RIGHT. This entry argued that adding a
row nobody has answers for would change nothing. Their correction: nobody has
answers because nobody was asked, so asking might produce a differentiator.

WHAT HAPPENED WHEN IT WAS ASKED.

- Three option nodes answering the question already stood in the corpus, from i1
  and i28, carrying no chart row. The cells existed; the row did not.
- Each candidate took a DIFFERENT one, each following from its own logic.
- A fresh second hand re-scored the three install axes. Nine cells moved.
- The axes with no candidate at solid baseline went from FOUR to ONE.
- One flat axis stopped being flat.

SO IT WAS A DIFFERENTIATOR, and this entry's own argument for leaving it was
wrong. The front did not move, which is the one thing the argument got right.

A FOURTH CELL WAS ADDED AFTERWARDS from a live prior-art scan and nobody picks
it: [[opt-one-binary-published-through-every-channel]]. It is recorded as an
unvisited cell rather than retro-fitted to a line.

## How it was found

BY THE ARITHMETIC RATHER THAN BY ANYBODY'S OPINION. The utopia point is the best
value ANY candidate reaches on each axis. It is supposed to be unreachable in
combination. It is not supposed to sit below solid baseline on its own.

FOUR AXES DO. A fresh machine running tops out at 2. The editor-and-shell setup
floor tops out at 1. A surface answering in one second tops out at 2. Stopping
before a partial install tops out at 2.

TWO INDEPENDENT SCORERS SAID THE SAME THING WITHOUT BEING ASKED. Both wrote that
no candidate addresses the setup floor, and one added that the requirement hangs
off `stand-up-a-product`, a function none of the three candidates writes about.

## Why it is one hole rather than four

THREE OF THE FOUR ARE THE SAME SUBJECT. Installing on a fresh machine, the floor
that install assumes, and stopping when a tool it needs is missing. They are
three clauses of one act.

THE CHART HAS EIGHT ROWS AND NONE OF THEM IS THAT ACT. The rows cover the root,
the open, where the machine state lives, what a copy withholds, corpus agreement,
the way in, the cluster cut and the drawn surface. Standing a product up on a
computer with nothing on it appears nowhere.

## Why no finder produced one

THE DELTA WAS FRAMED AS A MOVE, NOT AN INSTALL. Every finder searched around
where the machine-state folder sits and how a folder becomes a project. Install
was upstream of all of it and nobody's search reached back that far.

ONE OPTION NODE ALREADY RECORDED THE ABSENCE and it was not read as this. The
prior-art sweep behind `opt-no-installer-clone-the-template` found no account of
anybody doing this, calling it "the one part with no environment equivalent to
lean on".

## What closing it would take

A ROW ON THE CHART, with cells, reached by more than one finder. That is
enumerate-space's work rather than a gate's, and doing it inside this iteration
is a scope decision for the owner rather than a repair the agent may make.

WHAT IT IS NOT is a reason to withhold the front. Nothing on the chart is
dominated, and adding a row nobody has cells for would not change that.
