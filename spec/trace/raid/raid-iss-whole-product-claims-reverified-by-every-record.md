## What was observed

i27's verification serves ten non-test specs. This record's delta is the
resolution seam, the engine delta, the supervisor, the three process modules
and the form checks.

Of the ten, ONE is about anything this record built:
`tsp-read-back-inspection`, minted here, about the shape of the resolution
tests.

The other nine are the product's standing claims: the desk and its gates, the
first run on a fresh machine, the panel walkthrough, the tour, the prose laws,
the record provenance, the derivation analysis, the autonomy tiers and the
bound surface.

## Why marking them owed is not the answer

The owed mechanism exists for what a run genuinely cannot verify, and it
demands a register entry so the debt has an owner.

USED FOR NINE CLAIMS AT A TIME IT STOPS BEING A DEBT AND BECOMES THE NORMAL
CASE. A list where almost everything is owed reads the same as a list where
nothing is, and the reader skims both.

## Why ticking them is worse

A claim is an OBSERVATION. Ticking a whole-product demonstration nobody watched
is the fabrication the method forbids in as many words, and it is undetectable
afterwards because a ticked box looks identical whoever ticked it.

## The shape of the fix, not the fix

Named here rather than decided, because it is the owner's.

- A record's verification serves the specs its DELTA touches, and the standing
  ones are re-observed on a schedule or at a release rather than per record.
- Or the standing set gets a separate, less frequent verification of its own,
  and the per-record state stops carrying it.

Either way the question is WHICH CLAIMS THIS RECORD PUT AT RISK, and nothing
computes that today.

## Where it was recorded before

The first cloud run's field report reached the same conclusion from the other
end, item 10: an iteration about a keyword search inherited eight whole-product
claims including one about the editor panel.

TWO RECORDS HAVE NOW HIT IT INDEPENDENTLY, which is what moved it from a
report's suggestion to a register entry.
