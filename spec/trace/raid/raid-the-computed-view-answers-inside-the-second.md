## Not established

NOBODY HAS TIMED IT. The score of 2 on the one-second axis came from reading
the sentence "all computed once, server side" and reasoning that every look is
therefore a round trip. That is an argument, and an argument is how the
assumption got made rather than how it gets checked.

## Not controlled

THE BUDGET IS A PERSON'S PATIENCE, and the machine it runs on is a mid-level
laptop the register already names as the reference. What the resolution costs
depends on how much the view model has to carry, which is not yet decided.

## Why it is a tripwire rather than a worry

THE ENGINE'S OWN PERTURBATION HUNT PUTS THE RIVAL'S DEFICIT AT ZERO, with two
single-point swings available. This is the more plausible of the two. If it
fires, the winner was wrong, and the honest response is to say so rather than
defend the seat.

## Probe

TIME A COMPUTED VIEW AGAINST A REAL WALK.

- Resolve one state's whole view model, including greyness and its reasons.
- Deliver it to a surface and render.
- Record the round trip on the reference machine, over a real record rather
  than a fixture.

THE PROBE PASSES at or under 1000 ms for every surface request. It needs the
view model to exist, so it belongs to the spike milestone rather than to a
script.

## What happens if it holds

THE GRAFT KEEPS ITS COST AND LOSES ITS OBJECTION. Nothing about the design
changes; the record simply stops carrying an unmeasured number as if it
decided something.

## What happens if it fails

THE WINNER WAS WRONG. The repeater's latency score would move the count to two
against one and the seat with it. The register should carry that as an issue
rather than as a footnote on the ADR.
