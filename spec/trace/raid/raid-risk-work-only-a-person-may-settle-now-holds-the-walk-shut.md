## The hinge

TWO RULES MEET AND BOTH ARE RIGHT.

A STATE IS NOT LEFT WHILE IT HOLDS AN OPEN TOKEN. That is what stops a step
nobody did being finished by walking past it.

AN AGENT MAY NOT SETTLE A PERSON-ONLY ITEM. That is what makes the mark mean
anything at all.

TOGETHER THEY PARK THE WALK, on purpose, until a person acts.

## What changed, and what did not

LEAVING USED TO SETTLE EVERYTHING OPEN and skip the person-only ones, so such
an item never held anything — it sat there while the walk went by, and the
drawing showed a count nobody was going to clear.

NOW IT HOLDS. That is the honest behaviour and it is what the hold is for.

## Why it is graded plausible rather than certain

NOBODY HAS PLACED ONE YET on a state an unattended run must cross. The
mechanism exists and the collision has not happened.

## What is already done about it

THE REFUSAL NAMES THE PERSON RATHER THAN A CALL THE AGENT IS REFUSED. Offering
`se_work {act: "settle"}` there would hand back a call the store rejects on
sight, and the walk would loop on a remedy it cannot follow.

SO THE WALK CAN END THE TURN HONESTLY. Waiting on somebody's word is a
sanctioned stop, and the refusal says which item and whose it is.

## What closes it

A DECISION ON WHAT AN UNATTENDED RUN DOES with a person-only item it cannot
pass. Three answers are available and none is obviously right.

- WAIT, and the run spends itself at that state.
- SKIP IT, which gives back the whole meaning of the mark.
- REFUSE TO PLACE ONE where an unattended run will cross it, which is a check
  at the placing rather than at the walking.
