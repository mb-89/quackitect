A WRONG COUNT DRAWN BEAUTIFULLY IS WORSE THAN A RIGHT COUNT DRAWN PLAINLY. That
is why the figure crosses rather than being invented on the far side.

WHAT IT CARRIES. Two numbers per position, one per slot. What must still be
taken in, and what must still be produced.

WHAT THE SURFACE NEVER DOES: compute it. Only one of the two ends can then be
wrong, and this crossing makes that end the offer.

FAILURE BEHAVIOUR: a count that cannot be produced is shown as absent, never as
zero. A zero and an unknown look identical on a surface and mean opposite
things.
