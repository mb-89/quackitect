## Mechanism

THE STANDING PAIR IS HALF A PAIR. `req-every-call-records-the-model-that-answered-it`
puts the actual driver in the record. Nothing puts the named one there. So the
record answers "who did this" and cannot answer "was that the hand we asked for".

WHY RECONSTRUCTION DOES NOT SUBSTITUTE. In principle the named driver is
derivable: read the state, read its row, read the difficulty, read the mapping.
In practice all four move — the matrix is edited, the mapping is edited, rows are
added — so a derivation run next month answers with today's policy applied to
last month's call, and reports a gap that never happened. The named driver is a
fact about the moment of the call, and a fact about a moment has to be written
down at that moment or it is gone. This is the same argument
`req-the-complexity-value-is-read-live-and-never-pinned` makes in the other
direction, and the two do not conflict: read the policy live at decision time,
then pin what the policy said about THIS call.

WHAT IT UNBLOCKS. Both audit options need this and neither says so:
`opt-name-the-acceptable-over-driving-rate-in-advance` cannot compute a rate
without both numbers, and `opt-audit-a-sample-rather-than-reconcile-everything`
samples a comparison that does not exist yet. `req-a-weaker-driver-than-named-owes-a-recorded-reason`
is worse off still — it obliges a reason for a divergence the record cannot show,
so the obligation is unenforceable as it stands. Under this option the
divergence is visible and the missing reason is a detectable omission.

WHAT IT COSTS: two fields where there was one, and a decision about what to write
when the machine named nothing — the unmatched case. Writing the literal
no-match value keeps the field total, which is the cheaper of the two answers.
