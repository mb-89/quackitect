## Mechanism

TWO ATTRIBUTES THAT DO NOT VARY TOGETHER TODAY. What is withheld is one rule.
Which act is copying is anything from three.

MAKE THE FIRST DEPEND ON THE SECOND. Each producing act declares what it takes,
and the acts genuinely want different answers.

- A PACKAGED RELEASE takes the product and none of the machine state.
- A VENDORED ENGINE takes the method and the engine, and none of the walk that
  produced them.
- A PRODUCED PROJECT takes a scaffold, which is neither.

## It generalises something the standing option already admits

[[opt-exclude-at-the-packaging-boundary-and-prove-it]] SAYS TWO PATHS NEED IT
SEPARATELY, because a packaged copy and a vendored copy are produced by
different code and fixing one proves nothing about the other.

THAT IS THIS DEPENDENCY, NAMED AND NOT ACTED ON. The standing option treats the
difference as a testing burden. This one treats it as the rule's own shape.

## What it buys

THE TEST BECOMES ONE TEST PER ACT AND MEANS SOMETHING. Today a shared rule with
three consumers can be right for one and wrong for another, and nothing says so.

IT ALSO STOPS THE RULE GROWING BY UNION. A single list serving three acts ends
up holding everything any of them wanted excluded, which withholds from the acts
that had no reason to withhold it.

## What it costs

THREE DECLARATIONS DRIFT WHERE ONE CANNOT. This is the exact failure
[[opt-one-declaration-of-what-the-machine-state-folder-is]] exists to prevent,
and the two options pull against each other.

THEY CAN BE TAKEN TOGETHER, and that is probably the interesting reading. One
declaration says WHAT the machine-state folder is. A per-act table says WHO
withholds it. The first stays single and the second varies.

A NEW ACT ARRIVES WITH NO ENTRY. Under one shared rule a new producing act
inherits the safe answer. Under this one it inherits nothing, and forgetting to
declare is the same failure the standing option already calls the default
flipping from safe to unsafe.
