## Mechanism

WHAT IF THE ARRIVAL CLUSTER DOES NOT EXIST? Seven functions go: accounting for
the arrival, judging the runtime, supplying dependencies, placing the cage,
raising the lane, resolving the cited refs, handing over the means to call.

WHO DOES ITS JOB INSTEAD? The bootstrap cluster, which already does the same
work for a person at a keyboard. Both end at one place — a lane that answers —
and this iteration made that literal: bringing the product up now produces the
live lane, and the arrival's own hand-over function consumes it.

WHY THEY WERE EVER SEPARATE. One has a person present and the other does not.
That is a difference in what may be ASSUMED, not a difference in what is DONE.
A person supplies consent by opening a folder, notices a failure, and can
install something. Nobody supplies any of that on an unattended box.

WHAT SURVIVES THE TRIM AS AN INPUT RATHER THAN A CLUSTER. Whether a person is
present decides three things: whether a failure may stop and wait, whether
anything may be installed unasked, and whether the walk may reach a gate and
hold. Each is a parameter of one act rather than a reason for two.

WHAT IT COSTS. The arrival's own guarantee is two-sided and loud — either the
agent stands on a caged lane or it is told plainly what failed. Folding that
into a path built for a present person risks losing the second half, because a
person is the fallback that path assumes.

WHAT IT BUYS. Seven functions and one cluster boundary disappear, and the two
paths stop being able to drift apart. Today a fix to one is not a fix to the
other, and nothing says so.

THE HONEST WARNING. This is the biggest cluster in the delta's reach, which is
exactly what the method says to trim rather than the cheap ones. It is also
the one whose failure mode is an agent believing it is caged when it is not.

## A catalogue rule argues against this option

THE HEURISTICS FINDER HELD "small interfaces between big parts beat the
reverse" AGAINST THIS PAIR, and the rule says keep them apart.

TWO BIG PARTS MEET OVER TWO FLOWS. Seven functions on one side and six on the
other, joined by `flow-toolchain` and `flow-live-lane`. That is already the
shape the rule wants, and merging destroys it: thirteen functions with no
declared interface between the halves.

THE COUNTER-ARGUMENT IS STILL THE DRIFT. A small interface only earns its keep
while both sides are maintained. These two have drifted precisely because
nothing forces a fix on one to reach the other.

SO THE RULE IDENTIFIES THE PRICE RATHER THAN SETTLING THE QUESTION. Whoever
decides this pays either drift or a lost boundary, and should say which.
