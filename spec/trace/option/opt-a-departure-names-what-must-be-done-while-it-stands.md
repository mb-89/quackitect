## Mechanism

An aviation departure comes in two halves and both are conditions of dispatch.

- AN M-ITEM is a maintenance action taken BEFORE the flight departs, typically
  deactivating, securing or collaring the inoperative thing.
- AN O-ITEM is a procedure the crew performs before or during the flight,
  written on the entry's own page.

So the exception is never bare. It says what must happen instead, and the
flight is legal only once that has happened.

WHAT SURVIVES THE TRANSLATION, AND IT IS THE WHOLE POINT. Every mechanism the
external sweep found treats an exception as a subtraction — the rule stops
applying here. This one treats it as a SUBSTITUTION — the rule stops applying
here and something else applies instead.

A module that reaches the filesystem directly is the inoperative item. Its
M-item might be that it must route through the path jail even while it skips
the door. Its O-item might be that it must log what it reached.

WHAT THIS FIXES THAT AN EXPIRY DOES NOT. An expiry says the exception should
end. A compensating action says what protects us until it does, which is the
question a reader of the departure list actually has.

WHAT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT TRANSFER. The regulator. Aviation's substitutions
are approved by an authority before anybody may use them, and there is no
authority here. Ours would be authored by whoever writes the departure, which
is the same weakness the reason field already has.

WHAT IT COSTS. Two fields instead of one, and a compensating action nobody
checks is worse than none because it reads as protection.
