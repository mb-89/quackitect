## Mechanism

The rule accepts two kinds of departure and refuses only the third.

- A SETTLED reason, or a citation to a decision that carries one.
- AN OPEN QUESTION, stated as one — this departs and the argument has not been
  made yet.
- SILENCE, which is refused.

WHY IT DISSOLVES RATHER THAN TRADES. The contradiction assumed one demand for
both observers. The AUTHOR at write time needs a way to be honest in ten
seconds; the READER later needs something that resolves. An open question
serves both, and a fabricated reason serves neither.

THE COUNT IS THE PART THAT MAKES IT WORK. Open questions are reported as a
number. A list where most departures are open is visibly a list nobody has
argued, which a list of plausible sentences never is.

WHAT IT COSTS. It legalises the easy answer, so the list can fill with open
questions and stall there. The count is the only pressure against that, and a
count nobody looks at is no pressure at all.

THIS SYSTEM ALREADY DOES IT ONCE, ELSEWHERE. The mint guard's own rule says
that where a statement cannot be written cleanly yet, saying so is a legal
answer and the token carries the open question. What is refused there is
silence and paste, never honesty, and this is the same ruling applied to a
departure.
