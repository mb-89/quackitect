## Mechanism

THE TRIM. The record's life is the biggest cluster on the chart: routing,
holding, landing, closing, the archive and the test verdict, six functions
and roughly a quarter of the register. Ask the question of THAT one, because
the card says trim what is expensive.

WHO DOES ITS JOB. Git already does most of it.

- Opening is `git branch`.
- Holding is the branch existing.
- Landing is the merge, which git does whole or not at all already.
- Closing is deleting the branch.
- The archive is the history, which is immutable by construction rather than
  by a read-only rule somebody enforces.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY LOST, and this is the honest half. Three things have no git
equivalent.

- The RULING on findings at close. Git deletes a branch without asking what
  it produced.
- The RECOMMENDATION at routing. Git has no opinion about which vehicle holds
  which work.
- The refusal to land red. A merge does not consult a test verdict.

SO THE TRIM IS PARTIAL, and that is the finding. Four of the six functions
are re-implementations of something the environment already provides, and two
are genuinely ours. A candidate built this way would be much smaller and
would have to say where the ruling and the recommendation live.

THE QUESTION IT FORCES. If four of six are git under another name, is the
cluster a cluster at all, or is it an adapter?
