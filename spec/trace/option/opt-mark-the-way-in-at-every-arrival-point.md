## Mechanism

THE ABSTRACT PROBLEM. Somebody who has never been here, is not reading
carefully, and has somewhere to be, must find one specific way through.

HOW THE DOMAIN SOLVES IT, and every part of it is deliberate.

- ONE STANDARD FORM, learned somewhere else. Nobody reads an exit sign; they
  recognise it. The recognition was paid for by every other building.
- REPEATED AT EVERY DECISION POINT, not once at the entrance. A marker you
  passed is a marker you cannot use.
- PLACED WHERE THE EYE ALREADY GOES, rather than where it is tidy.
- IT WORKS WITHOUT INSTRUCTION. Nothing has to be read first for the marker
  to mean what it means.

WHAT TRANSFERS HERE. A person can arrive at this product in at least three
places: the code-hosting page, an editor opened on the folder, and a terminal
in it. The way in should be marked in all three, in one recognisable form,
rather than one readme at the root doing the work for all of them.

THE PART THAT ARGUES WITH OUR CURRENT SHAPE. A conventionally-named launcher
at the top of the folder is the single-entrance design. It marks one arrival
point, and it relies on the reader already knowing the convention — which its
own documentation admits.

WHAT BREAKS IN TRANSLATION. Exit signs have a legally mandated form, and we
have no such standard to borrow. The nearest equivalent is whatever each host
already scans for, which means the SAME meaning in three different forms
rather than one form in three places.

WHAT IT COSTS. Three surfaces to keep in step, and drift between them is
worse than a single missing marker, because a wrong sign sends somebody the
wrong way with confidence.
