## Why it stands

THE OWNER'S RULE, STATED EXACTLY: where the engine and the caller disagree about how much to run, the smaller of the two wins, in both directions. It is symmetric, and it corrects an earlier one-way ceiling that must not be built. The engine cannot express it today. The verb takes a question and nothing else, so a caller has no channel to name a smaller set, and the single rule that decides scope is a threshold that promotes everything to the whole battery once crossed. The second half — measuring how often that promotion fires — can be answered from the call log before anything is rebuilt.

## The owner added a second half, 2026-08-28

THE SCOPE CALCULATION ITSELF IS WRONG, and that is a separate defect from the
missing channel. The owner's words: it seems wrong.

MEASURED THE SAME DAY. A scoped run over an edit to three engine files
reported 593 changed files with no test that answers for them, and picked four
test files. A calculation that cannot map most of a diff is not choosing a
scope, it is giving up and saying so in a long list.

SO THE RULE AND THE CALCULATION SHIP TOGETHER. A minimum of two numbers is
worthless when one of them is guessed.
