## Why it stands

Six audience nodes stand and four of them carry no interest in the outcome, which is what an audience node is for. Nothing has been decided and nothing removed. THE COST OF ACTING IS LARGER THAN IT LOOKS: the four are named 120 times across 66 files, including roughly twenty use cases and twenty stories that name one as their actor, plus a check that references them. So deleting them strands an actor field in dozens of places. A register entry already stands about one of the four having no use case of its own.

## When it comes back

ready when the owner rules whether an agent is an audience for the product or a hand that operates it — and the ruling arrives with a migration for the forty-odd use cases and stories naming one of the four as their actor
