## Why it stands

One look-back step reads a ledger of questions the tooling could not answer. That ledger is empty, because across a whole round nobody put a question to it.

THE SIZE OF THE SILENCE. Nearly nineteen hundred recorded calls, spanning a round taken from its first checkpoint through to a shipped archive. The querying verb shows up in no bucket of the per-tool tally.

WHY THE STEP LEANS ON IT. Our method describes that ledger as the pre-sorted, no-cost portion of the mining work, and treats each unanswered query as a gap in the toolkit announcing itself in a person's own phrasing. Nothing queried means nothing sorted.

THE METHOD ALREADY CONCEDES ONE LIMIT: the ledger sees only what somebody remembered to try. It stops short of the harder case, where nobody tries anything.

THE RESULT IS INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM SUCCESS. A neglected verb and a toolkit with no gaps yield the very same output, separated by one number nobody prints. An earlier step in the same method warns against precisely this trap; this step does not.

TWO REPAIRS, UNRELATED TO EACH OTHER.

- Show how many times the verb was invoked, right beside its ledger.
- Put the verb where somebody would meet it. Nothing this walk read named it, and an unknown tool goes unused.
