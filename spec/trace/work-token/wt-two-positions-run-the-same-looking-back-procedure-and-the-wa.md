## Why it stands

Two positions run the same looking-back procedure, and the walker cannot tell which one it stands in. Every record carries one near its beginning; the top-level machine carries another on its own. Both serve an identical list of fifteen steps, so arriving at the beginning one after the work is finished hands over closing questions — check the agreement, sweep the ledger, walk the pile — with nothing marking them as belonging elsewhere. The collision has teeth because both share a single evidence sheet. A record's opening sheet was signed hours earlier against an empty inbox and a small window. Answering the closing version would overwrite every one of those fields, destroying the opening account. No refusal came, no warning came, and three steps were complete before the position was noticed. Either branch the procedure on which position serves it, or give each position its own sheet.
