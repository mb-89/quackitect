## Why it stands

A register entry of the kind that records borrowed time carries two header fields for a single fact, and they contradict each other on almost every one.

WHAT WAS COUNTED. Fourteen of fifteen standing entries named one date under one field and a date two days earlier under another. The fifteenth, created during this run, carried neither.

SO THE PAGE ANSWERS TWICE. A reader wanting the date of the most recent revisit is handed two of them, with nothing there to say which to trust.

A THIRD FIELD SITS BESIDE THEM holding the outcome of that revisit. It can only be true of one of the two dates, and it does not say which.

THE ARGUMENT IS OUR OWN. Borrowed time nobody revisits is a falsehood in the books. Borrowed time whose revisit is dated twice, differently, is that same falsehood carrying a stamp.

THE REPAIR IS A REMOVAL, not a reconciliation. Choose one field, strip the other from every entry, and add a check that turns away any entry carrying both.

WHICH SURVIVES IS STILL OPEN. One held the later date everywhere, hinting that it is the living one. Nobody followed it back to whatever writes it.
