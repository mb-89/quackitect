## What was counted

EVERY MARKED HEADING OVER EVERY METHOD CARD, taking a heading as one piece of
work — the rule as the design assumed it.

| figure | value |
| --- | --- |
| cards | 137 |
| marked headings in total | 424 |
| median per card | 2 |
| largest per card | 23 |
| cards over twenty | 1 |

## The assumption mostly holds

THE MEDIAN POSITION OWES TWO THINGS. A count read at a glance is a fair
assumption for almost every card in the tree, which is what the surface was
designed against.

## One outlier decides the design

`meth-derive-criteria` CARRIES 23. Two more sit in the teens: the gate review at
15 and requirement authoring at 14.

SO THE SURFACE MUST DRAW 23 WITHOUT BECOMING UNREADABLE, while drawing 2 almost
every time.

THAT IS A DIFFERENT PROBLEM FROM THE ONE THE ASSUMPTION IMAGINED. Not "is the
number small" but "does the rare large one degrade gracefully". A surface built
only for the median is a surface that breaks on the card people spend longest
in.

## What this does not measure

WHETHER A PERSON READS THE NUMBER FASTER THAN SOME BAR. The row it serves asks
for a two-second read, and that is a watched attempt rather than a count.

THIS EXPERIMENT MEASURES THE NUMBER. What a person does with it belongs to the
surface's own demonstration.

## A caveat the heading rule creates

THESE COUNTS ASSUME A HEADING IS A STEP, and a sibling spike found that rule too
narrow. Once cards carry an explicit mark, the counts will move: the gate review
falls from 15 to about 2, and the retro rises from 1 to 12.

THE SHAPE OF THE ANSWER SURVIVES THAT. A median in single figures with a rare
outlier in the twenties is what the surface has to handle either way.
