## Why this one

It is the only curve that helps the case the whole packet keeps returning to:
a piece of work with nothing to count.

A shell command cannot be estimated, which was observed rather than argued. An
honest "cannot estimate" leaves that caller exactly where it started, and this
is the only option on the chart that gives it something to act on anyway.

## How it works

The same handback and the same standing word as the measured answer, and TWO
cells different from it rather than one.

IT TAKES THE BARE LINEAR ESTIMATE where the measured answer takes the figure
and its basis as a pair. A duration here does not carry what it was computed
from.

AND IT TAKES THE NEXT-ASK TIME where the measured answer takes the uniform
listing. Every entry says when it is worth asking again, whether or not it
carries a duration.

THIS PROSE ONCE READ "the measured answer, with one field added", and it was
wrong on both counts. Corrected at i51's evaluate-set, where the scoring agent
found the picks and the prose disagreeing. The picks were and remain
authoritative, and they are what was scored.

For a job with a countable unit the next-ask time follows the estimate. For one
without, it follows how fast the entry itself is changing.

## The seams

THE NEXT-ASK TIME MEETS THE ESTIMATE, AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME QUANTITY.
That is the whole seam, and getting it wrong collapses this candidate into the
measured answer with a redundant field.

THE ESTIMATE IS ABOUT THE WORK. It answers when the work will end.

THE NEXT-ASK TIME IS ABOUT THE ANSWER. It answers when this entry will have
something new to say.

THEY COME APART IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, and each direction is a case the rule must
handle.

| the work | the estimate says | the next-ask time should say | why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| forty seconds from finishing | forty seconds | about forty seconds | they agree, and this is the easy case |
| two hours out, estimated once from little data | two hours | a few minutes | the estimate is what needs refreshing, not the work |
| nothing to count at all | cannot estimate | a short interval that lengthens | the entry has no prediction and still changes |
| finished since the last ask | nothing | never again | a settled entry needs no return |

THE THIRD ROW IS THIS CANDIDATE'S REASON TO EXIST. A shell command cannot be
estimated, and an honest refusal to estimate leaves the caller exactly where it
started. This is the only cell on the chart that answers it.

WITH THE LISTING. The next-ask time is per entry, not per call. A caller with
three jobs running takes the smallest of the three and asks then.

SO THE LISTING MAY CARRY ONE SUMMARY next-ask time beside the entries, and that
is a convenience rather than a second source. Computing it is a minimum over
what the entries already say.

WITH THE HANDBACK. Nothing crosses, and it inherits that seam unchanged.

## What it costs

A RULE FOR COMPUTING THE NEXT-ASK TIME that is not simply the duration
rounded. A next-ask time that is always the duration has added a field and no
information, and getting that rule wrong is easy.

AND IT CAN LEAVE WORK FINISHED AND UNNOTICED. A next-ask time that is too long
means the caller sits idle after the work landed. Nothing in this candidate
catches that.

## What it leans on

Everything the measured answer leans on, plus an unbuilt rule with no
measurement behind it.

THE NEXT-ASK TIME IS THE ONLY UNPROBED CELL ON THIS CURVE. Every other option
it picks was either measured on this machine or is a house rule already
applied elsewhere.
