## current_situation

## goal_system

TWO STANDING GOALS ARE PULLED AGAINST EACH OTHER BY THIS DELTA, and both conflicts have a ruling.

### Speed against rigor

THE PULL. Every check the walk runs costs time on the request path. The cheapest way to make a walk fast is to check less, and this round is chartered to make the walk fast.

THE RULING. THE WALK GETS FAST BY NOT REDOING WHAT IT ALREADY DID, NEVER BY DOING LESS. A hop that has already passed keeps its verdict instead of being re-decided. A hop nobody has judged is still judged, at full strength.

WHY THAT IS THE RIGHT WAY ROUND. The rigor is the product. A faster walk that checks less is a different and worse product wearing the same name, and nobody would be able to tell from the outside which one they were running.

THE TEST THAT KEEPS IT HONEST. Any change here must leave the same states green for the same reasons. A speed-up that turns a red hop green has broken the goal it was serving.

### A person's responsiveness against the engine's completeness

THE PULL. Answering within a second and computing everything a caller asked for are not always both possible.

THE RULING IS ALREADY MADE and it was restated today: past a second, the work moves to the background and the surface says what it is doing. Neither goal loses, because the answer stops being a choice between finishing and replying.

WHAT THIS ROUND OWES IT. The engine half of that rule has stood since the first round. The person's half was missing its background clause until today. This round's changes must not reintroduce a call that simply blocks.

### No new goal is needed

NOTHING HERE ARGUES FOR A GOAL THE PRODUCT DOES NOT ALREADY HOLD. A published time budget per hop is a measure on an existing goal rather than a new one, and it was ruled by the owner rather than derived here.

SO THERE IS NO ESCALATION. A delta needing a new goal would be arguing for a new vision, and this one is not.
