# i55-narration-gets-lean-the-decision-graph-s

## Goal

Narration gets lean: the decision graph stops being a second machine the agent drives by hand beside the one it is walking.

## Rough vision

THE OWNER HAS STORED MATERIAL FOR THIS, in their words about picture-in-picture substate machines. FIND IT BEFORE DESIGNING ANYTHING. Nothing here guesses at what that term means.

WHAT THE CURRENT SYSTEM COSTS, measured on one session rather than argued.

- A brief is capped at ninety characters, and one chaining three separator-joined parts is rewritten or refused. A number containing a comma was read as a chain and refused.
- The corrector turned one chained brief into four checklist items that meant nothing, and each then cost a call to resolve.
- The stall guard counts updates since anything CLOSED, so steady work on one long item reads as a stall and warns at five.
- A call carries one op, so closing five leftover nodes costs five calls that move no work.

THE SHAPE OF THE COMPLAINT. The bookkeeping shows up in the call log as calls that did nothing but bookkeeping.

QUESTIONS THE DESIGN HAS TO ANSWER, and they are questions rather than answers.

- Does a node need closing at all, or does the state it belongs to close it on exit?
- Can one call carry several ops?
- Should the checklist be derived from the walk rather than authored beside it?

WHAT DONE LOOKS LIKE. An agent narrates by working, and the log still tells the story without gaps. No call exists whose only purpose is to tidy the graph.
