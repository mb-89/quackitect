### Reopened and re-earned at gate-architecture, 2026-08-20

THREE OF THESE ROWS NAMED A MECHANISM AND THE METHOD FORBIDS IT.
meth-requirement-authoring:148 — "A named mechanism ('shall use a queue') is
design frozen as obligation. Name the outcome; the mechanism is M4's to choose."
items/requirement.md:143 — "WHAT, NEVER HOW."

#### The maximum

`req-a-milestone-takes-the-maximum-complexity-over-its-rows` named a reduction
step. It excluded every design that names a driver per state, which was three of
the four M4 later composed.

#### The roster

`req-one-model-list-is-read-live-from-the-repository` named a file layout. It
excluded every design holding no roster at all, including the one that scored
highest on surviving a host swap.

#### The declaration

`req-every-matrix-row-declares-its-complexity` named a declaration mechanism
and forbade derivation by construction. A loader that refuses a row which does
not DECLARE cannot accept a computed value.

#### Where the restatements came from

THE OUTCOME WAS IN EACH ROW'S OWN `breaks_if_removed` THE WHOLE TIME. No harm
was dropped and no demand was weakened. What left is the sentence about how.

#### Why it was not caught here

Every one of the three passes the EARS shape check, carries a breaks_if_removed,
carries a damage grade and refines a use case.

Nothing a requirement is checked for looks at whether the `shall` names an
outcome or a mechanism, and the three read exactly like the seven that do not.

#### What it cost

Measured at gate-architecture: after M4 spent seven finders and twenty-nine
options on the design space, every candidate on the chart violated at least one
of these three. No state held a must against a candidate until M5.

### A fourth one, found at gate-architecture when the must-check was finally run

`req-the-complexity-value-is-read-live-and-never-pinned` names a source and a timing: "shall be read from the matrix at the moment it is needed".

ITS OWN `breaks_if_removed` ASKS FOR NEITHER. "If complexity enters the demands, every demand in every standing record moves at once and every claim behind them reopens." The harm is the demand ledger and nothing else.

WHAT THE EXTRA CLAUSE COST, MEASURED. Two of the four candidates on M4's chart pick `opt-the-complexity-rides-the-cell-the-compiled-state-already-carries`, and that option's own text names this requirement's first half as the objection to it. Half the chart was ineligible on a clause nobody had argued for.

IT IS RESTATED TO ITS OWN HARM: keep a step's complexity out of every record's demand ledger, so that a complexity changing reopens no standing claim. The fatal grade stands. The test stands.

WHY IT WAS NOT FOUND WITH THE OTHER THREE. The first sweep looked for requirements naming a mechanism the SEED had chosen. This one names a mechanism nobody chose — it was written as a guard, and a guard that forbids too much reads exactly like a guard that forbids enough.

THE ISSUE HAD ALREADY BEEN RAISED AND NOTHING ACTED ON IT. `raid-iss-the-live-read-rule-forbids-more-than-its-own-reason-needs` was minted at M4, said this in one sentence, and sat open through four states and two gates. NAMING A DEFECT ACCURATELY IN THE RIGHT PLACE IS NOT FIXING IT, which is the failure shape contract rule 5 describes.

### And one of the restatements was itself wrong

`req-a-milestone-takes-the-maximum-complexity-over-its-rows` was restated a second time, an hour after the first.

THE FIRST RESTATEMENT READ "The engine shall walk no step by a driver weaker than that step's own difficulty requires." That makes the engine the driver.

ANOTHER MUST FORBIDS EXACTLY THAT. `req-the-machine-names-a-driver-and-starts-nothing`: "The lane shall publish the named driver on the pull and shall start no process on account of it, on any host and in any mode."

SO TWO MUSTS COULD NOT BOTH BIND, and under the first wording all four candidates failed — because none of them spawns anything, which is the other must working correctly.

WHAT FOUND IT: an independent must-check, run cold against all four candidates, commissioned at gate-architecture. Not a re-reading of the requirements.

THE SECOND WORDING NAMES THE ENGINE'S OWN ACT. It sizes units of work and publishes a difficulty; the demand is that the published figure is never weaker than the hardest step inside the unit, and that the distance to each step's own figure is readable.

THE LESSON IS ABOUT THE REPAIR AND NOT THE DEFECT. Trading a frozen mechanism for a fresh contradiction is what this repair invites, and it happened on the first attempt at three requirements. A restatement is checked the same way the original should have been: against the candidates, and against the other musts.

### Three nodes carried prose that fought their own statements

A SECOND COLD MUST-CHECK READ ALL TEN MUSTS AGAINST ALL FOUR CANDIDATES and reported, among other things, that the requirement layer was arguing with itself.

`req-a-milestone-takes-the-maximum-complexity-over-its-rows` — its Detail opened "THE MAXIMUM IS THE ONLY SAFE REDUCTION". Its restated statement licenses naming per state with a unit of one. A reader taking the Detail rather than the statement mis-scores three of four candidates, exactly as the id does. Corrected to what is actually forbidden: a published figure weaker than the hardest step inside the unit it covers.

`req-the-complexity-value-is-read-live-and-never-pinned` — its Detail cited `demandOf` where the check that decides what a demand holds is `demandsFor` at engine/iterations.ts:289. Both functions exist and the record was citing two of them for one verification as though they were the same check. Corrected, with the line numbers.

`req-a-machine-decision-repeats` — minted at derive-criteria as the fifteenth AXIS, on the argument that the standing fourteen could not express why the chosen design is chosen. It carries `priority: must`, so it never scored anything, and `cut-criteria` recorded that as correct. THE AXIS WAS MINTED FOR A JOB, MADE A GATE, AND EXCLUDED FROM THE JOB. The priority stays a must and the hole derive-criteria named is still open; what would close it is a `should` measuring how much of a decision's derivation the record carries, and minting that at a gate about to score against it is the one edit that cannot be honest.

### What this says about restating requirements

EVERY ONE OF THE THREE IS PROSE LEFT BEHIND BY A CHANGE TO A STATEMENT. Two are sentences that were true before the restatement and false after. The third is a mismatch between what a node was minted for and what its priority lets it do.

NOTHING MECHANICAL LOOKS AT THIS. The EARS check reads the statement. The damage grade, the refinement and the verify method are fields. A Detail section arguing against its own statement passes every check a requirement faces, which is the same shape as the defect the restatements were repairing.

SO A RESTATEMENT IS NOT DONE WHEN THE STATEMENT IS RIGHT. The node's own prose is part of what the next reader takes, and this record has now twice found a reader quoting a candidate's or a requirement's prose rather than the field that binds.

### And two more Details were doing it, found by the fourth pass on the same day

THE FIRST THREE CORRECTIONS WERE NOT A SWEEP. They were the three a reader happened to notice, and a fourth cold pass over the same register found two more of exactly the same shape.

`req-one-model-list-is-read-live-from-the-repository` — its Detail argued that the pattern is already in the tree twice and "THE LIST IS THE THIRD OF EXACTLY THAT KIND". That presumes a held list, and the statement licenses holding nothing. The two shapes that satisfy it are now written out, with the held file as an EXAMPLE rather than the demand.

`req-every-matrix-row-declares-its-complexity` — its Detail said "The complexity KEY takes the same treatment". A key is a declaration, and a derived value has none. Corrected to what must fail loudly: a row the engine reaches at an applying column and cannot get a complexity for, however it would have been arrived at.

### The pattern is now five for five and it is worth naming as one thing

EVERY MECHANISM-NAMING STATEMENT LEFT A MECHANISM-PRESUMING DETAIL BEHIND IT. Five requirements were restated at this state today; five had prose below the frontmatter that still argued for the mechanism the statement had dropped.

NOTHING MECHANICAL LOOKS BELOW THE FRONTMATTER. The EARS check reads `statement`. The damage grade, the refinement, the priority and the verify method are fields. A Detail arguing against its own statement passes every check a requirement faces.

AND THE DETAIL IS WHAT A READER TAKES. This record has now twice caught a downstream state quoting a node's prose rather than the field that binds — a scorer anchoring on a candidate's paragraph rather than its picks, and a cut promoting an axis on a sentence a requirement's Detail contradicts.

SO A RESTATEMENT IS NOT DONE WHEN THE STATEMENT IS RIGHT, and this is the second time this form has had to say so. The first time it was said about three nodes and the sweep that produced it missed two more.

### The source was the problem, and it was restated after these were

A FIFTH COLD PASS ASKED WHERE THE MECHANISMS CAME FROM. `uc-let-the-machine-name-the-driver` had them in its own steps: "It takes the MAXIMUM", "one file in the repository and the same file on every host", "It puts the model name on the pull", "live from the matrix, and never from a pin", with a precondition naming a model list and a guarantee promising a model name.

SO THIS STATE DID ITS JOB CORRECTLY AGAINST A SOURCE THAT HAD ALREADY DECIDED. Every one of the five mechanism-naming musts is a faithful derivation from a step that named a mechanism.

FOR MOST OF A DAY THE RESTATEMENTS FLOATED FREE OF THEIR SOURCES. Five musts named outcomes while each still cited a step mandating what it had dropped. The sharpest is `req-the-complexity-value-is-read-live-and-never-pinned`, restated to permit a pinned read, citing "uc-let-the-machine-name-the-driver step 2" — a step that read "never from a pin".

THE USE CASE IS NOW RESTATED and the `source_refs` on all five point at steps that name outcomes. Nothing in the register moves as a result: the demands were already the outcomes, and what changed is that their sources now agree with them.

### What this says about the repair order

THE REPAIR RAN UPWARDS AND SHOULD HAVE RUN DOWNWARDS. Requirements were restated first, then the function layer, then the flows, then the candidates, then — five passes later — the use case they all derive from.

EACH LAYER WAS REPAIRED AGAINST THE LAYER ABOVE IT, and the layer above was wrong. That is why the same defect kept reappearing in a new place: it was being copied down faster than it was being removed.

WHAT WOULD HAVE COST LESS: asking, at the first mechanism-naming requirement, where the mechanism came from. The answer was one file away and it took four cold readers to look.
