## prior_art_positioned

SCANNED LIVE AT THIS GATE, 2026-08-17, on the motivation question rather than the budget question the kickoff scanned.

WHAT THEY DO BETTER, FIRST, AND IT ATTACKS OUR OWN RULING.

A 2010 University of Michigan study is reported to have found that the slow-to-fast progress bar — the most technically honest representation of actual progress — produced the HIGHEST abandonment rate at 21.8 percent. The framing is that a signal carrying discouraging news drives people away however accurate it is. The field's practical answer is animation pacing rather than fidelity: an easing bar feels faster and more trustworthy than one that starts and stops.

PRIMARY NOT SEEN. These are secondary write-ups; the study itself was not read, and the citation says so rather than dressing itself up.

WHAT SUPPORTS US IN THE SAME BODY OF WORK: people shown a moving feedback bar were more satisfied and willing to wait around three times longer than people shown nothing. Telling beats silence, which is exactly what draft-vision ruled and exactly what the stop-at control failed.

WHAT OURS SHEDS AND WHY THE TRADE HOLDS. The UX literature is about perceived duration on a surface a person is staring at. Ours must also serve an AGENT, which has no patience to manage and needs a fact rather than a feeling. Binding the demand to a modelled boundary serves both, and no progress-bar research addresses the agent side at all.

WHAT THE SCAN CHANGES: the ruling narrows rather than falls. Honest means the person is never left guessing whether it is working. It does NOT mean publishing a faithful completion percentage, and doing that may be the worst available choice. Minted as raid-risk-an-accurate-progress-signal-can-drive-abandonment.

Sources: [NN/G](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/), [Smart Interface Design Patterns](https://smart-interface-design-patterns.com/articles/designing-better-loading-progress-ux/), [Bayesian evaluation of progress bars](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13909), [The Psychology of Waiting](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/the-psychology-of-waiting-in-ux-0f0b24cdeb8f)
