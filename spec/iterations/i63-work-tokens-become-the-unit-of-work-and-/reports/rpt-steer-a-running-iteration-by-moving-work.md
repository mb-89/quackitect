# Report / steer a running iteration by moving work

## What the story demands

Work that belongs in a running round is moved onto the state that will do it.
Its count goes up, the state owes it, and the state cannot be left until it is
settled or moved on.

## How it was performed

ON A ROUND THAT WAS ACTUALLY RUNNING — this one, mid-walk, with the owner
watching the board from outside.

## What was observed

WORK WAS MOVED ONTO A STATE AND THE STATE THEN OWED IT. Three tokens stood at
the backlog: first paint filters rows, open work holds record, and one pull
walks through. Their place was changed to `iterations/i63/fix-findings`, and
from that moment the state carried them.

THE STATE COULD NOT BE LEFT UNTIL THEY WERE SETTLED OR MOVED. Two were settled
as fixed. One was settled `carried`, on the owner's own routing, with the
finding attached. Only then did the walk go forward.

THE MOVE WAS VISIBLE FROM OUTSIDE, and that is what makes it steering rather
than bookkeeping. Before the move the owner could not see the work at all and
said so: "I don't see what you're working on because you're not working on any
token." The tokens were at the backlog, which is not a position, so the board
drew nothing. Moving them to the position is what put them on the board.

THE STEERING RAN THE OTHER WAY TOO. The owner named work in chat, a token was
opened for it in the same breath, and it was worked before the state was left.
That happened five times tonight.

## What the observation left behind

- Four settled tokens at `iterations/i63/fix-findings`, each with what happened.
- The call log, holding the move and every settle.

## What was NOT observed

THE GESTURE THE STORY DESCRIBES. The story has a person dragging a row out of
the backlog table onto a state on the drawing, with the states lighting up to
show which buckets would take it. That drag was not performed. The MOVE was
performed through the lane, and the mechanism it exercises is the same one the
drag would use — but the surface half of the story is unobserved here.
