# Report / see every open thing at one glance

## What the story demands

The machine itself shows how much is owed on every state, so the whole picture
is readable without asking anybody.

## THE HEADLINE

THE PICTURE IS THERE AND IT WAS READ. What was not done is looking at the
drawn counts on screen, because the agent may not look at a screen unasked.

## What was observed

EVERY STATE, WITH ITS STANDING, IN ONE ANSWER. `se_surface` was called through
the evening and each time printed all 64 states of this record with their
colour and their marks. A state holding open work carries `open` beside its
name, so the states that owed something were readable in one pass.

THE COUNT WAS RIGHT AND IT MOVED. Early in the evening five states carried
`open`: enumerate-space, run-candidates, run-spikes, build-steps and run-demos.
fix-findings did NOT, and that was the tell that its four tokens were parked at
the backlog rather than at the position. The picture was correct about a
mistake I had made, before I knew I had made it.

THE TWO STORES OFF TO THE SIDE ARE IN THE SAME PICTURE. The backlog and the
retro both appear as their own groups in the work editor, with their own
counts — measured tonight at 395 rows in one pane, both groups shipping closed
so they do not bury the states.

## What the observation left behind

- The surface answers in the call log, each one a whole-machine picture.
- The measured editor card: four top-level groups, backlog and retro among
  them, each carrying its own count.

## What was NOT observed

THE GLANCE ITSELF. The story is about reading counts along a state's edges on
the drawn machine, without clicking. That is a screen, and the agent does not
look at one unasked. What was read is the same fact in text.

SO THE ENGINE HALF IS PROVEN AND THE DRAWING HALF IS THE OWNER'S TO CONFIRM.
The data behind the counts is served and correct; whether it reads at a glance
is a judgment only somebody looking can make.
