# The spike drawing

One state per unknown picked at rank-unknowns. Seven were picked from the
exposure chart, filtered by whether a timeboxed probe could actually settle
them.

All seven are independent and hang off start; the join waits for every one.

## The two that could reverse the design

THE CROSS-PANEL DRAG and THE READ-EVIDENCE SCOPE each get a day, because a
negative answer does not lose a comparison — it reopens one that was signed,
with a structure built on top of it.

## The five that shape it

MINTING COST, HEADING PER ITEM, VOLUME, PATH LENGTH and POSITION COUNT get half
a day each, and the path length is an hour. None of them can unseat the winner;
each of them changes what the build has to do.

## What each state writes

It writes its finding back into the register entry the line names, and mints
nothing. A spike that finds nothing writes that it found nothing, with the
figures it took.

## Why the timeboxes are uneven

THE BOX IS SIZED BY WHAT A WRONG ANSWER COSTS, never by how hard the question
looks. An hour is enough for the path length because the probe is a count. A
day is right for the drag because the answer decides whether a surface exists.

## What the round already knows

THE PICK METHOD IS RECORDED at rank-unknowns: exposure first, then whether a
timeboxed probe can settle it, then whether the iteration's own goal already
exercises it. Entries that are WORK rather than unknowns were deliberately not
picked, on the owner's ruling of 2026-08-26 that the register had been carrying
too much of that.
