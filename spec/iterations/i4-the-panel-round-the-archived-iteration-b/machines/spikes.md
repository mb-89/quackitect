# The spike drawing

One timeboxed spike per unknown the ranking seeded. Three were seeded and all
three hang off start; the join waits for every one.

- TIME THE COMPUTED VIEW. The winner took its seat on a tied count and this is
  the cell that would have moved it. The budget is already breached at 3468,
  3966 and 4026 milliseconds, so the question is not whether the surface is
  fast but whether resolving the view makes a broken thing meaningfully worse.
- WRITE THE EMITTER PREDICATE. The guard won the fatal axis and nobody has
  written the rule it rests on. It passes when the flags are a short list and
  every entry is plainly a second surface or plainly an exemption. It fails
  when the exemption list is the longer one, because that is a convention
  arriving with paperwork.
- BUILD A PASSIVE VIEW. The newest assumption, and the only one needing a
  running extension. It asks what the host insists on deciding for itself.

## Why these three and not the others

FOUR MORE WERE CONSIDERED AND DECLINED AT rank-unknowns, each with its reason
on the record: two are already probed, one waits on a history no timebox can
hurry, and one needs a machine this one is not.

## The timeboxes are real

RUNNING OUT IS A VERDICT. A spike that reaches its box without an answer
reports what it learned and what it would take, and that report is the
finding. Throwaway means throwaway: what survives is the finding, never the
code.

## What no spike may do

NO SPIKE DECIDES ANYTHING. A finding that shakes the architecture returns to
the architecture gate. A finding that shakes a requirement ripples to the
requirements milestone. Neither happens quietly here.
