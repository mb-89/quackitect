## prfaq

PRESS RELEASE

QUACKITECT'S WALK NO LONGER STOPS WHEN ITS WORK TAKES A MINUTE.

An agent driving a Quackitect machine can start a long job and carry on. One call tells it everything still running and how much longer each piece needs. A step whose leaving condition runs a program hands the walk back at once and delivers its verdict later.

Before this, a single step could hold the agent's only verb for over a minute. On one measured occasion it held it for sixty-eight seconds and two calls timed out at the tool boundary. One of those had partly landed, so the agent was told its work had failed when it had in fact moved.

"The machine was asking me to sit and watch it think," is the shape of the complaint. On a laptop that is annoying. On a cloud box nobody is watching, a frozen verb is a run that never finishes and nobody notices.

The report answers the caller rather than a screen. Every figure it gives names the measurement it was computed from, and a job with no basis says it cannot estimate rather than inventing a number.

Available to every machine the engine drives, with no configuration.

HOSTILE FAQ

Q. Is this not just a status endpoint? Why does it need a major?

The report is not the major. The deferred exit verdict is. Today a hop completes when its exit script returns, one rule with one moment and one answer. Letting a state be left with a verdict still owed means green has a third value, and every reader of green inherits it. That is the walking core's execution model, not a feature on top of it.

Q. So you are breaking the one rule that makes green cheap. What breaks with it?

Three readers, named: the gate that asks whether its feeders are green, the route drawer that asks which hops already pass, and the panel that paints a state by its status. Each has two answers today and needs three. This is registered as `raid-risk-a-hop-that-finishes-later-makes-green-ambiguous`, graded `crippling`, and it is the thing that must be designed before anything is built.

Q. Why not simply make the scripts faster?

Because the long one is the verification battery, and it is long by design. The engine's own kill timer for a condition script is 600,000 ms, and its comment records that a 150-second cap already killed the battery mid-run. There is no version of this where the script is short.

Q. Why not raise the tool timeout instead?

That trades a timeout for a hang. The caller still learns nothing while it waits, and the measured failure was not only the timeout — it was a call that had partly landed and reported failure. A longer timeout makes that window wider, not narrower.

Q. Polling is cheap and an estimate is a lie with a number on it. Why not just build a better status verb?

The steelman is right about the danger and wrong about the cost. The danger is real enough that the goal system ruled conflict one for honesty: every figure names its basis, and a job with no basis says so. The cost is the part the argument misses — the measured incident is not cheap polling, it is a caller told the work failed while it had moved. The non-freezing verb stands on its own with no estimate at all.

Q. Your estimate rests on timings your own register calls corrosive. Why should anyone believe the number?

They should not believe it more than its basis warrants, which is why the basis rides with it. `raid-asm-battery-timings-measure-work` records summed case time of 1,534,695 ms against a wall of 76,985 ms. That instrument is explicitly not repaired here, and `raid-risk-a-time-remaining-is-believed-more-than-it-deserves` is graded `expected` because of it.

Q. What does the report say on a fresh container with no history?

That it cannot estimate. This is the one question the vision stated as settled and had not answered, and it is now `raid-asm-a-first-run-has-timings-to-estimate-from` with a written probe. A fresh cloud box is not a rare edge — it is every unattended run's first test call.

Q. Jenkins has done this for years. What makes yours worth building?

Jenkins does it better on the axis that matters most: its estimate derives from prior builds of the same job, at scale, for years. Ours has neither. What ours has is the caller. Every alternative reports to a person watching a dashboard, and none answers the program that started the work and must decide whether to wait.

Q. You are narrowing the test scope in the same iteration. Is that not two risks at once?

It is, and the register carries it as `raid-risk-a-narrower-test-scope-misses-a-break`. What makes it defensible is that today's fallback is not the safe option it looks like: running every test when nothing answers for the diff does not answer for the diff either. Ten batteries ran in one session on that fallback, most fired by markdown edits.

Q. Why is the third piece in this iteration at all rather than its own?

Because it is the same reader. An agent that can now keep working while a job runs will start more jobs, and a battery fired by a markdown edit is the job it will start most often. Fixing the report without fixing what it reports on would make the waste visible and leave it there.

Q. What would make you stop?

Finding that the hop-completion path already supports a deferred verdict, so nothing in the walking core moves. That was looked for at the kickoff and not found: the exit script is awaited inline at `deliverable/engine/session.ts:3686`, which is exactly why the pull freezes.

3. WHETHER A BASIS EXISTS AT ALL FOR A NON-BATTERY JOB. The FAQ assumes the estimate is arithmetic over recorded work. That holds for a battery, which knows its case count. It does not obviously hold for an arbitrary shell command, and nothing in the packet says what such an entry reports. NOT PREVIOUSLY REGISTERED, and it is now folded into the scope's follow-up: the progress lines a condition script already emits are the cheapest candidate basis, and this iteration excludes reading them.

WHAT DID NOT NEED FOLDING. The steelman against building this at all was answered at the kickoff gate and the answer still holds. The Jenkins comparison was checked and its limit is recorded: the declaration was seen, the derivation rule was not.

ONE THING THE FAQ SHARPENED RATHER THAN BROKE. The iteration's hard edge is the third goal. The other three could each be cut without breaking what is left. The non-freezing exit cannot, because it changes what green means.
