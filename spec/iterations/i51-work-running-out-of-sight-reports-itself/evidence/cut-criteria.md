THE CUT IS NOT A GUESS. Every candidate on this chart changes what the product says about work it cannot see. Nothing on any of the four curves touches search, the survey, the sweep, the scope decision or the ninety-odd other functions the pool governs.

SO THE ROWS ARE NOT CUT FOR BEING MINOR. They are cut because every candidate meets them identically by construction, which is the only legal reason and is checkable from the candidate records without a score anywhere.

THE SORT DID ALMOST NOTHING, and it is worth saying why. Two rows is not an ordering problem. `req-a-wrong-act-never-passes-silently` is graded fatal and `req-call-answers-in-one-second` corrosive, so the mechanical sort puts them in that order and no judgment move is owed.

THE LINE IS DRAWN ON THE SCALE rather than on a count, as the method demands. Everything above it ends the product or stops a use case. Both survivors are above it, so the threshold is not doing work here either.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE EVALUATION. With two axes and four candidates the scoring is small, and the real comparison will be carried by the register entries rather than by the score. That is worth knowing before the totals appear rather than after.

AND IT SAYS SOMETHING ABOUT THE POOL ITSELF. Ninety-seven rows cut as one class is a criterion set derived for the whole product, being asked a question about one change. That is not wrong, and it is the reason this state exists at all.
