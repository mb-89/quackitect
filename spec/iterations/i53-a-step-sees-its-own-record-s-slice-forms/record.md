# i53-a-step-sees-its-own-record-s-slice-forms

## Goal

A step sees its own record's slice: forms draw what the open iteration owns, not everything the repository has ever held.

## Rough vision

THE PROBLEM. Two steps present the whole inherited corpus with no mark of ownership, so the person cannot tell their work from everybody else's.

WHAT WAS COUNTED.

- A judgment step showed one hundred and nine pairs, of which roughly ten belonged to the open iteration. The session concluded that answering them would set weights binding on every future decision, and halted.
- A chart step drew twenty-six rows where nine belonged. That defeats its own instruction, which says each line must speak to every row.

WHAT DONE LOOKS LIKE.

- A form draws the open record's slice by default, and says how much it left out.
- What is inherited is still reachable, marked as inherited, and never mixed in unlabelled.
- The instruction on each step matches what it actually shows, so an author is not asked to answer for rows that are not theirs.

WHY THESE TWO ARE ONE PIECE. Both are the same defect in the same layer: a form built from the whole corpus where the record's own slice was meant. The fix is one rule about what a step is allowed to draw, and two steps then stop breaking it.

## Carried in by the retro of 2026-08-24

THE CRITERION POOL SHOULD BE THE ROUND'S OWN, not the product's whole history.

WHAT HAPPENS TODAY. The step that cuts criteria offers every criterion the
product ever had. One round's candidates differed in a single decision each,
so eighty-odd rows were struck by position rather than by judgment.

WHY IT BELONGS HERE. This record already says a form draws what the open
record owns. That is the same rule, and the criterion step is a third state
breaking it.

THE SAME SHAPE PROBABLY BINDS THREE MORE. Deriving criteria, probing
assumptions and partitioning functions all draw from the whole corpus today.

THE RULE IS ALREADY WRITTEN into the cutting step's own guidance. The
mechanism is what is missing.
