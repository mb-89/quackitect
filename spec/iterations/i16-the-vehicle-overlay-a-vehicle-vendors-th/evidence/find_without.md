## anything_else

### The table is per cluster and the real trims are finer

THE FORM ASKS ONE ROW PER CLUSTER, which is right for the shared partition and too coarse for what this iteration is deciding. the-bootstrap holds four separate questions, and the interesting trims are inside it rather than at its boundary.

THE TWO THAT MATTER, in order of how much they cut.

- CUT THE UPDATE CHANNEL, and the entire accounting apparatus goes with it. That is [[opt-no-record-because-the-copy-takes-no-updates]], and it is what the product ships today: a fresh single-commit repository with no merge base.
- CUT THE OVERLAY, and the copy edits what it received in place. That is [[opt-no-overlay-the-copy-edits-what-it-received]], legal because the owner ruled that nothing in a copy is sealed.

THEY ARE NOT THE SAME TRIM AND THE SMALLER ONE IS THE TRAP. Removing the overlay leaves the accounting question standing and destroys the pristine baseline several other options need to answer it. The method's own warning is about exactly this: trimming what is cheap feels productive and saves nothing.

### Both new nulls fail a demand, and naming which one is the finding

NEITHER IS BEATEN ON A SCORE. Each fails a `must` row outright.

- The update-channel null fails [[req-overlay-drift-reported]], and contradicts the owner's ruling that a copy can pull engine updates from its source.
- The overlay null does not fail a stated requirement. It is out on cost, and its cost is the one three vendoring systems document in their own words: the tree is deleted and rebuilt, and nothing checks whether it was modified.

THAT ASYMMETRY IS WORTH KEEPING. One is refused by a demand and the other is merely expensive, and a chart that scored them the same would be wrong.

### Four trims were already done, and citing them is the point

THE HOLDING PEN, THE QUERY, THE DISPOSITION AND THE WALK each already carry a null option minted by an earlier iteration. i1 and i15 asked those questions and recorded the answers.

SO THIS STATE MINTED NOTHING FOR THEM. Adding a second null to a cell that has one is how a chart acquires phantom width, and this iteration has already made that mistake once today with the tree-naming pair.

AND ONE OF THOSE STANDING NULLS IS THE STRONGEST TRIM ON THE WALK CLUSTER, from i27: delete the resolution decision and make every path carry its tree. Its own node calls it the null option for that whole iteration. It applies unchanged to this one, because a copy that is told which tree it drives on every invocation needs no pointer at all — and the rootward reading found that self-location, which needs no record either, is what mature tools actually use.
