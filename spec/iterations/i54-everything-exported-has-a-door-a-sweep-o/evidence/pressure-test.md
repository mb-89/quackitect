Every codebase drifts from its design. Until now the only way to find out where was to read the whole thing.

### Why not just use dependency-cruiser?

THE SAME SUBJECT, REACHED A DIFFERENT WAY. This answer used to say different subject, and that is false against the tool's own rules reference.

Importing `node:fs` IS a module dependency. dependency-cruiser's reference names `not-to-core` as an example rule forbidding dependencies on core modules, and lists `fs` and `node:test` under the `core` dependency type. Every rule carries a `from` as well as a `to`, so who may reach a capability is directly expressible.

WHAT IT CANNOT SEE is a reach that does not go through an import: a shell command, or a dynamic require. That is the same hole our own write-time guard has, measured at 38 of 178 modules. It is not a point in our favour.

AND ITS EXCEPTIONS HAVE NO AUTHOR. `depcruise-baseline` generates the violation list mechanically and lowers each entry to ignore. Nobody typed a line of it, so nobody can be asked about any line. Its comment field is documented as not used in any rule logic.

### 79 modules import node:fs directly. You cannot move them and you have no ratchet.

TRUE, AND NOTHING HERE MOVES 79 MODULES. Scope covers the 42 judged sites in seven files. The absence of a ratchet is registered, and the entry names the specific trap of answering it by folding a frozen set into the exemption registry.

### The reason requirement will decay into boilerplate within a month.

THE MOST LIKELY WAY THIS FAILS, and there is no mechanism against it. Nothing judges whether a reason is any good. The widget card ignores a bullet with no reason, which catches the empty case and not the lazy one.

WHAT WE HAVE INSTEAD IS A KILL CRITERION rather than a defence: if the list grows until the exception is the norm, the rule was too wide and gets narrowed.

### You are one agent writing a rule for your own engine and blessing your own gates.

TRUE AND LOGGED. It is the first override on the kickoff gate's verdict, with the mechanical cause named: the hands ceiling reads from the gate it gates, so no reviewer could be registered before it signed.

## findings_folded

ONE THING FOLDED BACK AND ONE DID NOT, and the difference is worth recording.

WHAT FOLDED. Writing the hostile FAQ moved the reason-quality problem from a background worry to the named way this fails. The scope box now has to say that hatch entries get their reasons written and reviewed as part of the build, rather than collected afterwards. Collected-afterwards reasons are the boilerplate the FAQ predicts, and the widget card's own guard catches only the empty case.

THAT CHANGES THE ORDER OF WORK. The 22 unimprovable sites are not a leftover pile to document at the end. They are the first real test of whether the reason requirement survives contact, and they should be written while the person still remembers why each site is what it is.

WHAT DID NOT FOLD, AND WHY THAT IS THE HONEST ANSWER. The FAQ's strongest attack — nobody reads a long registry — was already the second assumption in the register, with a probe that drafts the list before building it. The pressure test found it again rather than finding it first. That is a confirmation, not a new finding, and calling it new would inflate what this state produced.

NOTHING UPSTREAM WAS CONTRADICTED. The vision, the actual and the delta all survive the test. The press release could be written from them without reaching for anything they do not say, which is the weak form of evidence this state can offer and is stated as weak.

THE ONE PLACE THE PRESS RELEASE OVERREACHED IN DRAFT. An earlier line claimed the engine "now routes every disk write through one door". It does not and will not: 22 judged sites stay outside by design, and 53 are unjudged. The line was cut rather than softened, because a press release that needs a footnote to be true is the failure this state exists to catch.

## scope

THIS EFFORT TAKES ON FIVE THINGS, and they are ordered so the cheapest certain one goes first.

1. THE CONTAINMENT PREDICATE, FIRST AND ALONE. Export it from paths.ts and delete its five hand-written copies. It needs no door, no design decision and no new mechanism, and it settles a live disagreement between two guards on two recursive deletes. The delta grades that issue crippling. It can land before anything else is agreed.

2. THE EXPORTS SWEEP, WHICH IS THE SEEDED RECORD. Replace the hand-written list of two with a sweep over every exported entry point, name everything no surface can reach, and answer each with a door or a recorded deletion. The two already-found pieces get theirs.

3. THE MECHANISM, GENERALISED FROM THE ONE THAT ALREADY RUNS. Take the four parts of the widget guard and make them a shape a second rule compiles into: one rule, one registry, one declared hatch, two callers with no second copy.

THE REASON IS THE ENTRY AND NOT METADATA ON IT. That is the single thing the field scan found nobody else doing, and it is the part worth building rather than borrowing. A registry line without a reason is not a line.

NO BLANKET SWITCH. Rust has --cap-lints allow, Bazel has --check_visibility=false, dependency-cruiser has severity ignore. The widget precedent has no equivalent and this one will not gain one, because adding it undoes everything above it.

4. THE DISK DOOR, OVER THE JUDGED SITES ONLY. 42 write sites in seven modules are judged as improvable and those are what the door covers. The 22 judged as unimprovable go on the declared hatch with their reasons, which is also the first real test of whether the reason requirement survives contact.

5. THE PRINCIPLE, WRITTEN ONCE INTO GUIDANCE where the machine reads it, rather than restated per rule.

THE OUTWARD DOOR STAYS IN SCOPE AND IS CONDITIONAL, exactly as disk was. Its 52 sites get the same judgment pass disk got, and it earns a door on that count or it does not. The kickoff gate's goals put it in and this state does not take it out; what it adds is that the door is earned by evidence rather than assumed by symmetry.
