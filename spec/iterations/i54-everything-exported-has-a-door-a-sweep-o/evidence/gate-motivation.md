### Press release

### Quackitect can now tell you where your codebase departs from its own design, and why

EVERY TEAM HAS RULES IT NO LONGER FOLLOWS. All database access goes through one place. Nothing in the UI layer reads a file. A year later, forty places do it anyway, each for a reason that made sense that day and that nobody wrote down.

QUACKITECT NOW MAKES THE BYPASS EXPLAIN ITSELF. A capability gets one door. Code that reaches past it is refused. The way through is an entry in a registry, and an entry without a written reason is not accepted.

"The forty shortcuts were never the problem," says the maintainer. "The problem was that the forty reasons existed only in forty people's heads on forty different days. Now they are a file I read in a minute."

THE FIRST CAPABILITY IS THE ENGINE'S OWN WRITES TO ITS RECORD FILES. Thirty places wrote a signed claim file directly, none of them replacing the file atomically and none checking a hash first. They now go through one writer that does both.

AVAILABLE IN THE ENGINE FROM THIS ITERATION.

### The hostile FAQ

### You measured 64 writes, scoped down to 30, and built a helper function. Why is that an architecture principle?

IT IS A FAIR HIT AND IT CHANGED THE FRAMING.

THE DELIVERABLE IS THE PATTERN, AND DISK IS ITS FIRST TEST. One rule, one registry, a reason per entry, no second copy. Thirty sites is small on purpose: a pattern proven on a small capability can be argued about cheaply, and a pattern proven on 117 sites cannot be un-built.

WHAT WOULD MAKE THE ANSWER A DODGE. If no second capability ever goes through it, the honest description is a helper function with a registry bolted on. The iteration is only worth its size if the pattern generalises, and that is registered as `raid-asm-the-widget-exemption-shape-generalises-to-a-whole-capability`.

### Your registry will fill with the words needed for performance. You have bought a field, not a discipline.

UNANSWERED, AND NOW REGISTERED AS `raid-asm-a-demanded-reason-is-a-considered-reason`.

A REQUIRED FIELD MAKES AN ENTRY NON-EMPTY. Nothing about it makes the entry thoughtful. Rust displays its `reason` in the lint message, and nobody claims Rust's allow-comments are insightful.

THE PROBE IS CHEAP AND IT IS WRITTEN DOWN. Read the widget registry's existing entries and count how many reasons are specific to their own line. The sample is small and that limit is stated in the entry.

### Go's internal packages have no exception mechanism at all and they work. Why is a registry not just a worse Go?

GO IS BETTER ON THE AXIS IT CHOSE, AND THAT IS SAID FIRST. No configuration, no linter to remember, the rule is the directory name and the compiler enforces it. Nothing here beats that.

THE PRICE GO PAYS IS THAT THE RULE MUST BE OBEYABLE ALWAYS. Where an exception is genuinely needed, a Go team moves the code or exports something it did not want to export. The bypass still happens; it just leaves no record and shows up as a widened public interface instead.

WHERE THE DOOR CAN BE MADE CHEAP ENOUGH, GO'S ANSWER WINS AND WE SHOULD TAKE IT. That is a real design instruction from this scan, not a concession.

### You already exempted the biggest writer in the codebase. What is a one-door rule that starts with an exemption?

`run.ts` IS NOT AN EXEMPTION. It is a place the rule does not apply, because its writes are appends to logs it owns and its paths are already jailed by three module-local helpers.

THE DISTINCTION IS LOAD-BEARING AND IT IS ALSO CONVENIENT FOR US, so it deserves suspicion. The test is whether the reason could be written for any module. It could not: 0 of 10 was measured, not asserted.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THE DISTINCTION. If the design ends up writing a scope rule that happens to exclude exactly the modules that were inconvenient, this answer was rationalisation.

### The containment bug is your best finding and it needs no door. Should this iteration not be that fix and nothing else?

THAT IS THE STRONGEST ARGUMENT IN THE FAQ AND IT IS NOT FULLY ANSWERED.

THE FIX IS SMALL AND THE ISSUE IS GRADED CRIPPLING. Five hand-written copies of one predicate, two of them disagreeing about absolute paths while guarding recursive deletes. It could ship on its own this week.

WHAT THE REST BUYS ON TOP. The containment fix removes one duplicate check. It does nothing about thirty writes to signed files with no atomic replacement, which is the other measured defect and the one that can lose a signature.

SO THE HONEST POSITION IS TWO ITEMS, NOT ONE. Whether they are one iteration or two is the owner's call at the gate, and the scope form says so.

### Six systems, and the one that forces a reason is a third-party plugin nobody installs. Is your differentiator just a thing nobody wants?

POSSIBLE, AND THE SCAN CANNOT RULE IT OUT. Adoption of `require-description` was not measured, and asserting it would be fabrication.

THE COUNTER-EVIDENCE IS THAT TWO TOOLCHAINS BUILT NEIGHBOURING MECHANISMS BY DEFAULT. Rust's `#[expect]` and ESLint's unused-directive report both exist to stop stale exceptions accumulating. That is evidence teams care what happens to exceptions over time, which is adjacent to caring why they were made, and adjacent is not the same.

### Your own audience is one maintainer of one codebase. Who is this press release for?

NOBODY YET, AND THE BUSINESS CASE SAYS SO IN THOSE WORDS. There is no acquirer, and the effort is argued in the maintainer's currency.

A PRESS RELEASE FOR AN AUDIENCE OF ONE IS STILL WORTH WRITING, because it forces the promise into a sentence a stranger could disagree with. Two of the questions above came from doing that.
