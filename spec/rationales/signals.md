# Why

The earlier versions of this project ran retros over the same kind of record,
and each one learned where the signal hides. This note carries what they
learned, so a reader starts where the last one stopped.

## 1. What makes it

A bad output is evidence about the thing that made it: a prompt, a rule, a
form, a refusal or a tool. Repairing the output buys one output. The second
version's retro held a rule with two halves. Fix the system and leave the
person alone, and fix what made the fault as well as the fault.

## 2. Refusals and failures

A refusal that keeps firing, or a command that keeps failing, marks a place
the machinery and the agent disagree. Ranked by rule, the log shows which
disagreement costs the most.

## 3. Two records

The log says what happened, and the transcript says why. Where the log shows
a retry, the transcript names the misreading behind it. A report written off
the counts alone gives the shape of a day and none of its reasons.

## 4. The thinking

The agent's thinking holds what it keeps to itself: the doubt before a guess,
the worry it sets aside, the plan it drops halfway. One long session worried
six times about its own length, and each time it said it was cutting scope.
It cut care too.

## 5. The owner's corrections

A correction is the most direct signal the record holds. An ask the owner
repeats is one the machinery failed to carry the first time.

## 6. Shell commands

One session ran a thousand shell commands, and most of them found a symbol,
read a line range or swapped a string. Each group is a verb the engine lacks,
written out one command at a time.

## 7. A script written twice

A helper written once and thrown away costs nothing. The same helper written
again next session names what the machinery lacks.

## 8. The tiring worker

Measured by fifths across one long session, the error rate tripled in the
last fifth while the length of a thought halved. A reader comparing the
first and last hours of a chapter sees the same shape.

## 9. Repeats

One bad afternoon is a story. The same shape three times, in chapters no
reader shares, is a property of the machinery.

## 10. The cheapest change

A check runs unread, a refusal arrives when needed, a gate stops the thing
happening, and a prefill makes the right answer easy. A sentence works only on
a reader who remembers it, so it comes last.

## 11. What went well

A retro asking only for faults loses the practice worth keeping. Naming what
made a chapter go well keeps the next retro from trading that practice away.

## 12. Each failed review alone

The third version read every failed review on its own. Five failures in one
record turned out one shape in five forms: a cheap proxy passing for the real
thing. Read in a batch, the shapes hide, and read one at a time, the repeat
shows.

## 13. Tools unused or corrected

A tool nobody calls is one the hand misses or doubts. A tool called and then
corrected answers the wrong question. Both point at the tool, and neither
shows in a count of what the hand did.
