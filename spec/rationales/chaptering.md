# Why

A retro reads everything since the last one, and that runs to days of
transcripts. No reader holds that at once, so the window splits into chapters
a reader takes whole.

## 1. The timeline first

The input holds days of transcripts across a hundred files. A hand cutting
without a map opens files at random and cuts where it happens to look. The
timeline counts the lines of every source per hour, so the hand sees where
the work happened before it reads a word.

## 2. Content sets the edges

The retro before this one cut its chapters by the clock alone, and five of
thirteen came back empty. A chapter holds one piece of work, so the content
sets its edges. Six hours holds half a working day, which a reader covers in
one pass, so it guides the length and binds nothing.

## 3. Idle time carries no chapter

A night or a weekend holds no work, and a chapter made of one gives a reader
nothing to read. The cut sits inside the idle stretch, so the stretch joins a
chapter's edge and adds no line to it.

## 6. The engine places the lines

A chapter is a span of time, and the engine places every timed line by its
time. So no line lands in two chapters or in none, and a reader receives its
lines as file and line ranges. The hand decides where to cut, and the engine
does the counting a hand gets wrong.
