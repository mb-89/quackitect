The conformance standard for the method in [[ref-rcm-nowlan-heap]]. It exists
because the name was being abused: its own foreword says processes that
differ significantly from the original were being sold under it, and that
some are actively counterproductive.

## The seven questions, and they are asked in order

- What are the FUNCTIONS of the asset, and to what standard?
- In what ways can it FAIL to fulfil them?
- What CAUSES each functional failure?
- What HAPPENS when each failure occurs?
- In what way does each failure MATTER?
- What should be done to PREDICT OR PREVENT it?
- What should be done if no suitable task can be found?

THE SEQUENCE IS PART OF THE STANDARD. A checklist applied before the function
is stated is the defect this standard was written to name.

## The three selection rules

- A task must be technically feasible AND worth doing.
- Where several qualify, the most cost-effective one wins.
- ASSESS AS IF NO TASK IS CURRENTLY BEING DONE.

THE LAST ONE IS THE ANTI-INERTIA RULE, and it transfers to a corpus for free.
A page may not be justified by the fact that somebody already maintains it.

## The closed set of policies

Its own vocabulary names six, and two of them are missing from SORTED.

- on-condition tasks
- scheduled restoration, which is overhaul
- scheduled discard
- FAILURE-FINDING, for a failure nobody would notice
- RUN-TO-FAILURE, which is a decision and not an absence of one
- one-time changes

## What scheduled restoration demands

The two age criteria from the founding report, plus one more: the task must
restore the item's resistance to failure to a level the owner tolerates.

## What an on-condition task demands

A defined potential-failure condition, a measurable interval between that
condition and the functional failure, and a check interval shorter than it.

NO MEASURABLE PRECURSOR MEANS NO ON-CONDITION TASK. That is most prose, and
pretending otherwise is the abuse the standard names.
