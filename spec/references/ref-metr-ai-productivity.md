The only randomised evidence found on whether an agent helps in the setting
an overhaul actually runs in.

## What was measured

THE SETTING IS THE POINT. Sixteen experienced developers, 246 tasks, in
mature repositories they had worked on for about five years. Tacit standards,
judgment-heavy work.

THE RESULT. With the tools allowed, they took 19% LONGER.

THE PART THAT SHOULD WORRY ANY AGENT-RUN SWEEP. They predicted a 24% speedup
beforehand. AFTER finishing, they still estimated a 20% speedup. The
measurement said the opposite, and nobody in the room could tell.

## What it does not show

IT IS BOUNDED. It is one trial, sixteen people, on early-2025
tooling, and its authors frame it as a snapshot and have since changed the
experiment's design.

HOW TO READ IT BESIDE THE OPPOSITE RESULT. Google measured most of a
migration's edits as machine-written and landed
([[ref-swe-at-google]] is the same programme's static-analysis half). These
are not in conflict. The Google work had an AUTOMATIC VALIDATOR and this did
not, and that is the predictor across every study found.
