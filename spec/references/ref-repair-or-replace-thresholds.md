Three published rules that turn "fix it or throw it away" into something
checkable. Each contributes one clause worth stealing.

## The ratio, from Army Regulation 750-1

Its Maintenance Expenditure Limit is the one-time cost to repair an item to
serviceable condition, expressed as a PERCENTAGE OF THE REPLACEMENT PRICE.
Above the limit, replace.

TWO CLAUSES TRANSFER VERBATIM.

- The limit is reviewed at least annually.
- Required repairs may NOT be split into separate estimates to duck it.

Read on 2026-08-25. The Army Publishing Directorate URL served a stub through
this proxy; the text was read from a government mirror at
https://www.kansastag.gov/DocumentCenter/View/692/

## The override, from 41 CFR 102-34.265

A replacement schedule that permits replacement rather than demanding it. Its
own words: the standard is a MINIMUM ONLY, and a thing may be kept longer if
it runs without excessive maintenance cost or substantial loss of value.

A SCHEDULE PLUS A NAMED TEST FOR IGNORING IT is the shape a corpus exception
wants. The age table itself does not transfer, because prose has neither wear
nor resale value.

## Sunk cost, from 10 CFR Part 436 Subpart A

The federal life-cycle-cost method. Its analysis is systematic, over a stated
study period, and EXCLUDING SUNK COSTS.

THAT CLAUSE IS THE WHOLE REASON THIS NOTE EXISTS. Effort already spent on a
document is not an argument for keeping it.

The subpart also names four ranking measures — net savings, savings to
investment ratio, adjusted internal rate of return, simple payback — plus an
uncertainty analysis. The ranking IDEA transfers. The formulae want cost
figures a prose corpus does not have.
